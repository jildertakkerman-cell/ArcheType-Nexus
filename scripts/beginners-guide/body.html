Lore Archive

Yu-Gi-Oh! began in 1996 as Kazuki Takahashi's manga about Yugi Muto, a boy who solves the ancient Millennium Puzzle and shares his body with the spirit of a pharaoh. The card game his characters played, Duel Monsters, became so popular that Konami turned it into a real one: the Official Card Game launched in Japan in 1999, and the Trading Card Game reached North America in 2002. Blue-Eyes White Dragon and Dark Magician were in that first English set, and both still get new cards today.

Chapter 1 · Play

The basics

In short Get your opponent from 8000 Life Points to 0. You get one Normal Summon a turn but as many Special Summons as your cards allow, and a chain always resolves last in, first out.

This is the short version, just enough to follow a game. The official Rulebook and Master Duel's tutorial fill in the details.

How you win

  • Bring your opponent's Life Points from 8000 down to 0.
  • Your opponent needs to draw a card, but their Deck is empty: they deck out.
  • A card with its own win condition says you win, such as holding all five pieces of Exodia in your hand.

What's in a deck

  • Main Deck: 40 to 60 cards. This is what you draw from.
  • Extra Deck: up to 15 Fusion, Synchro, Xyz and Link Monsters.
  • Side Deck: up to 15 cards to swap in between games.
  • No more than 3 copies of any card across all three, or fewer if the Banlist says so.

How a turn goes

Each player starts with 5 cards. The player who goes first doesn't draw and can't attack on their first turn.

  1. Draw Phase: draw 1 card.
  2. Standby Phase: a few card effects happen here; usually nothing does.
  3. Main Phase 1: Summon monsters, activate Spells, Set cards. Most of the game happens here.
  4. Battle Phase: each of your monsters can attack once.
  5. Main Phase 2: another Main Phase, after combat.
  6. End Phase: "end of turn" effects happen. If you hold more than 6 cards, discard down to 6.

Summoning

Once per turn you get a free Normal Summon. Level 1 to 4 monsters come straight out. Level 5 and 6 need 1 monster Tributed, and Level 7 or higher need 2.

Special Summons come from card effects or from the Extra Deck, and there's no limit on them. That's where modern combos come from: one card Summons another, which Summons another.

Monsters sit upright in Attack Position or sideways in Defense Position. You can switch a monster's position once per turn, but not on the turn it arrived or after it has attacked.

Chains: the last card in happens first

Whenever something is activated, the other player can respond with something faster. Responses stack up into a chain, and the chain resolves from the top down. Here's the most common chain in the game:

  1. Chain Link 1 You activate Snake-Eye Ash's effect to add a card from your Deck to your hand.
  2. Chain Link 2 Your opponent responds by discarding Ash Blossom & Joyous Spring, which negates effects that add a card from the Deck to the hand.

Chain Link 2 resolves first, so Ash Blossom negates the search. When Chain Link 1 resolves, it does nothing. That's a handtrap in action.

How to read a card

In short Every monster shows its name, Attribute, Level, Type and ATK/DEF in the same places. Its text follows a fixed grammar: the condition comes before the colon, the cost before the semicolon, and the effect after it.

This is Ash Blossom & Joyous Spring, one of the most-played cards in the game. Tap a numbered spot on the card to see what that part tells you.

Ash Blossom & Joyous Spring

  1. Name. When card text puts a name in quotation marks, it means exactly that card.
  2. Attribute. FIRE. Some effects only work with monsters of one Attribute.
  3. Level. Three stars, so Level 3. The Level decides whether a monster needs a Tribute, and which Synchro and Xyz Monsters it can help make.
  4. Artwork. Just the picture; it doesn't change how the card plays.
  5. Type line. [Zombie/Tuner/Effect]: its Type is Zombie, it's a Tuner, and it's an Effect Monster.
  6. Card text. What the card does. The next part breaks it down.
  7. ATK and DEF. 0 ATK and 1800 DEF. Ash Blossom isn't meant to fight; you use it from your hand.

The card text, decoded

Modern card text follows the same grammar, called PSCT. Tap a coloured part of Ash Blossom's text.

When a card or effect is activated that includes any of these effects (Quick Effect): You can discard this card; negate that effect.
● Add a card from the Deck to the hand.
● Special Summon from the Deck.
● Send a card from the Deck to the GY.
You can only use this effect of "Ash Blossom & Joyous Spring" once per turn.

Condition, before the colon
When you're allowed to use the effect. Here: when your opponent activates a card or effect that does one of the things in the list.
Quick Effect
You can use it during either player's turn, even in response to something.
Cost, between the colon and the semicolon
What you pay to activate it. Ash Blossom discards itself, and that stays paid even if Ash Blossom is negated.
Effect, after the semicolon
What happens when it resolves: Ash Blossom negates the effect it responded to.
The list

Watch a turn

In short A simple first turn and your opponent's reply using classic cards, then a real modern combo you can step through. Together they put the basics, the field and the card types to work.

Modern decks fit far more into a turn, but every turn follows this same pattern. Tap any card on the board to read it.

  1. You won the dice roll and chose to go first. You start with 5 cards, and the player going first skips their first draw.
  2. In Main Phase 1 you activate Reinforcement of the Army. A Normal Spell sits face-up on the field while it resolves.
  3. It resolves: you search Celtic Guardian, a Level 4 Warrior, from your Deck.
  4. Its job done, Reinforcement of the Army goes to the GY.
  5. You Normal Summon Celtic Guardian in Attack Position. It's Level 4, so it needs no Tribute.
  6. You Set Mirror Force face-down in your backrow. A Trap can't be activated the turn it's Set, but it will be ready on your opponent's turn.
  7. Going first, you can't attack, so you end your turn. Your end board is one monster and one Set card.
  8. Your opponent draws, then Normal Summons Alexandrite Dragon, a Level 4 monster with 2000 ATK.
  9. In their Battle Phase, Alexandrite Dragon attacks Celtic Guardian. 2000 ATK beats 1400 ATK, so Celtic Guardian is about to be destroyed.
  10. You respond by flipping Mirror Force face-up. Its condition is met (an opponent's monster declared an attack), so it starts a chain.
  11. Mirror Force resolves and destroys all of your opponent's Attack Position monsters. Alexandrite Dragon goes to their GY, so the attack never lands.
  12. Mirror Force goes to your GY too. You traded one card for one of theirs, and Celtic Guardian is still standing. That's your first turn and your opponent's reply, start to finish.

    Copy before you build

    Building a deck from scratch is one of the hardest skills in the game, so don't start there. Netdeck a list someone already tested, play it for a while, and work out why each card made the cut. Once you know the deck, swapping cards to suit your style comes naturally.

    Pick a deck you actually like

    The strongest deck changes with every new set and every Banlist. The deck you love the look of will still be fun in a year. Every archetype on this site has a page covering its playstyle, key cards and combos. Try the deck finder above, browse them all, or use Random Archetype on the home page for inspiration.

    Where to find decklists

    • YGOProDeck: a card database plus decklists from recent tournaments.
    • Master Duel Meta: lists and a tier list for Master Duel.
    • Deck profile and combo videos on YouTube. Search for the deck's name plus "combo" to see it in action.
    • The archetype pages on this site, many with step-by-step combo simulators.

    Try it before you buy it

    Build the deck in the free EDOPro simulator (or in Master Duel, if you have the cards) and play a dozen games before buying paper cards. It costs nothing, and it's the quickest way to find out whether a deck suits you.

    Buying paper cards

    A Structure Deck is the easiest way in: a playable core of one theme in a single box, and many players buy two or three copies to get more of the key cards. For anything else, buy singles. Opening booster packs is fun, but it's a lottery, not a way to build a specific deck.

    What goes in a deck

    Keep the Main Deck at or close to 40 cards, so you draw your best cards more often. A typical modern deck splits into two parts:

    Engine Non-engine

    The engine is your archetype: starters, extenders and searchers that make your plays. The non-engine part (handtraps and board breakers) stops your opponent. Roughly two-thirds engine is a common shape, but it varies a lot from deck to deck.

    Where to play

    In short Learn in Master Duel, test decks in EDOPro, and bring small-size sleeves plus pen and paper to your local store.

    Master Duel Official

    Konami's free-to-play online game for PC, consoles and phones. It uses the same rules as the paper game and handles every ruling for you, which makes it the best place to learn. You unlock cards over time, and it has its own card release schedule and Banlist.

    Official site

    EDOPro Unofficial

    A free, fan-made simulator for computers. Every card is available straight away, so it's ideal for testing a deck before buying it. It saves replays as .yrpX files, which our Replay Analyzer can break down turn by turn.

    Project Ignis: EDOPro

    Your local store In person

    Official Tournament Stores run regular events called locals. Ask what their nights are like, since some are relaxed and some are competitive, and tell your opponents you're new. Most players are happy to explain a card.

    What to bring to the table

    • Sleeves: Yu-Gi-Oh! cards are smaller than Magic or Pokémon cards, so look for "small" or "Japanese size" sleeves, not "standard".
    • A deck box to carry your Main, Extra and Side Deck together.
    • Pen and paper to track Life Points. Official tournaments expect a written record, even if you also use an app.
    • A die or coin: a few cards roll or flip, and it's handy for deciding who chooses to go first.
    • Optional: a playmat to protect your cards, and a binder for trading.

    How the Banlist works

    In short Forbidden means 0 copies, Limited 1 and Semi-Limited 2. The TCG, OCG and Master Duel each keep their own list.

    Konami limits cards that are too strong with the Forbidden & Limited List, usually just called the Banlist. It changes several times a year, so a deck can lose a key card overnight. That's why step 6 says to check it before buying.

    Forbidden

    0 copies

    Pot of Greed

    Can't be played at all. Pot of Greed simply draws 2 cards, which turned out to be far too good.

    Limited

    1 copy

    Harpie's Feather Duster

    Only one copy per deck, like Harpie's Feather Duster, which destroys every Spell and Trap your opponent controls.

    Semi-Limited

    2 copies

    Droll & Lock Bird

    Up to two copies per deck, like the handtrap Droll & Lock Bird.

    Unlimited

    3 copies

    Ash Blossom & Joyous Spring

    Not on the list, so the normal 3 copies are allowed. That's most cards.

    Every version of the game has its own list

    The TCG, the OCG and Master Duel each keep a separate Banlist, so the same card can be legal in one and banned in another. Make sure you're reading the right one. For example, as of 26 September 2026:

    Beginner FAQ

    My opponent's first turn took ten minutes. Is that normal?

    In modern Yu-Gi-Oh!, yes. Most decks play a long combo on their first turn to set up an end board. You're not just watching, though: every time they activate something you can respond, and that's exactly when handtraps come in.

    Can I ask to read my opponent's cards?

    Always. You can ask to read any face-up card, check how many cards are in their hand, Deck and GY, and look through either GY. Nobody knows every card, and asking is completely normal, at locals and at big events alike.

    What does "any responses?" mean?

    Your opponent is giving you the chance to respond to what they just did. Say "no" to let it go through, or activate a handtrap or Quick Effect.

    I lost before I got a turn. Is my deck bad?

    Probably not. Facing a full end board when you go second is part of the game. That's why decks play handtraps and board breakers, and why you can side in more between games. Going first is a big advantage, so don't judge a deck on a single game.

    Should I buy booster packs?

    Open them for fun if you enjoy it, but don't rely on them to build a deck. A Structure Deck plus the singles you're missing gets you a real deck for much less.

    Is it OK to bring a casual deck to locals?

    Usually, yes. Locals range from very casual to very competitive, so ask the store what their night is like. Tell your opponents you're new; most will happily slow down and explain their cards.

    Where do I ask rules questions?

    The r/Yugioh101 community on Reddit exists for exactly this, and the official Rulebook covers the basics. At an event, ask a judge; that's what they're there for.
