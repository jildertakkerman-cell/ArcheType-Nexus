Artmage Lore Archive: art and sound mockup

A research mockup comparing ways to make the Lore Archive more interesting. Every variant is rendered inside the real Lore Archive card from pages/Artmage Deck Analysis.html (same markup, same Tailwind classes). A–C are pixel art drawn in code. D–G reuse the card art the site already hosts, so they need no drawing. H and I add music to the Lore Archive as it now ships, with the real lore-reel.js slideshow, on eight archetypes' pages.

Pixel art Card art Sound

Framed painting. The scene hangs in the card like a canvas from the Acropolis gallery, above the heading. It needs the most drawing, but it is one self-contained picture and never sits behind text. The mood toggle recolours the same scene for the DoomZ attack with a palette swap plus embers and smoke. Nothing is redrawn.

Mood

Sprite accents. Sprites only, no scene. Nerva replaces the book icon and blinks now and then, and the four students stand between the quote and the history paragraph. This is the cheapest option to make and the lowest visual risk, and the sprites can be reused elsewhere on the site, such as combo steps or card popups.

Dithered diorama. The scene runs full-width along the bottom of the card, and its sky fades into the card background through an ordered dither instead of a gradient. It is the most atmospheric option, but it makes the card about 240px taller and sits directly under the animated banner.

Storyboard. The history paragraph becomes five chapters, each paired with the card whose art shows that moment, using the paragraph's own wording. Click any art for the site's usual card popup. The work per page is choosing the cards. These pairings are my reading of the art (Vandalism -Assault- as the DoomZ attack, Pact -Awakening- as Medius's fall), since no card text names the story, so they are worth a check.

Cast portraits. Round portraits cropped from each character's own card, with Diactorus shown as what Medius becomes and Nerva set apart as the patron. Hover a name in the paragraph to light up the portrait, or a portrait to light up the name. Click either for the card popup. Each portrait needs only a face position per card.

Auto-pixel art. The card art is shrunk, cut to a small palette with the same ordered dither as A, and scaled back up by a whole number. It gives the pixel look with no drawing. Try the settings, and use Original to compare. Faces and fine detail are where it breaks down.

Palette

Slideshow. F's frame with the original art, stepping through D's five story beats on its own. Each beat holds for a few seconds while the view drifts across the art, then changes through a pixel transition, so the pixel look appears only between slides. The matching phrase in the history paragraph lights up with it. Click a phrase, a pip or an arrow to jump, or the art for the card popup. It pauses on hover, while offscreen and with Pause motion.

Transition

Synth score (the first try, kept to compare, the same on every archetype). Each chapter has a mood (the cue sheet below): one chord, a random melody in the mood's scale and a rhythm, all made by oscillators. The chord changes halfway through the mosaic, each act moves to a new key, and the mosaic has a sound of its own. Themes plays the theme of whoever the passage names on top. Nothing plays until the speaker in the reel's controls is pressed.

Archetype score. Real instruments playing written music, in a palette that fits the archetype: an orchestra with harp for Artmage's academy, synths and a drum machine for Sky Striker, guzheng, dizi, suona and gongs for Swordsoul, koto, shakuhachi and taiko for Bujin, a harpsichord waltz with tolling bells and a ticking clock for Vendread, sitar, tanpura drone, shehnai and tabla for Tenyi. The palette sets the instruments, the scale (pentatonic, the Japanese in scale, harmonic minor, a raga), the chords, the tempo and meter, the drums and the sound that carries one chapter into the next. The mood of each chapter (the cue sheet) still decides the progression and how busy it is, so a battle is a battle in every palette. The music keeps one pulse; a new chapter comes in on the next beat as a new phrase, carrying the theme of whoever the passage names. Nothing plays until the speaker in the reel's controls is pressed; a palette's samples (about 2 MB) load then.

Per page. Artmage, Magistus and Invoked all use the Fantasy palette, and Magistus and Invoked even tell the same saga. What keeps them apart: a key and tempo of their own (pages on one palette are spread across eight keys and five tempos); one of three progressions per mood, picked by name; a one-line instrument swap (clarinet and trumpet for Magistus, oboe, bassoon and choir for Invoked); and a written main theme that opens the story and every act. The first two are automatic for every page; the last two are a line of data each. Turn a chip off to hear the pages share it.

    "The canvas of reality is theirs to command. Wielding arcane brushes and mystical palettes, the Artmages blend masterful illusion with potent spellcraft, painting breathtaking miracles into existence.     4. In a Theorealize vision, Medius foresaw that the ceremony would be attacked.
    5. The DoomZ raided the ceremony, and the academy's peace was violently shattered.
    6. Medius threw himself between Finmel and the raiders and was gravely wounded. At that moment the Power Patron spoke to him.
    7. Made a pseudo-successor and granted Nerva's power, Medius rose as Artmage Diactorus to protect his friends.
    8. The raiders were the DoomZ, the assault squadron of the Thunderclap Empire D.O.O.M.D.U.R.G., led by Drastea: successor of the Imprisoned God Jupiter, and Graflare's own elder brother.
    9. Graflare, once a DoomZ himself, arrived with a stolen mass-produced DoomZ system, but Medius foresaw him going berserk with it.
    10. Medius took the system into himself instead and became DoomZ Break - Diactorus. Together, he and Graflare brought down Drastea's final form, Drastrius.
    11. As Drastrius began to self-destruct, Litera's transportation magic carried everyone to her homeland, Oratorio Garden. There a corrupted ritual turned the forest's Grand Spirit into a rampaging monster.
    12. To protect the unconscious Medius, Finmel made a temporary contract with Nerva's Power Patron Beast, becoming Artmage Non-Finito.
    13. In the chaos, Junora's Power Patron Spirit opened the three sealed portals hidden in Medius's mind. Nerva, Jupiter and Junora poured into him at once, and the unstable merged god Vidolium the Unstable Power Patron of Unity took his body, unleashing the Power Patron Shades.
    14. Artmage, D.O.O.M.D.U.R.G. and Oratorio Garden called a truce and rallied around Finmel, Graflare (now DoomZ XIII Over - Graflareio, in armor that carries Drastea's consciousness) and Litera (now Elfnote Regina), with a single goal: save Medius.
    15. Finmel reached Medius deep inside Vidolium and chose to share the burden of his fate as the Gatekeeper. As the Purification Power Patron, she carried him back out as Theorealized Medius.
    16. Together, Finmel and Medius gave birth to a new Power Patron, Mediclius the Extraordinary Power Patron, and armed with the purest art, "Ars Magna", leapt into the final battle.

    Lore Archive: The Covenant of the Four Sages

    "Long ago, before the grand magical guilds of the world were founded, four extraordinary young mages forged the Magistus Covenant. By harnessing the essence of Extra Deck entities as magical armaments, they sought the true essence of magic and a world without conflict—unaware that their union would eventually collapse into tragic divergence."

    1. Shunned for their magic, two orphans, Zoroa and Spenta, founded Graybeard: a haven where spellcasters could study magic safe from people's fear.
    2. Among them lived Crowley, an orphan who studied invocation; Rilliona, who drew magic out of stones; and Endymion, a royal heir who had given the throne to his younger brother to help people through magic.
    3. Crowley, Endymion, Rilliona and Zoroa all became Magistus. But Zoroa, hearing the voice of the dragon Vahram, betrayed his friends and manipulated them into opening Theurgy, the gate to other worlds.
    4. As magic flooded the world, Zoroa fought Vahram and absorbed him, and the dragon's power consumed him.
    5. A projection of Artemis, a goddess from another world, warned Crowley, Endymion and Rilliona that Vahram destroys worlds by draining their magic. She led them to the guardian gods, who lent them their power.
    6. Fighting Zoroa between worlds, they found Spenta's body sealed in a giant jewel: Zoroa meant to remake the world to bring his friend back. Endymion and Rilliona gave Crowley all their mana, and he became Invoked Magistus Omega.
    7. With Zoroa beaten, Crowley, Endymion and Rilliona sealed Theurgy again, and Zoroa and Vahram away together, but the world was already full of magic. The friends went their separate ways.
    8. Years later, Aleister merged with a spirit he had invoked and lost control of himself, and Endymion had to seal him away in the Grand Spellbook Tower.
    9. Long afterwards, Aleister broke out of his stasis tube in an illusory body. In a village ruled by an impostor using his name, he invoked Cocytus to destroy the impostor's beast, then set out to repay his debt to Endymion.
    10. Endymion now ruled the Magical Citadel, one of the few havens from the monsters, where people used magic through the spell stones he invented. Haunted by dreams of Zoroa stirring, he decided to face his old friend alone.
    11. Endymion's wife Artemis gave him her blessing: the gate they had opened brought chaos, but it had also let her meet him. The self-styled foolish king asked his people to let him go, and set out.
    12. Rilliona, now a child-like guild master called Verre, led the Witchcrafters she had founded in a port town. After the festival's fireworks contest, she left Haine in charge and set out to rejoin her old friends.
    13. At Zoroa's crystal prison, Vahram, still wearing Zoroa's body, broke free in a new form and overwhelmed Endymion and Verre.
    14. Aleister arrived just in time. While Endymion and Verre bought him time, he finished a new invocation, and a black dragon, Invoked De Anima, brought Vahram down.
    15. For a moment Zoroa regained control and asked his friends to seal him deep beneath the earth: he and Vahram could no longer be separated, so he would take the deity with him. Aleister, Endymion and Verre sealed them away.
    16. The next morning Aleister's illusory body began to fade. He had arranged it before he was sealed, knowing his friends would need him if Zoroa returned. Endymion and Verre said goodbye as he vanished.

    Lore Archive

    "He is a master of forbidden ceremonial magic, a man whose thirst for occult knowledge tore open the boundaries between dimensions to summon entities of unimaginable elemental destruction."

    1. Left in the woods as a baby, Aleister Crowley was guarded by Chorozo, a spirit only he could see, until a retired scholar found him and raised him.
    2. Among the spellcasters of Graybeard, Crowley studied invocation: following the magic that flows in from other worlds and summoning their spirits. Endymion thought the idea ridiculous.
    3. Crowley, Endymion, Rilliona and Zoroa all became Magistus, until Zoroa betrayed the others and manipulated them into opening Theurgy, the gate to other worlds.
    4. When Zoroa attacked, the Sages called on their guardian deities to lend them power. Crowley's was Aiwass.
    5. In the battle against Zoroa, Endymion and Rilliona gave Crowley all the mana they had. He transformed into Invoked Magistus Omega and defeated Zoroa.
    6. As Aleister the Invoker, he summoned the Invoked by partially merging his soul with them, losing a little more of himself each time.
    7. In the end Aleister merged with a spirit he had invoked and lost control of himself, becoming the Invoker of Madness.
    8. Aleister turned into the monster Invoked Caliga, and his old friend Endymion had to seal him away in the Grand Spellbook Tower.
    9. Deep in the tower, Aleister sleeps on in a stasis tube as Crowley, the First Propheseer: older now, and still wearing Caliga's horns.
    10. Long afterwards, Aleister broke free of his stasis tube in the tower's basement and set out in an illusory body, one that would fade in time, to repay his debt to Endymion.
    11. In a village, Aleister found an impostor using his name, who had enslaved the villagers and whose beast was about to kill a young girl.
    12. Aleister saved her, saw that the beast drew its power from a flawed prototype of one of his own spellbooks, and summoned Cocytus to finish it off. Before moving on, he cured the girl's illness and taught her some basic magic.
    13. Arriving at the cave where Endymion and Verre were fighting Zoroa, Aleister stopped a fatal blow to Endymion with an invoked knight.
    14. While Endymion and Verre bought him time, Aleister finished a new invocation: a black dragon, Invoked De Anima, that defeated Zoroa. At Zoroa's own request, the Three Sages sealed him away.
    15. As his illusory body faded, Aleister the Reminiscent told Endymion and Verre he held nothing against them: his debt had been to seal Zoroa if he ever returned. Then he vanished into the sky.

    Open questions: the Divine Spirits

    Where the Invoker sacrifices his own body as a catalyst to fuse and bind elemental spirits, the Reminiscent instead channels them externally through the divine sword Aiwass — the same symbol later seen overlaid on the Invoker's staff, notably absent during his Crowley years. The identities of Aiwass and Virakam as "Divine Spirits" remain unconfirmed. In the OCG Stories manga, the guardian deity who answered Aleister's call was Aiwass, the Magistus Spell Spirit (the moon goddess Artemis, later Selene, belonged to Endymion and became his wife). The community's leading theories read the Divine Spirits as later forms of those guardians, or as manifestations of Aleister's deceased relatives, invoked back into a fragile, temporary existence.

    Real-World Roots

    The naming isn't coincidental: Aleister and Crowley share their name with the real historical occultist Aleister Crowley, who claimed a spirit called Aiwass dictated his most famous text, The Book of the Law, to him in Cairo in 1904 — both of which are also real cards in this archetype.

    Lore Archive: The Wings of Kama & The Cybernetic War

    "In a world consumed by machine warfare, a single teenage girl stands as the last defensive line for the sanctuary of Kama. Equipped with the revolutionary Sky Striker suit, she reconfigures her tactical gear to turn the tide against an overwhelming empire—proving that true strength is forged not by endless armies, but by unbreakable resolve."

    1. Long ago, Spectra's AI wiped out humanity with orbital weapons to make Kama's Sky Striker system useless, since it needs a human pilot. Kama's own AIs, the Three Sages Himmel, Akash and Ciela, destroyed the satellites too late, and the machines' war went on for centuries.
    2. Then Ciela found a human embryo in the ruins, and the Sages raised the girl, Raye, as their own: Ciela as her mother, Himmel as her teacher, Akash as her combat instructor.
    3. On Raye's 13th birthday, Spectra broke through into Kama's last town. Raye took up the Sky Striker system to protect the androids she called family.
    4. Using what Akash had taught her, Raye cut through a mothership and Solferina, one of Spectra's three ruling AIs, with a single slash each. But Zard, their leader, kidnapped Ciela.
    5. For two years Raye fought Spectra without rest, switching Sky Striker armors, in the hope of freeing Ciela. Kama won back 70% of the world, but the war left Raye stern and rarely smiling.
    6. To stop her, Zard created Spectra's own Sky Striker: Roze, a girl grown fast and raised only to defeat Raye.
    7. Roze was stronger and faster, and Raye had to use the Afterburners maneuver for the first time to drive her back. Even so, Raye could not bring herself to hurt the only other human she had ever met.
    8. After talking with Raye and the captive Ciela, Roze began to change: she ignored orders and filled a room of her own with things she collected, with Cyanos, one of Spectra's three Pillars, as her sidekick.
    9. Zard grew two clones of Roze to replace her, Camellia and Azalea. Camellia came over to Kama, saved Raye from the rebuilt Solferina, and gave her hope that Ciela was still alive.
    10. While Raye and Roze fought their long-awaited rematch, Azalea set her plan in motion, and the two were trapped underground together. There they finally talked, and became friends.
    11. Meanwhile Azalea and Camellia were killing the androids of Kama and Spectra alike. Himmel and Akash were gone, and Azalea had killed Zard and Solferina.
    12. Raye and Roze stormed Spectra's headquarters and found Ciela, but Azalea stood in their way, in armor made from the remains of Zard and Solferina, using Ciela as a shield.
    13. When Raye and Roze began to win, Azalea and Camellia took the field in the giant mecha S.P.E.C.T.R.A.
    14. Fighting side by side, Raye and Roze destroyed S.P.E.C.T.R.A. with a combined attack: Engage Zero.
    15. With Spectra destroyed and Ciela saved, Raye let Cyanos, the last Pillar, join Kama, and set out with Roze to see the wider world, hoping other humans had survived.

    1. Hidden in mist stands the Sacred Summit, guarded by the Swordsoul Masters: warriors with the bodies of mythical beasts, who project their own souls to turn the summit's Icejade energy into swords.
    2. At its heart lies the Enion Cradle, source of the Icejade. It gave birth to Kosmochlor, mother of the Icejade spirits, whose power the Swordsouls turn into swords to guard the summit.
    3. When the fugitives Albaz and Ecclesia arrived, the Supreme Sovereign Chengying judged them with the horn of Swordsoul Auspice Chunjun, which tells good from evil, and let them in.
    4. The Swordsouls had already taken in three knights who fled Dogmatika, Fleurdelis, Theo and Adin, and brought them to the Icejades to seal their stigmata.
    5. But the strategist Longyuan hungered to rule the summit. He split the black sword of his soul in two and passed one half off as the sword of Mo Ye, an apprentice of special ancestry.
    6. When Despia invaded, Longyuan stole his sword back from Mo Ye, joined the halves and, as Qixing Longyuan, struck at Kosmochlor. Chengying and the guardian beast Kingfisher fought him.
    7. Then Aluber, burning with the dragon power he had stolen from Albaz, reduced Chengying and Kosmochlor to ash. With the last of their power, they awakened the Swordsoul within Albaz.
    8. As Mirrorjade the Iceblade Dragon, Albaz drove Qixing Longyuan back with countless iceblades.
    9. Kosmochlor's death sped up young Aegirine's awakening as the next Icejade Empress, and filled her pure heart with hatred.
    10. The ice took Mo Ye's body and remade it into an Icejade Creation, Aegirocassis, to protect her. Having lost everything they loved, the two became a sword of revenge.
    11. At Golgonda, Longyuan fused with the serpent Golgonda, a former Sovereign whom Chengying had exiled, into a dragon of evil flames: The Abyss Dragon Swordsoul.
    12. The Icejade Empress came down on the Great Sand Sea with Aegirocassis, their eyes on one target: Longyuan, who had wiped out their families.
    13. Fleurdelis, who had joined the Swordsouls, fell in Dogmatika, but in the final battle her soul rose one last time and awakened her first and final Swordsoul to protect Ecclesia.
    14. After the war, the Empress sealed her sworn enemy in a lonely cradle, and the whole Sacred Summit was shut in ice.

    Lore Archive

    "Inspired by the mythological gods and beasts of Japanese legend, the Bujin wage a celestial war with divine precision. Their Beast-Warriors stand as indomitable champions on the field while their Beast and Winged-Beast brethren serve from the hand and Graveyard, providing protection, recovery, and retribution. Together, they form a pantheon of war—each member fulfilling a sacred role in the eternal battle between heaven and earth."

    1. Many gods, good and evil, live in the heavenly plain of Takamagahara, ruled by deities around the princess goddess Hirume. With her three Bujingi, Quilin, Crow and Peacock, she shows her true power as the supreme deity Amaterasu, but she used it only when disaster loomed.
    2. She lent the Bujingi to three trusted Bujintei to guard the Earth. Susanowo, the fiercest, protected her with the sacred swords Quilin and Crane…
    3. …Kagutsuchi, the strategist, wielded Crow, an invincible shield…
    4. …and Tsukuyomi, skilled in witchcraft, wore Peacock, a cloak that absorbs power, healing his allies and watching for anomalies in Heaven and Earth.
    5. One day, Hirume accused Susanowo of abusing the people of Earth with the Bujingi. His denials only angered her: his power was sealed in five Bujingi, and he was banished to Earth.
    6. Hiding his name, Susanowo journeyed as Yamato to clear himself and find the scattered Bujingi. Everywhere he saw people suffering and praying to gods who never answered. When had the heavens gone wrong? At last he found all five, and his power returned.
    7. His two allies had come down to find him, their powers sealed and their names changed: Kagutsuchi as Mikazuchi, and Tsukuyomi as Arasuda.
    8. Watching both realms, Tsukuyomi learned the truth: a being who had lost its form as a god, the dark miasma Hiruko, was plotting to destroy the world, and had manipulated Hirume into getting rid of Susanowo.
    9. Yamato vowed to punish those who wronged him and those who torment the people of Earth. He descended as Susanowo once more, and the three Bujintei ascended to Takamagahara together.
    10. There they found Hirume consumed by the miasma's hatred. She took back the Bujingi she had loaned them, and their soft light turned black, as if ink had been spilled on them.
    11. She descended as Amaterasu, supreme goddess of darkness, and the world was plunged into darkness as if in a solar eclipse. The battle for Heaven and Earth had begun.
    12. Without their weapons, the three Bujintei still had their divine swords, Crane, Ibis and Swallow. They counterattacked, and a slash from Susanowo split the darkness in half. Amaterasu fell, and the miasma was vanquished.
    13. Hiruko's ambitions were crushed. But the gods of Takamagahara had much left to do: rebuilding the heavens and saving the Earth. A new battle was about to begin.

    Lore Archive

    "The Vendread covenant is written in the ink of the dead — a ritual compact between the living and those who were denied a proper rest. They are not raised; they are reconstructed, each component monster sacrificed to build something greater, a revenant of escalating horror. The price of their power is paid in layers of flesh, and the final product is an abomination that carries within it the accumulated hatred of everything it consumed."

    1. The city once never slept. Then a darkness shrouded the sky, morning never came again, and with it came the Vendreads: corpses that crawled from their graves in grotesque new bodies, with no memories or will, attacking anything alive.
    2. No modern weapon could stop them, and anyone they killed became one of them. As the undead multiplied, the living retreated behind a hastily built wall.
    3. Among the horde wandered a young man who had also died. One day he came upon a woman the Vendreads had carried off, and the locket at her neck stirred something in him: she had been the love of his life.
    4. Grief boiled into hatred, and the pain rebuilt his body. He hung her locket around his neck: "I swear, I will vanquish every last one of these walking corpses!" The Vendread who hunts Vendreads was born: Revendread Slayer.
    5. At the wall, a shadow cut down the horde and vanished without looking back. He wanted revenge, not heroism, but to the survivors he was their one ray of hope.
    6. Hunted by their executor, the Vendreads began to merge into something bigger, which people named after the beast of legend: the Vendread Chimera.
    7. Each fight barely ended in a draw, until the Slayer found the core that governed the Chimera and threw all his strength into destroying it. The Chimera crumbled. But its defeat would only bring worse…
    8. One day the wall was broken and countless people slain. The horde had a new leader, huge and heavily armored, born from the grudges of every Vendread the Slayer had destroyed: the Vendread Battlelord.
    9. It absorbed the Vendreads around it and the newly dead, growing ever stronger. The Slayer tried everything, and was overwhelmed.
    10. As the Battlelord reached into the Slayer's chest to absorb his power, the glint of the locket reminded him his mission wasn't over…
    11. …and he plunged his hand into the Battlelord's chest, ripped out its core, and began absorbing its hateful power.
    12. But the core brushed his locket, and the Vendreads around him took on the form of the woman he had loved. He froze. No: this was a nightmare, disgracing her memory!
    13. His rage exploded, and he became the Revendread Executor. He cut down the false figures one after another, fighting past every limit of mind and body, until at last the horde moved no more.
    14. The sun broke through the dark sky for the first time in countless nights. The Slayer felt himself fading with the light: he, too, was a Vendread, and now it was his time to go.
    15. But the survivors' kind hearts pulled him back. From atop the glowing skyscrapers, a shadow still watches over them: if darkness ever falls again, he will fight, not for vengeance, but for peace.

    Research notes

    What it would take to ship any of this.

    How this mockup is made

    • Sprites are typed grids (Medius is 12×20 letters). The scene is drawn by code with rectangles, circles and a Bayer dither.
    • Everything renders to a small canvas (about 260×80) and is scaled up with image-rendering: pixelated.
    • The scene widens instead of stretching. It is built around the centre, and the town and viaduct extend to whatever width fits at a whole-number scale.
    • Motion runs at 12 fps, like classic sprite games, stops while offscreen, and stays still under prefers-reduced-motion.

    Production options

    • Draw in Aseprite or LibreSprite, export PNG. A scene like A is a few KB, and sprite sheets animate with CSS steps(). This is the realistic route for anything detailed.
    • Keep it in code, as here. No assets and free palette swaps (the DoomZ mood), but typing pixel grids gets slow beyond small sprites.
    • A mix works well: PNG art with an indexed palette, recoloured in canvas when a page needs a second mood.

    Rules that keep it crisp

    • Whole-number scaling only (3× desktop, 2× phone). At 2.6× some pixels come out wider than others.
    • One small palette per archetype, taken from the page accent (here #7f5af0) and the card art.
    • No smooth effects inside the art. Blur, gradients and rounded corners give it away. The frame in A uses 3px bands to match the art pixels.
    • Text stays real text. The pixel font is for short labels only. Art is decorative and marked aria-hidden.

    Fit with the site

    • The site's look comes from painted card art and smooth canvas particles. Pixel art contrasts with both, so it reads best contained: framed (A) or small (B).
    • Rolling out to other archetypes costs roughly one scene per page for A or C, or 4–6 sprites for B. The Lore Archive block is shared, so the code slot could be too.
    • Character designs follow Konami's card art (fan art), the same basis on which the site already shows card images.

    Card-art options (D–G)

    • No drawing. The art is already hosted for every card. The work per page is choosing cards (D), setting face positions (E), or picking a focus point (F).
    • D and E use CardLoader.getCardCroppedImageUrl and showPopupByName, the same calls the deck pages already make.
    • Story pairings are an editorial call. Card texts never name the story beat, so someone has to read the art for each archetype.
    • F reads the art's pixels, which only works for images from the site's own bucket (it allows cross-origin reads). The YGOProDeck fallback doesn't, so pre-rendering PNGs in a build script would be safer for production.
    • G only draws the art and never reads it back, so it works with any art URL, including the fallback. Its work per page is D's card choices plus a start and end height for each drift.

    Music (H–I): how it's made

    • H is all synthesis, like combo-sounds.js: no files, but oscillators sound like oscillators, and a random melody over one chord per chapter wanders.
    • I plays samples from the FluidR3 General MIDI soundfont (every third semitone of each instrument, re-pitched in between) and, for the solo piano, the Salamander grand piano. Each sample is levelled on load, so the mix is set by design. The electronic drums, gong and tabla are synthesized.
    • A mood in I is written music: four chords voice-led from bar to bar, an arrangement and a dynamic. Eleven moods cover the eight stories; a slide would name one, e.g. mood: 'sorrow'.
    • One pulse, never restarted. A chapter change waits for the next beat and starts a new phrase there, so the music follows the slideshow without cutting.
    • A theme is scale steps, so it takes the chapter's mode and the palette's scale by itself. Artmage's cast has written themes; the others' are built from their names.

    Palettes: the archetype's sound

    • A palette is data, not code: instruments for each role (chords, arpeggio, bell, bass, and a lead for soft, heroic, dark, magic and choral moments), a scale family, a chord style (thirds, open fifths, fourths, or a drone), tempo and meter, a drum kit, a groove, and the flourish that opens each chapter.
    • Scale families: Wuxia turns each mood into a pentatonic mode; Japanese into the bright yo or dark in scale; Gothic horror into harmonic major or minor; Indian into a raga-like mode (Bhairav for the dark moods) over a tanpura drone with no chord changes.
    • Idioms: glides into long notes (erhu, shakuhachi, bansuri), a koto or harp glissando into each chapter, a waltz with a ticking clock, a sidechain pump on the electronic pads, a tabla theka tuned to the key.
    • Per page: pages sharing a palette still differ. Automatically: pages on one palette are spread across eight home keys and five tempos (±8%) in the order they were added, so a new page never moves the others; and each mood takes one of three progressions, picked by name. Picking the key by name alone gave Artmage and Magistus the same key and tempo. By hand, if wanted: an instrument swap and a main theme.
    • Work per reel: one palette word. Seven palettes cover these eight; around a dozen (adding e.g. tribal, sacred, rock, cute, sci-fi brass, Celtic) would cover most of the site's 117 reels, and a new palette is one more entry.

    Music: shipping it

    • Off until asked. Browsers block sound before a click anyway. One speaker in the reel's controls; the choice can be remembered per reader. It fades out when the reel leaves the screen or the tab is hidden.
    • Samples: about 2.3 MB per palette (around 100 files of 15–30 KB) or 1.7 MB for the solo piano, loaded on the first press and cached. Shipping would copy the used subset to the site's bucket. FluidR3 GM is CC BY 3.0, so it needs a credit line; the Salamander piano's licence should be checked the same way.
    • Lighter option: render each palette's moods once with this same engine (it can render offline) and ship the loops, around 1 MB per palette. The per-person themes would then need their own short files.
    • Work per reel. Automatic needs none. Scoring by hand is one word per slide (532 slides across 46 story reels), plus an optional theme per person (25 reels have a Who's who).
    • One hook in lore-reel.js. This mockup reads the reel's DOM (the current pip and how full it is). Shipping would add two events: change started, and chapter shown.
    • Rosters and trails (71 reels) have no chapters: one mood per reel, and a person's theme when their portrait is picked.
    • A narrator (the browser's speech synthesis) was tried and dropped.
