Lore Archive

Ars Magna is Latin for "the Great Art". Citrinitas, the yellowing, is one of the four stages of the alchemists' Great Work, and Philosophorum means "of the philosophers", as in the philosopher's stone.

The archetype is the payoff of the Medius saga. Purified by the Purification Power Patron, the form Finmel Artmage took, Medius the Pure rises as Mediclius the Extraordinary Power Patron and takes control of the powers of the three deities who ruled the warring nations: Nerva of Creation, Jupiter of Destruction and Junora of Tuning. Mediclius's right eye is Finmel's.

Each Ars Magna monster shows Mediclius channelling one of those deities. Citrinitas shows Medius the Pure beside the Purification Power Patron, ringed by the three deities' circles, and Philosophorum shows Mediclius facing what looks like a new form of Vidolium the Unstable Power Patron of Unity. Mediclius's name comes from Mercury, called the water star in East Asian tradition, which is why it is WATER. The Ars Magna trio are EARTH, WIND and FIRE, so the finished board holds all four classical elements.

Gameplay Evolution Summary

Beyond the Brave (OCG 18 July 2026 · TCG 8 October 2026): five cards that turn Power Patron Link Monsters into a self-refilling removal engine. The three Level 8 monsters spend themselves from the hand for searches, then come back from banishment whenever an Extra Deck monster of their deity's type is Special Summoned. A Link Summon brings all three back at once.

Archetype Overview

An engine for Power Patron decks, not a standalone deck.

Ars Magna is a five-card engine. Its three Level 8 monsters do their first job from the hand: you banish one to search a monster, search a Spell/Trap, or bring back a banished teammate. Once banished, each one waits for a Special Summon of the Extra Deck type its deity rules, then returns to the field by its own effect. A Link Summon wakes all three.

What they do after that depends on a Power Patron Link Monster, above all Mediclius the Extraordinary Power Patron. With one on the field, Ars Magna the Finite and the Infinite banishes a monster, Ars Magna the Corruption and the Purification banishes Spell/Traps, and Ars Magna "Citrinitas" lets both do it on the opponent's turn as Quick Effects. Ars Magna "Philosophorum" banishes the trio again, so the next Link Summon brings them back and the loop continues.

The three rules of the engine

  1. Spend it from the hand

    Each monster's first effect banishes itself from your hand as the cost, so the archetype starts combos without a Normal Summon.

  2. Return on the deity's summon

    While banished, each monster Special Summons itself when a Fusion, Synchro or Xyz Monster of its type is Special Summoned. Any Link Monster counts for all three.

  3. Pay off through a Power Patron Link

    The removal effects, Citrinitas's Quick Effects and every battle bonus need a Power Patron Link Monster on your field. The searches, the returns and the draw don't.

The Engine: Banish, Summon, Return

Every Ars Magna monster travels the same loop. Getting into banishment is the cost of its first effect; getting out is free.

  1. Hand. Banish an Ars Magna monster from your hand as the cost of its ① to search a card or revive a teammate.
  2. Banished. It waits there until a Fusion, Synchro, Xyz or Link Monster of its type is Special Summoned.
  3. Summon. The matching Special Summon triggers its ② and it returns to the field. A Link Monster returns all three.
  4. Field. With a Power Patron Link Monster such as Mediclius, its ③ removes a card, and Citrinitas lets it do so on the opponent's turn. Philosophorum ① or the Division and the Union's ③ banishes it again, and the loop restarts.

Whose summon doesn't matter

The ② effects trigger on any Special Summon of the right type. That includes your opponent's Link Summons and a Power Patron Link Monster brought back by Philosophorum.

Why Mediclius

From the Extra Monster Zone, Mediclius points to three Main Monster Zones. If the returning trio fills them, it becomes unaffected by your opponent's activated effects and unlocks the Quick Effect that banishes itself and every card your opponent controls.

What Citrinitas changes

With Citrinitas face-up, the Finite and the Infinite's monster banish and the Corruption and the Purification's Spell/Trap banish become Quick Effects. Each is once per turn, and both are ready again whenever the trio returns on a new turn.

Theorealized Medius adds to the loop: every face-up banish, including each Ars Magna cost, can search another Ars Magna card.

Playstyle & Ratios

Play all five cards as a package inside a Power Patron deck. The same package works going first as a loop and going second as a battle package.

Going first

Build, then reload on their turn

Combo into a board with I:P Masquerena and Philosophorum Set, keeping Citrinitas face-up. Your removal is the trio's ③ effects, which come back every time the trio returns, so you can spend them freely on the opponent's turn instead of saving a single negate.

Going second

The 9000 ATK Mediclius

Once it points to a monster, Mediclius negates your opponent's face-up monsters and halves their ATK. Banish the Finite and the Infinite to triple Mediclius's original ATK to 9000, and the Division and the Union to let it attack monsters up to three times. The Corruption and the Purification keeps it from being destroyed by battle. All three bonuses come from hand effects that cost no Normal Summon.

The whole Ars Magna package costs 10 points. The real cost decision is Theorealized Medius.

Strengths

  • Bodies that come back on their own. Any Link Summon, including your opponent's, can return up to three Level 8 monsters from banishment.
  • Removal on the opponent's turn. With Citrinitas, you get a monster banish and a Spell/Trap banish that reset each time the trio returns.
  • Starts without a Normal Summon. Every Ars Magna starter pays its cost from the hand.
  • Cheap to add. It's a five-slot package, and only Citrinitas carries Genesys points.

Weaknesses & counterplay

  • No Power Patron Link, no payoff. The removal, Citrinitas's Quick Effects and the battle bonuses all need one.
  • Search-heavy. Droll & Lock Bird stops the chain after the first search, and Ash Blossom hits every Deck search. The Division and the Union's revive doesn't touch the Deck, so Ash Blossom can't stop it.
  • Sequencing. Some Elfnote cards lock you into Synchro Summons for the rest of the turn, so make your Link Summons before you use them.
  • Bricks. Mixing the Ars Magna, Medius, Elfnote and Power Patron packages means several cards you don't want to draw naturally.
  • Mediclius placement. It needs the Extra Monster Zone to point at anything, and board breakers like The Great Gallant Bandit punish a board that relies on it alone.
