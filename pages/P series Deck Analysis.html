Executive Summary

The informal ":P" series, consisting of I:P Masquerena, S:P Little Knight, and EM:P Meowmine, represents a cornerstone of modern deck building. This engine, linked by a shared naming convention and deep synergistic design, provides generic, powerful, and recursive interruptions. Its core function is to convert a minimal board presence into multiple layers of reactive disruption during the opponent's turn, making it one of the most efficient and widespread Extra Deck packages in the competitive Yu-Gi-Oh! TCG.

Deconstructing the Trio

I:P Masquerena

I:P Masquerena

The foundational piece. Her Quick Effect allows for a Link Summon during the opponent's turn, turning any Link monster with an on-summon effect into a powerful interruption.

S:P Little Knight

S:P Little Knight

The apex of utility and Masquerena's prime target. Provides two powerful banishing effects: one for removal on summon, and a second to interrupt or dodge opposing effects.

EM:P Meowmine

EM:P Meowmine

The recursive extender. It acts as a free Special Summon to enable combos and provides a follow-up bounce effect that triggers when I:P or S:P are used.

The Core Sequence: A Formidable End Board

The engine's primary strength is its ability to establish a simple yet powerful end board. By ending their turn with I:P Masquerena and one other monster, a player threatens to unleash a devastating series of interruptions. This single play creates a chain of effects that can dismantle an opponent's turn, showcasing the formidable combined potential of the entire :P series.

1Opponent's Turn Activation

In response to an opponent's play, activate I:P Masquerena's Quick Effect to Link Summon.

2Summon S:P Little Knight

Using I:P and another monster, summon S:P Little Knight. I:P's effect grants it protection from destruction by card effects.

3Chain of Interruptions

S:P's on-summon effect banishes a card. If EM:P Meowmine is in the GY/hand, its effect also triggers to bounce a card. S:P then remains on field, threatening a second banish effect.

Competitive Analysis

Strengths

  • Generic & Flexible: Fits into any deck that can make Link-2s.
  • Resource Efficient: Generates multiple interruptions from a small investment.
  • Built-in Protection: I:P grants destruction immunity, while S:P can dodge targeted effects.

Weaknesses

  • Effect Negation: Vulnerable to hand traps like Effect Veiler or Infinite Impermanence on I:P.
  • Timing Exploits: I:P can only be activated in the Main Phase, leaving it vulnerable to plays in other phases.
  • Battle Removal: S:P Little Knight's low ATK makes it susceptible to being destroyed by battle.

Top Tier Integration

  • Snake-Eye/Fire King: Effortlessly makes the engine as part of its main combo.
  • Live Twin/Spright: The ideal home for the full 3-card engine due to its focus on Link-2 monsters.
  • Orcust: Provides flexible, non-DARK-locking interaction.

New Card Spotlight: W:P Fancy Ball

For years the series was a trio. W:P Fancy Ball makes it a quartet, and it is the first member that is not a cheap utility body. It is a Link-3 payoff built to be summoned on the opponent's turn, off I:P Masquerena, so that its monster negate is live the moment it lands. Get that timing wrong and you have a 2400 ATK vanilla.

Debut · Master Duel 4th Anniversary OCG · Limit Over Collection: The Heroes TCG · Magnificent Monsters · Sep 2026

W:P Fancy Ball

W:P Fancy Ball
Link-3 DARK Cyberse 2400 ATK Top / BL / BR
Materials

Two or more Effect Monsters. Fully generic, so any monster-heavy shell can make it, but three bodies is a real price next to S:P Little Knight's two.

Quick Effect · Negate and banish

When the opponent activates a monster effect on the field or in the graveyard, banish this card until the End Phase to negate that effect and banish the monster. Only works if W:P Fancy Ball was Special Summoned that turn.

Quick Effect · Off-turn Link Summon

During the opponent's Main Phase, Link Summon immediately after the effect resolves. If W:P Fancy Ball is one of the materials, you may also use one Link-2 or lower monster the opponent controls. Neither player can respond with Link Monster effects.

Limits

Each effect is once per turn. No protection of its own, and nothing it does touches Spells or Traps.

The Text, Verbatim

2+ Effect Monsters
When your opponent activates a monster effect on the field or GY (Quick Effect): You can banish this card that was Special Summoned this turn until the End Phase; negate that effect, and if you do, banish it. During your opponent's Main Phase, you can (Quick Effect): Immediately after this effect resolves, Link Summon 1 Link Monster, and if this card you control would be used as material for that Link Summon, you can also use 1 Link-2 or lower monster your opponent controls. Neither player can activate the effects of Link Monsters in response to this effect's activation. You can only use each effect of "W:P Fancy Ball" once per turn.

Read the negate condition first. "That was Special Summoned this turn" is the whole card. Summoning W:P Fancy Ball on your own turn hands the opponent a free turn to break it, which is why the intended line runs through I:P Masquerena during their Main Phase instead.

What Actually Changes for the Series

I:P gets a second destination

The Masquerena pivot no longer has to end on S:P Little Knight. Deciding between a banish and a negate is now a real read on the opponent's hand.

You can take their monster

The off-turn Link Summon eats one Link-2 or lower monster they control, and Link Monsters cannot respond. A freshly summoned Link-1 or Link-2 disappears before it does anything.

Both effects in one turn

The Link Summon does not have to use W:P Fancy Ball. Feed it two spare bodies instead and it stays on the field with its negate still held.

Extra Deck space is the cost

It only functions alongside I:P Masquerena and a climb target, so it is realistically a two or three slot commitment, not a one card add.

What does not happen

Making It on Your Own Turn

Summoned during your turn it has no negate once the turn passes, and no protection while it waits. The opponent gets a free turn to run it over.

Two bodies at the end of your turn become a monster negate, a stolen Link Monster and a banish on theirs.

Rulings Worth Knowing

Four points that decide most games where this card shows up.

1

The End Phase return is not a Special Summon. It comes back face-up, but it does not re-arm the negate. Next turn it is a plain 2400 beater.

2

Stealing a monster is optional. With no legal Link-2 or lower target on their side, the Quick-Link still works using only your own monsters.

3

The two effects only conflict if it feeds itself. Banish it to negate and it is gone as material. Use it as material and it cannot negate. Keep it on board and you get both.

4

Reviving it re-arms it. Anything that Special Summons it from the graveyard makes the negate live again for that turn.

The honest split. Gorgon of Zilofthonia is the better answer to a single unaffected boss monster, because a continuous effect cannot be chain blocked. W:P Fancy Ball is the better answer to a board that is still being built, because it removes pieces permanently and converts them into your own threat.

Should You Play It?

Strong, not automatic
  • It is not a replacement for S:P Little Knight. It sits next to it, and both want I:P Masquerena already in the Extra Deck.
  • Play it in shells that flood the board and can spare three bodies. Link-heavy piles and banish-themed Cyberse strategies are the natural homes.
  • Early write-offs called it a flop because of the material cost. Competitive results since have been good enough to keep it in Extra Decks that have the room.
  • If your Extra Deck is already tight, it is the first of the four to cut. The trio does the heavy lifting on far less investment.

Where It Shows Up

  • Maliss: shared Cyberse typing and a banish-first game plan, with plenty of link material.
  • Lunalight: graveyard revival lines pair with the A Bao A Qu re-arm.
  • Unchained: the off-turn quick link already existed, so Rage now goes into W:P Fancy Ball before S:P.
  • Snake-Eye and Live Twin piles: spare Level 1 bodies mean you can Link Summon without eating W:P itself.

Where It Falls Over

Hand traps go straight through. The negate only reaches monster effects on the field or in the graveyard, so anything activating from the hand is untouched.
Chain blocking beats it. The negate is an activated effect. Put the important monster effect on Chain Link 1 and W:P Fancy Ball can only answer Chain Link 2.
Nothing for Spells and Traps. It offers no protection to the rest of your board and folds to standard board breakers.
One turn of value. Without a revival effect it is live for exactly the turn it hits the field, then reverts to a body.
Three materials is a lot. Decks that struggle to reach I:P Masquerena in the first place gain nothing here.

What the W Stands For

The series has always hidden networking terms in plain sight. I:P is Internet Protocol, fitting for a masked data courier. S:P is Security Protocol, a nod to Chiyomaru's old S-Force allegiance. W:P reads as Waypoint Protocol, the two of them on the run together.

Two more readings sit underneath it. In Japanese shorthand a capital W stands for "double", and the art is literally the pair sharing one motorcycle. On a meta level it also reads as World Premiere, because the card launched digitally first before reaching physical play. The colon and P are the tongue-out emoji on Masquerena's console, which has been there since her original artwork.
