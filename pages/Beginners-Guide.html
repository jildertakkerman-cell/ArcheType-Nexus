Lore Archive

Yu-Gi-Oh! began in 1996 as Kazuki Takahashi's manga about Yugi Muto, a boy who solves the ancient Millennium Puzzle and shares his body with the spirit of a pharaoh. The card game his characters played, Duel Monsters, became so popular that Konami turned it into a real one: the Official Card Game launched in Japan in 1999, and the Trading Card Game reached North America in 2002. Blue-Eyes White Dragon and Dark Magician were in that first English set, and both still get new cards today.

The basics

In short Get your opponent from 8000 Life Points to 0. You get one Normal Summon a turn but as many Special Summons as your cards allow, and a chain always resolves last in, first out.

This is the short version, just enough to follow a game. The official Rulebook and Master Duel's tutorial fill in the details.

How you win

  • Bring your opponent's Life Points from 8000 down to 0.
  • Your opponent needs to draw a card, but their Deck is empty: they deck out.
  • A card with its own win condition says you win, such as holding all five pieces of Exodia in your hand.

What's in a deck

  • Main Deck: 40 to 60 cards. This is what you draw from.
  • Extra Deck: up to 15 Fusion, Synchro, Xyz and Link Monsters.
  • Side Deck: up to 15 cards to swap in between games.
  • No more than 3 copies of any card across all three, or fewer if the Banlist says so.

How a turn goes

Each player starts with 5 cards. The player who goes first doesn't draw and can't attack on their first turn.

  1. Draw Phase: draw 1 card.
  2. Standby Phase: a few card effects happen here; usually nothing does.
  3. Main Phase 1: Summon monsters, activate Spells, Set cards. Most of the game happens here.
  4. Battle Phase: each of your monsters can attack once.
  5. Main Phase 2: another Main Phase, after combat.
  6. End Phase: "end of turn" effects happen. If you hold more than 6 cards, discard down to 6.

Summoning

Once per turn you get a free Normal Summon. Level 1 to 4 monsters come straight out. Level 5 and 6 need 1 monster Tributed, and Level 7 or higher need 2.

Special Summons come from card effects or from the Extra Deck, and there's no limit on them. That's where modern combos come from: one card Summons another, which Summons another.

Monsters sit upright in Attack Position or sideways in Defense Position. You can switch a monster's position once per turn, but not on the turn it arrived or after it has attacked.

Chains: the last card in happens first

Whenever something is activated, the other player can respond with something faster. Responses stack up into a chain, and the chain resolves from the top down. Here's the most common chain in the game:

  1. Chain Link 1 You activate Snake-Eye Ash's effect to add a card from your Deck to your hand.
  2. Chain Link 2 Your opponent responds by discarding Ash Blossom & Joyous Spring, which negates effects that add a card from the Deck to the hand.

Chain Link 2 resolves first, so Ash Blossom negates the search. When Chain Link 1 resolves, it does nothing. That's a handtrap in action.

How to read a card

In short Every monster shows its name, Attribute, Level, Type and ATK/DEF in the same places. Its text follows a fixed grammar: the condition comes before the colon, the cost before the semicolon, and the effect after it.

This is Ash Blossom & Joyous Spring, one of the most-played cards in the game. Tap a numbered spot on the card to see what that part tells you.

Ash Blossom & Joyous Spring

  1. Name. When card text puts a name in quotation marks, it means exactly that card.
  2. Attribute. FIRE. Some effects only work with monsters of one Attribute.
  3. Level. Three stars, so Level 3. The Level decides whether a monster needs a Tribute, and which Synchro and Xyz Monsters it can help make.
  4. Artwork. Just the picture; it doesn't change how the card plays.
  5. Type line. [Zombie/Tuner/Effect]: its Type is Zombie, it's a Tuner, and it's an Effect Monster.
  6. Card text. What the card does. The next part breaks it down.
  7. ATK and DEF. 0 ATK and 1800 DEF. Ash Blossom isn't meant to fight; you use it from your hand.

Watch a turn

In short A simple first turn and your opponent's reply using classic cards, then a real modern combo you can step through. Together they put the basics, the field and the card types to work.

Modern decks fit far more into a turn, but every turn follows this same pattern. Tap any card on the board to read it.

  1. You won the dice roll and chose to go first. You start with 5 cards, and the player going first skips their first draw.
  2. In Main Phase 1 you activate Reinforcement of the Army. A Normal Spell sits face-up on the field while it resolves.
  3. It resolves: you search Celtic Guardian, a Level 4 Warrior, from your Deck.
  4. Its job done, Reinforcement of the Army goes to the GY.
  5. You Normal Summon Celtic Guardian in Attack Position. It's Level 4, so it needs no Tribute.
  6. You Set Mirror Force face-down in your backrow. A Trap can't be activated the turn it's Set, but it will be ready on your opponent's turn.
  7. Going first, you can't attack, so you end your turn. Your end board is one monster and one Set card.
  8. Your opponent draws, then Normal Summons Alexandrite Dragon, a Level 4 monster with 2000 ATK.
  9. In their Battle Phase, Alexandrite Dragon attacks Celtic Guardian. 2000 ATK beats 1400 ATK, so Celtic Guardian is about to be destroyed.
  10. You respond by flipping Mirror Force face-up. Its condition is met (an opponent's monster declared an attack), so it starts a chain.
  11. Mirror Force resolves and destroys all of your opponent's Attack Position monsters. Alexandrite Dragon goes to their GY, so the attack never lands.
  12. Mirror Force goes to your GY too. You traded one card for one of theirs, and Celtic Guardian is still standing. That's your first turn and your opponent's reply, start to finish.

    Copy before you build

    Building a deck from scratch is one of the hardest skills in the game, so don't start there. Netdeck a list someone already tested, play it for a while, and work out why each card made the cut. Once you know the deck, swapping cards to suit your style comes naturally.

    Try it before you buy it

    Build the deck in the free EDOPro simulator (or in Master Duel, if you have the cards) and play a dozen games before buying paper cards. It costs nothing, and it's the quickest way to find out whether a deck suits you.

    Buying paper cards

    A Structure Deck is the easiest way in: a playable core of one theme in a single box, and many players buy two or three copies to get more of the key cards. For anything else, buy singles. Opening booster packs is fun, but it's a lottery, not a way to build a specific deck.

    What goes in a deck

    Keep the Main Deck at or close to 40 cards, so you draw your best cards more often. A typical modern deck splits into two parts:

    Engine Non-engine

    The engine is your archetype: starters, extenders and searchers that make your plays. The non-engine part (handtraps and board breakers) stops your opponent. Roughly two-thirds engine is a common shape, but it varies a lot from deck to deck.

    In short Forbidden means 0 copies, Limited 1 and Semi-Limited 2. The TCG, OCG and Master Duel each keep their own list.

    Konami limits cards that are too strong with the Forbidden & Limited List, usually just called the Banlist. It changes several times a year, so a deck can lose a key card overnight. That's why step 6 says to check it before buying.

    Beginner FAQ

    My opponent's first turn took ten minutes. Is that normal?

    In modern Yu-Gi-Oh!, yes. Most decks play a long combo on their first turn to set up an end board. You're not just watching, though: every time they activate something you can respond, and that's exactly when handtraps come in.

    Can I ask to read my opponent's cards?

    Always. You can ask to read any face-up card, check how many cards are in their hand, Deck and GY, and look through either GY. Nobody knows every card, and asking is completely normal, at locals and at big events alike.

    What does "any responses?" mean?

    Your opponent is giving you the chance to respond to what they just did. Say "no" to let it go through, or activate a handtrap or Quick Effect.

    I lost before I got a turn. Is my deck bad?

    Probably not. Facing a full end board when you go second is part of the game. That's why decks play handtraps and board breakers, and why you can side in more between games. Going first is a big advantage, so don't judge a deck on a single game.

    Should I buy booster packs?

    Open them for fun if you enjoy it, but don't rely on them to build a deck. A Structure Deck plus the singles you're missing gets you a real deck for much less.

    Is it OK to bring a casual deck to locals?

    Usually, yes. Locals range from very casual to very competitive, so ask the store what their night is like. Tell your opponents you're new; most will happily slow down and explain their cards.

    Where do I ask rules questions?

    The r/Yugioh101 community on Reddit exists for exactly this, and the official Rulebook covers the basics. At an event, ask a judge; that's what they're there for.

    The pieces of the game: zones, piles, and the kinds of Summon. Practise these

    LP (Life Points)

    Learned Your health: both players start at 8000, and 0 means you lose.

    Your health total. Both players start at 8000, and you lose when yours reaches 0.

    You'll hear "I'm at 2000 LP, one more attack and I'm done."

    I know this one

    GY (Graveyard)

    Learn this one firstLearned The discard pile, and a resource many cards use.

    The face-up pile beside your field. Destroyed, used, discarded and Tributed cards go here. Either player can look through either GY at any time. Many modern cards have effects that work from the GY, so treat it as a second hand rather than a trash can.

    You'll hear "Banish it so they can't use it in the GY."

    See also Banish, Revive / Reborn, Mill

    I know this one

    Banish

    Learn this one firstLearned Removing a card from play, usually for good.

    Set a card aside away from the field, face-up unless the card says face-down. Banished cards are much harder to get back than cards in the GY, so banishing is one of the strongest ways to get rid of something.

    Example Called by the Grave banishes a monster from your opponent's GY and shuts its effects off for a while.

    See also GY (Graveyard)

    I know this one Watch it on the field

    Main Deck

    Learned The 40 to 60 card pile you draw from.

    The face-down pile you draw from: 40 to 60 cards. You can play up to 3 copies of any card (fewer if the Banlist says so). Staying close to 40 means you draw your best cards more often.

    See also Extra Deck, Side Deck / Siding

    I know this one

    Extra Deck

    Learn this one firstLearned Up to 15 Fusion, Synchro, Xyz and Link Monsters you Summon directly.

    A separate pile of up to 15 Fusion, Synchro, Xyz and Link Monsters. You never draw these. You Summon them straight from the Extra Deck when you meet their requirements, and most combos end with Extra Deck monsters on the field.

    You'll hear "That effect locks me out of my Extra Deck for the rest of the turn."

    See also Materials, EMZ (Extra Monster Zone), Lock / Summoning restriction

    I know this one

    Side Deck / Siding

    Learned Up to 15 cards you swap in between games of a match.

    Up to 15 extra cards you can swap into your deck between games of a match, to prepare for the deck you're facing. "Siding in" a card means putting it in for the next game.

    You'll hear "I sided in three floodgates for game two."

    See also Tech / Tech card, Match / Game 1, 2, 3

    I know this one

    Field / Board

    Learned Everything on the table; "board" means one player's cards on it.

    Everything on the play area: monsters, Spells and Traps. "Board" usually means the cards one player has on the field right now.

    You'll hear "What does their board look like?"

    See also End board, Backrow

    I know this one

    Backrow

    Learned Your Spell & Trap Zones, or the face-down cards in them.

    Your Spell & Trap Zones (the row behind your monsters), or the face-down cards in it. A lot of backrow usually means Traps are waiting to go off.

    You'll hear "They Set three backrow, careful."

    Example Mirror Force is the classic Trap that sits in the backrow waiting for an attack.

    See also Set / Face-down, Board breaker

    I know this one

    EMZ (Extra Monster Zone)

    Learned The two shared middle zones where Link Monsters are Summoned.

    The two monster zones in the middle of the table, shared by both players. You can only use one of them. Link Monsters (and face-up Pendulum Monsters from the Extra Deck) must be Summoned there or to a zone a Link Monster points to. Fusion, Synchro and Xyz Monsters can go straight into your regular Main Monster Zones. The field diagram shows where it is.

    See also Extra Deck

    I know this one

    Set / Face-down

    Learned Placing a card face-down.

    Place a card face-down. A Set monster is in face-down Defense Position and uses your Normal Summon for the turn. Set Spells and Traps wait in the backrow, and a Trap can't be activated the turn it was Set unless its text says otherwise.

    See also Backrow, Normal Summon / Tribute Summon

    I know this one Watch it on the field

    Normal Summon / Tribute Summon

    Learned Your one free Summon from the hand each turn.

    Your one free Summon each turn: put a monster from your hand onto the field. Level 1 to 4 monsters need nothing. Level 5 and 6 need you to Tribute 1 monster you control, and Level 7 or higher need 2. Setting a monster face-down uses the same once-per-turn allowance.

    You'll hear "It uses your Normal Summon, so it's a big commitment."

    See also Special Summon

    I know this one Watch it on the field

    Special Summon

    Learn this one firstLearned Any other Summon, from effects or the Extra Deck, with no limit per turn.

    Any Summon that isn't your Normal Summon: one made by a card effect, or from the Extra Deck using materials. There's no limit per turn unless a card says so, which is why a modern turn can include ten or more Summons.

    Example Nibiru, the Primal Being punishes a player who Summons five or more monsters in one turn.

    See also Combo / Line, Materials

    I know this one

    Materials

    Learned The monsters used up to Summon an Extra Deck monster.

    The monsters you use up to Summon an Extra Deck monster. Fusion, Synchro and Link materials go to the GY. Xyz materials are tucked under the Xyz Monster instead, and get detached to pay for its effects.

    See also Tuner, Link climb

    I know this one

    Tuner

    Learned The kind of monster every Synchro Summon needs one of.

    A kind of monster needed for Synchro Summons: 1 Tuner plus any number of non-Tuners whose Levels add up to the Synchro Monster's Level. It says "Tuner" on the card's type line.

    Example Ash Blossom & Joyous Spring and Effect Veiler are both Tuners, a handy bonus for Synchro decks.

    I know this one

    Level, Rank & Link Rating

    Learned The number that sizes a monster: Level, Rank (Xyz) or Link Rating.

    The number that says how big a monster is. Most monsters have a Level (the stars). Xyz Monsters have a Rank instead, and Link Monsters have a Link Rating ("Link-3") equal to the number of materials they need. A Rank or Link Rating is not a Level, so effects that care about Levels ignore them.

    I know this one

    Attribute

    Learned The element in a monster's top-right circle, like DARK or FIRE.

    The element in the circle at the top right of a monster: DARK, LIGHT, EARTH, WATER, FIRE, WIND or DIVINE. Many effects care about it, such as "add 1 Level 1 FIRE monster".

    Example Snake-Eye Ash searches a Level 1 FIRE monster, so only FIRE monsters count.

    See also Type (monster type), Level, Rank & Link Rating

    I know this one

    Type (monster type)

    Learned A monster's species, like Dragon or Warrior, shown in square brackets.

    A monster's species, printed in square brackets above its text, like [Dragon] or [Spellcaster]. There are more than 20 Types, and many archetypes and effects care about one of them. Don't mix it up with the card type (Normal, Effect, Fusion and so on), which the frame colour shows.

    Example Reinforcement of the Army only searches Warriors.

    See also Attribute

    I know this one

    Token

    Learned A stand-in monster made by an effect that vanishes when it leaves the field.

    A stand-in monster created by a card effect. It isn't a real card: it can't be in your Deck or hand, and if it leaves the field it simply disappears. Tokens are often used up as Tribute or Link material.

    Example Nibiru, the Primal Being hands your opponent a big Token after it Tributes the field.

    See also Materials

    I know this one

    Quick-Play Spell

    Learned A fast Spell you can use in response, or on the opponent's turn once Set.

    A Spell with a lightning-bolt icon. You can activate it from your hand during your own turn, even in response to something. If you Set it, you can also use it on your opponent's turn, but not on the turn you Set it.

    Example Called by the Grave and Book of Moon are Quick-Play Spells.

    See also Spell Speed, Set / Face-down

    I know this one

    Continuous Spell / Trap

    Learned A Spell or Trap that stays on the field and keeps working.

    A Spell or Trap with an infinity icon. It stays on the field after it's activated and keeps working until it leaves the field.

    Example Skill Drain and Call of the Haunted are Continuous Traps.

    See also Continuous effect, Floodgate

    I know this one

    Counter Trap

    Learned The fastest Trap: only another Counter Trap can respond to it.

    A Trap with an arrow icon, and the fastest card in the game: only another Counter Trap can be activated in response to it. Most Counter Traps negate something.

    Example Solemn Judgment and Solemn Strike are Counter Traps.

    See also Spell Speed, Negate

    I know this one

    Field Spell

    Learned A Spell played in the Field Zone; one per player.

    A Spell that goes in the Field Zone. Each player can have one; if you activate a new one, your old one is sent to the GY. Many archetypes have a Field Spell that powers up the whole deck.

    See also Field / Board

    I know this one

    Equip Spell

    Learned A Spell attached to one monster, usually to power it up.

    A Spell attached to one monster, usually to power it up. If that monster leaves the field or is turned face-down, the Equip Spell is destroyed.

    See also Continuous Spell / Trap

    I know this one

    Flip effect

    Learned An effect that activates when the monster is turned face-up.

    An effect marked "FLIP:" that activates when the monster is turned face-up, for example when a Set monster is attacked or Flip Summoned.

    Example Man-Eater Bug: "FLIP: Target 1 monster on the field; destroy it."

    See also Set / Face-down

    I know this one

    Effects & rulings 16

    How card effects are used, answered and limited. Practise these

    Chain / Chain Link

    Learn this one firstLearned A stack of activations that resolves last in, first out.

    When a card or effect is activated, both players get the chance to activate something in response. Each response is a new Chain Link, and the chain then resolves backwards: the last card activated happens first. The chain example above walks through one.

    You'll hear "Chain Link 2: Ash Blossom."

    See also Respond / Chain to, Resolve, Spell Speed

    I know this one

    Respond / Chain to

    Learned Activating something right after your opponent does, so yours resolves first.

    Activate a card or effect right after your opponent activates one, so yours goes on top of the chain and resolves first. When an opponent asks "any responses?", they're giving you that chance before they carry on.

    You'll hear "In response to the search, I discard Ash."

    See also Chain / Chain Link

    I know this one

    Resolve

    Learned The moment an effect actually happens.

    The moment an activated effect actually does what it says. Activating a card and resolving it are two separate moments, and in between it can be responded to or negated.

    See also Chain / Chain Link, Negate

    I know this one

    Negate

    Learn this one firstLearned Cancelling a card's activation, effect or Summon.

    Cancel something. Negating an activation stops the card completely. Negating an effect stops what it does (for a monster, often until the end of the turn). Negating a Summon means the monster never arrives.

    Example Solemn Judgment negates a Summon or a Spell/Trap activation, and destroys that card.

    See also Omni-negate, Interruption / Disruption

    I know this one

    Quick Effect

    Learned A monster effect you can use during either player's turn.

    A monster effect you can use during either player's turn, even in response to something, not only in your own Main Phase. Handtraps are Quick Effects, and newer cards print "(Quick Effect)" in their text.

    See also Spell Speed, Handtrap

    I know this one

    Trigger effect

    Learned An effect that activates when something happens, like being Summoned.

    An effect that activates when something happens: "If this card is Summoned…", "If this card is sent to the GY…". Many starters are trigger effects that search as soon as they're Summoned.

    Example Snake-Eye Ash: "If this card is Normal or Special Summoned: You can add 1 Level 1 FIRE monster from your Deck to your hand."

    See also Missing the timing

    I know this one

    Ignition effect

    Learned A monster effect you activate in your own Main Phase.

    A monster effect you activate yourself during your own Main Phase, much like a Spell Card. It needs no special timing, but it can't be used in response to anything.

    I know this one

    Continuous effect

    Learned An effect that's always on while its card is face-up.

    An effect that is simply always on while its card is face-up. It never starts a chain. Many floodgates work this way.

    Example Once Skill Drain is active, it keeps negating the effects of every face-up monster on the field.

    See also Floodgate

    I know this one

    Spell Speed

    Learned The 1, 2 or 3 rating that decides what can respond to what.

    Decides what can respond to what. Spell Speed 1: Normal Spells, Ignition effects and most Trigger effects. They start chains and can't respond to anything. Spell Speed 2: Quick-Play Spells, Traps and Quick Effects. They can respond to Spell Speed 1 or 2. Spell Speed 3: Counter Traps. Only another Spell Speed 3 card can respond to them.

    See also Chain / Chain Link, Quick Effect

    I know this one

    Hard once per turn (HOPT)

    Learn this one firstLearned An effect you can use once per turn across all your copies.

    Text like "You can only use this effect of [card name] once per turn" limits that effect to once per turn across every copy you have. Nearly every modern card has it, and it's the main thing that stops combos from looping forever. A close cousin, "You can only activate 1 [card name] per turn", limits activating the card itself.

    You'll hear "It's HOPT, so the second copy does nothing this turn."

    Example Sangan's search is hard once per turn.

    See also Once per turn (soft OPT)

    I know this one

    Once per turn (soft OPT)

    Learned Once per turn for each copy of the card.

    "Once per turn" with no card name attached. Each copy can use the effect once per turn, so a second copy can use it again.

    See also Hard once per turn (HOPT)

    I know this one

    Target / Non-targeting

    Learned Picking a specific card as you activate an effect.

    An effect targets when its text says "target", and you pick the card when you activate it. Some monsters can't be targeted, so removal that doesn't target ("destroy all", "banish face-down") gets around them.

    You'll hear "It can't be targeted, so you need a non-targeting out."

    Example Evenly Matched doesn't target, so it can remove cards that are protected from targeting.

    See also Out

    I know this one

    Cost

    Learned What you pay to activate a card; it stays paid even if negated.

    What you do to activate a card, before anyone can respond: pay LP, discard, Tribute, detach. In card text, the cost comes before the semicolon (;). If the activation gets negated, the cost stays paid.

    Example Ash Blossom & Joyous Spring discards itself as its cost.

    See also PSCT (Problem-Solving Card Text)

    I know this one

    Lock / Summoning restriction

    Learned A restriction a card puts on you for the rest of the turn.

    A limit a card puts on you for the rest of the turn, such as "you cannot Special Summon from the Extra Deck, except Fiend monsters". Being "locked into" a type means you can only use that kind of monster for the rest of the turn, so the order of your plays matters. "Lock" can also mean a board of floodgates that stops the opponent from playing at all.

    See also Floodgate

    I know this one

    PSCT (Problem-Solving Card Text)

    Learned Konami's standard wording for card text, where the punctuation matters.

    Konami's standard way of writing card text. A colon (:) ends the condition for activating. A semicolon (;) comes right after the cost or target. "And if you do" means both parts happen together, but only if the first part succeeded. "Then" means one after the other. "Also" means both happen independently.

    See also Cost, Target / Non-targeting

    I know this one

    Missing the timing

    Learned Why some old "When ... you can" effects fail to activate.

    An old rule that still matters. An optional "When … you can" effect fails to activate if its trigger wasn't the very last thing that happened, for example if it happened partway through a chain. "If … you can" effects don't have this problem, which is why modern cards almost always say "If".

    See also Trigger effect

    I know this one

    Card roles 24

    Words for the job a card does in a deck. Practise these

    Starter / One-card starter

    Learn this one firstLearned A card that starts your combo, ideally on its own.

    A card that begins your combo. A "one-card starter" needs nothing else in your hand and can make your whole end board alone; other starters need one specific partner card, which players call a two-card combo. The more starters a deck plays, the more often it gets going.

    You'll hear "Snake-Eye Ash is a one-card starter, so almost any hand with it plays."

    Example Snake-Eye Ash is a one-card starter in Snake-Eye decks: it searches the next piece as soon as it's Summoned.

    See also Extender, Combo / Line

    I know this one

    Extender

    Learn this one firstLearned A card that makes your combo bigger or keeps it going.

    A card that makes your combo longer or bigger, or lets it keep going after the opponent interrupts it, but usually can't start the combo on its own. Starters get you going; extenders get you further.

    You'll hear "They had one starter and two extenders, so one handtrap wasn't enough."

    See also Starter / One-card starter, Play through

    I know this one

    Handtrap

    Learn this one firstLearned A card used from your hand on your opponent's turn to stop them.

    A card you use straight from your hand during your opponent's turn to stop part of their combo. Handtraps are how the player going second fights back before their first turn.

    You'll hear "I'm holding two handtraps and waiting for the right moment."

    Example The classics: Ash Blossom & Joyous Spring, Effect Veiler, Infinite Impermanence and Droll & Lock Bird.

    See also Choke point, Going first / going second

    I know this one

    Board breaker

    Learn this one firstLearned A card that clears or gets around a finished board.

    A card that clears or gets around the opponent's finished board, usually when you go second. Some destroy everything, some negate, some banish. "Breaking" a board means clearing enough of it that you can make your own plays.

    Example Harpie's Feather Duster (all their Spells and Traps), Lightning Storm, Dark Ruler No More and Evenly Matched.

    See also Going first / going second, Nuke / Board wipe

    I know this one

    Boss monster

    Learned The big monster your deck is built to reach.

    The big payoff monster your deck is built to reach: strong, hard to remove, or both. Most archetypes have one or two.

    You'll hear "Once the boss hits the field they can't do anything."

    Example Blue-Eyes Ultimate Dragon for classic Blue-Eyes, or Accesscode Talker as a finisher in many Link decks.

    I know this one

    Engine

    Learned The group of cards that makes your deck's plays.

    A group of cards that work together as a unit. "The engine" is the core of your deck, and you'll also hear about adding a small engine from another archetype into a deck.

    See also Non-engine, Archetype

    I know this one

    Non-engine

    Learned Cards that stop your opponent instead of making your plays.

    The cards in your deck that aren't part of your combo, mostly handtraps and board breakers. They're there to stop your opponent, not to make your own plays.

    See also Engine, Handtrap, Board breaker

    I know this one

    Searcher

    Learned A card that adds another specific card from your Deck to your hand.

    A card whose job is to search: take a specific card from your Deck to your hand. Decks full of searchers are consistent, because any card can fetch the one they need.

    Example Reinforcement of the Army searches a Level 4 or lower Warrior.

    See also Consistency

    I know this one

    Floater

    Learned A monster that replaces itself when it leaves the field.

    A monster that replaces itself when it leaves the field, usually by Summoning or searching another card. Destroying a floater doesn't really get rid of anything.

    Example Mystic Tomato Summons a monster from the Deck when it's destroyed by battle. Sangan searches when it goes from the field to the GY.

    I know this one

    Staple

    Learned A card almost every deck plays.

    A card that almost every deck plays or considers, because it's good whatever your archetype. Most handtraps and board breakers are staples.

    You'll hear "Ash is a staple, it's never a wasted pickup."

    See also Generic

    I know this one

    Generic

    Learned A card any deck can use, not tied to an archetype.

    Not tied to any archetype, so any deck can play it, as long as it can meet the card's requirements.

    See also Staple, Archetype

    I know this one

    Tech / Tech card

    Learned A card added to beat a specific deck or situation.

    A card added to beat specific decks or situations, rather than to help the deck's own plan. "Teching" a card means adding it for that reason.

    You'll hear "I teched a Kaiju for their big boss monster."

    See also Side Deck / Siding

    I know this one

    Garnet

    Learned A card your deck needs but you never want to draw.

    A card your deck needs to contain, because other effects use it from the Deck, but that's bad to draw. Drawing garnets is one of the most common ways to brick.

    I know this one

    Brick / Bricking

    Learn this one firstLearned A hand, or a card, that can't do anything.

    A hand that can't do anything useful, or a single card that's awful to draw. "I bricked" means you drew a hand that couldn't make a play.

    You'll hear "Five Spells and no monster, full brick."

    See also Garnet, Dead card, Consistency

    I know this one

    Dead card

    Learned A card that does nothing in the current situation.

    A card that does nothing in the current situation.

    Example Harpie's Feather Duster is dead if your opponent never Sets a Spell or Trap.

    I know this one

    Beatstick / Beater

    Learned A monster whose job is simply to attack hard.

    A monster whose job is simply to attack hard: high ATK, little or no effect.

    Example Blue-Eyes White Dragon: 3000 ATK and no effect at all.

    I know this one

    Vanilla

    Learned A Normal Monster: no effect, just stats.

    A Normal Monster: no effect, just stats and a line of flavour text, on a yellow card. A few archetypes are built around them.

    Example Dark Magician and Blue-Eyes White Dragon.

    I know this one

    Wall

    Learned A monster that's hard to attack past.

    A monster that's hard to attack past, usually thanks to very high DEF or protection effects.

    Example Big Shield Gardna, with 2600 DEF.

    I know this one

    Floodgate

    Learned A card that shuts off a whole kind of play.

    A card with an ongoing effect that shuts off a whole type of play, such as no monster effects, or only one monster of each Type. Some hit both players. Decks built around them are called Stun decks.

    Example Skill Drain negates every face-up monster's effects. Anti-Spell Fragrance makes Spells wait a turn. There Can Be Only One allows each player only one monster of each Type.

    See also Continuous effect, Combo, control & stun decks

    I know this one

    Interruption / Disruption

    Learned Anything that stops the opponent partway through their turn.

    Anything that stops the opponent partway through their turn: a negate, a handtrap, a Trap that destroys something. Players rate a board by how many interruptions it has.

    You'll hear "Their end board has three interruptions."

    See also Negate, End board

    I know this one

    Omni-negate

    Learned An effect that can negate almost anything.

    An effect that can negate almost anything, any card or effect, usually once per turn. These are the scariest pieces of an end board.

    See also Negate, Bait

    I know this one

    Out

    Learned A card that could get you out of a losing position.

    A card that can get you out of a losing position. "What are my outs?" means "what could I draw that wins this or saves me?"

    You'll hear "My only out to that floodgate is Harpie's Feather Duster."

    See also Topdeck

    I know this one

    Win condition (wincon)

    Learned How a deck actually plans to win.

    How a deck actually plans to win. Usually that's attacking with big monsters, but it can also be burn damage, making the opponent run out of cards, or a special win like holding all five pieces of Exodia.

    I know this one

    Toolbox

    Learned A deck that fetches a different answer for each situation.

    A deck that can search or Summon one of many different answers depending on the situation, like reaching into a toolbox for the right tool.

    I know this one

    Actions 21

    The verbs. Most of these are shorthand for something a card effect does. Practise these

    Search

    Learn this one firstLearned Adding a specific card from your Deck to your hand.

    Use a card effect to add a specific card from your Deck to your hand. Searching is different from drawing, which gives you the top card.

    You'll hear "Normal Summon, search, search, and we're off."

    I know this one Watch it on the field

    Draw

    Take the top card of your Deck into your hand: once per turn in your Draw Phase, or more with effects. Extra draws are powerful because they give you cards you'd never otherwise see.

    Example Pot of Greed (draw 2) was so strong that it has been Forbidden for years.

    Dump / Send

    Learned Sending a card you choose from your Deck to the GY.

    Send a card you choose from your Deck to the GY. Great for decks whose cards work from the GY.

    Example Foolish Burial dumps any monster.

    I know this one Watch it on the field

    Mill

    Learned Sending cards from the top of your Deck to the GY.

    Send cards from the top of your Deck to the GY. Unlike a dump, you don't get to choose which cards.

    Example Card Trooper mills up to 3 cards.

    See also Dump / Send

    I know this one Watch it on the field

    Discard / Pitch

    Learned Sending a card from your hand to the GY.

    Send a card from your hand to the GY. "Pitching" a card usually means discarding it to pay for another effect.

    Example Handtraps like Ash Blossom & Joyous Spring discard themselves.

    See also Cost

    I know this one Watch it on the field

    Tribute

    Learned Sending your own monster to the GY for a Summon or a cost.

    Send a monster you control to the GY, either for a Tribute Summon or to pay for an effect. Older players still call it a "sacrifice".

    See also Normal Summon / Tribute Summon

    I know this one Watch it on the field

    Detach

    Learned Removing a material from under an Xyz Monster.

    Take a material out from under an Xyz Monster and send it to the GY, usually to pay for that monster's effect. An Xyz Monster with no materials left often can't use its effect.

    Example Number 39: Utopia detaches a material to stop an attack.

    See also Materials

    I know this one Watch it on the field

    Pop

    Learned Destroying a card.

    Destroy a card, usually a single card with an effect.

    You'll hear "Pop the Field Spell before you Summon anything."

    Example Mystical Space Typhoon pops one Spell or Trap.

    See also Nuke / Board wipe

    I know this one Watch it on the field

    Nuke / Board wipe

    Learned Destroying or removing everything on one or both sides.

    Destroy or remove everything, or everything on one player's side.

    Example Raigeki destroys all your opponent's monsters. Dark Hole destroys every monster on the field.

    See also Board breaker, Overextend

    I know this one

    Bounce

    Learned Returning a card from the field to the hand.

    Return a card from the field to its owner's hand. A bounced Extra Deck monster goes back to the Extra Deck instead, which makes bounce great against big bosses.

    Example Compulsory Evacuation Device bounces one monster.

    See also Spin

    I know this one Watch it on the field

    Spin

    Learned Returning a card from the field to the Deck.

    Return a card from the field to its owner's Deck, either shuffled in or put on top. Like bounce, a spun Extra Deck monster goes back to the Extra Deck.

    Example Phoenix Wing Wind Blast puts a card on top of the Deck. Knightmare Unicorn shuffles one in.

    See also Bounce

    I know this one Watch it on the field

    Revive / Reborn

    Learned Special Summoning a monster from the GY.

    Special Summon a monster from the GY back onto the field.

    Example Monster Reborn revives a monster from either player's GY. Call of the Haunted revives one from yours.

    See also GY (Graveyard), Recycle / Add back

    I know this one Watch it on the field

    Recycle / Add back

    Learned Getting a used card back from the GY or banishment.

    Get a used card back from the GY (or from banishment) to your hand or Deck. Decks that do this a lot have good "recursion" and are hard to run out of cards.

    Example Monster Reincarnation adds a monster from your GY back to your hand.

    See also Grind game

    I know this one Watch it on the field

    Excavate

    Learned Revealing cards from the top of your Deck, usually to pick one.

    Reveal cards from the top of your Deck, usually to pick one of them and put the rest back or away.

    Example Pot of Prosperity excavates 3 or 6 cards and adds 1 of them to your hand.

    I know this one

    Steal / Take control

    Learned Taking control of your opponent's monster.

    Take control of an opponent's monster and use it as your own, usually until the end of the turn.

    Example Change of Heart.

    I know this one

    Swing / Attack

    Learned Attacking.

    Attack. To "swing for game" or "swing for lethal" is to make the attack that wins.

    See also Lethal / Game, Crash

    I know this one

    Crash

    Learned Attacking into an equal or stronger monster on purpose.

    Attack into a monster with equal or higher ATK on purpose, either to clear it (equal ATK destroys both) or to set off an effect when your own monster is destroyed.

    I know this one

    Burn

    Learned Damage dealt by a card effect instead of an attack.

    Damage from a card effect rather than an attack. "Burn decks" try to win this way.

    Example Ookazi deals 800 damage. Magic Cylinder turns an attack back on the attacker.

    See also Win condition (wincon)

    I know this one

    Scoop

    Learned Conceding the game.

    Give up the game (you "scoop up" your cards). In a best-of-three match, scooping a lost game early to save time for the next one is completely normal.

    See also Match / Game 1, 2, 3

    I know this one

    Link climb

    Learned Using Link Monsters as material for bigger Link Monsters.

    Use a Link Monster as material for a bigger one: a Link-2 into a Link-3, then a Link-4. A Link Monster can count as a number of materials equal to its Link Rating, so a few monsters can climb into a big finisher.

    See also Level, Rank & Link Rating, Materials

    I know this one

    Rank-up

    Learned Turning an Xyz Monster into a higher-Rank one.

    Summon a higher-Rank Xyz Monster by using one you already control as the material, usually with a "Rank-Up-Magic" Spell. The old materials come along underneath.

    See also Level, Rank & Link Rating

    I know this one

    Strategy 18

    How players talk about turns, plans and games. Practise these

    Combo / Line

    Learned A string of effects played in one turn; one route through it is a "line".

    A string of card effects played one after another in a single turn, each leading into the next, to build a strong board. One specific route through a combo is called a "line". Many deck pages on this site have a step-by-step combo simulator.

    You'll hear "What's the line if they Veiler my first monster?"

    See also Starter / One-card starter, End board

    I know this one

    Going first / going second

    Learn this one firstLearned Who takes the first turn, and what each choice gives up.

    In game one, the winner of a dice roll or rock-paper-scissors chooses. If you go first, you can't draw or attack on your first turn, but you set up a board before your opponent does anything. If you go second, you draw and can attack right away, but you face whatever they built. Most modern decks prefer to go first, and decks that like going second pack board breakers.

    See also End board, Board breaker, Handtrap

    I know this one

    End board

    Learn this one firstLearned The cards left on your field when your turn ends.

    What's on your field when you end your turn, especially your first turn. A good end board holds several interruptions to stop the opponent's turn.

    You'll hear "The end board is two negates and a floodgate."

    See also Interruption / Disruption, Board breaker

    I know this one

    Choke point

    Learned The one card or effect a combo can't do without.

    The one card or effect a combo can't do without. Hit the choke point with your handtrap and most of the turn falls apart; hit the wrong card and they play through it.

    See also Handtrap, Play through

    I know this one

    Bait

    Learned Making a play just to draw out the opponent's answer.

    Make a less important play first so the opponent spends their negation on it, then make the play you actually care about.

    You'll hear "Bait the negate with the first monster, then go for the real combo."

    See also Omni-negate

    I know this one

    Play through

    Learned Carrying on with your combo after an interruption.

    Keep your combo going after an interruption, usually thanks to extenders or a second route. "Can it play through Ash?" asks whether the deck still does something when its first search is negated.

    See also Extender

    I know this one

    OTK / FTK

    Learned Winning from 8000 LP in one turn, or on the very first turn.

    An OTK (One Turn Kill) takes the opponent from 8000 LP to 0 in a single turn. An FTK (First Turn Kill) wins on the very first turn of the game, before the opponent plays at all. FTKs are rare, and the Banlist usually shuts them down.

    See also Lethal / Game

    I know this one

    Lethal / Game

    Learned Enough damage to win this turn.

    Enough damage to win this turn. "I have lethal" or "that's game" means the opponent is about to lose.

    I know this one

    Card advantage (+1 / −1)

    Learned Having more cards to work with than your opponent.

    Counting cards. If one of your cards takes out two of your opponent's, you gained a card (+1). A card that draws 2 is also a +1. It matters most in long games; in fast combo games, what the cards do matters more than how many there are.

    See also Grind game

    I know this one

    Overextend

    Learned Committing more cards to the field than you need to.

    Commit more cards to the field than you need, so that one board wipe or one big negate costs you everything.

    See also Nuke / Board wipe

    I know this one

    Consistency

    Learned How often a deck gets to do what it wants.

    How often a deck gets to do what it wants. Plenty of starters and searchers and few garnets make a deck consistent.

    See also Starter / One-card starter, Brick / Bricking

    I know this one

    Grind game

    Learned A long game of trading cards over many turns.

    A long game where both players trade resources over many turns. Decks with lots of recursion and card advantage are good at grinding.

    See also Recycle / Add back, Card advantage (+1 / −1)

    I know this one

    Combo, control & stun decks

    Learned The three broad ways decks play: combo, control and stun.

    Three broad playstyles. Combo decks build a big board in one explosive turn. Control decks answer everything the opponent does and win slowly. Stun decks use floodgates to stop the opponent from playing at all. Plenty of decks sit somewhere in between ("midrange").

    See also Floodgate, Combo / Line

    I know this one

    Topdeck

    Learned The card you draw for the turn, especially when you need a specific one.

    The card you draw at the start of your turn, or needing that draw to be exactly the right card. "I topdecked my out" means you drew the one card that saved you.

    See also Out

    I know this one

    Win-more

    Learned A card that only helps when you're already winning.

    A card that's only good when you're already winning. It looks strong, but it does nothing when you're behind, which is exactly when you need help.

    I know this one

    Power creep

    Learned New cards gradually outclassing old ones.

    New cards slowly getting stronger than old ones over the years. It's why older favourites need new support to stay competitive.

    See also Support

    I know this one

    Mirror match

    Learned Both players using the same deck.

    A game in which both players use the same deck.

    I know this one

    Deck out

    Learned Losing because you must draw from an empty Deck.

    Losing because you have to draw a card but your Deck is empty. A few decks try to make the opponent deck out on purpose by milling them.

    See also Mill, Win condition (wincon)

    I know this one

    Community & tournaments 22

    Words from deck lists, events and the wider player base. Practise these

    Archetype

    Learn this one firstLearned A family of cards with a shared name that work together.

    A family of cards that share part of their name and are designed to work together, like Blue-Eyes or Snake-Eye. Most decks are built around one or two. This whole site is organised by archetype: browse them here.

    See also Support, Engine

    I know this one

    Support

    Learned New cards printed for an existing archetype.

    New cards printed for an existing archetype. When an old deck gets support, it can suddenly become strong again. On the home page, sort by "Support (Newest)" to see which archetypes just got new cards.

    See also Power creep

    I know this one

    Meta

    Learned The strongest, most-played decks right now.

    The decks that are currently the strongest and most played: the "meta decks". The meta shifts with every new set and every Banlist.

    See also Tier 0 / 1 / 2 / Rogue

    I know this one

    Tier 0 / 1 / 2 / Rogue

    Learned An informal ranking of decks by tournament results.

    An informal ranking of decks by tournament results. Tier 1 decks win a lot. Tier 2 decks are strong but less common. Rogue decks are off the radar but can surprise people. "Tier 0" means one deck is so dominant that everyone has to play it or plan to beat it. Nobody agrees on the exact cut-offs.

    See also Meta

    I know this one

    Netdeck

    Learned Copying a decklist someone else published.

    Copy a decklist that someone else published, often one that did well at a tournament. It's the smartest way to build your first deck.

    See also Decklist / Ratios

    I know this one

    Decklist / Ratios

    Learned The exact cards in a deck and how many of each.

    The exact cards in a deck and how many copies of each: the "ratios".

    See also Netdeck

    I know this one

    Top / Top cut

    Learned Reaching the final rounds of a tournament.

    Finishing high enough at a tournament to reach the final elimination rounds. "This deck topped a YCS" means it placed near the top of one.

    I know this one

    TCG / OCG

    Learned The two regional versions of the paper game.

    Two versions of the same paper game. The TCG (Trading Card Game) is played in the Americas, Europe and Oceania. The OCG (Official Card Game) is played in Japan and elsewhere in Asia. The OCG usually gets new cards months earlier, and each has its own Banlist.

    See also Banlist (F&L list), MD (Master Duel)

    I know this one

    MD (Master Duel)

    Learned Konami's official free online version of the game.

    Konami's official, free-to-play online version of the game. It uses the same rules as the paper game, but it releases cards on its own schedule and has its own Banlist.

    See also TCG / OCG

    I know this one

    Format

    Learned Which rules and cards are allowed.

    The rules and card pool you're playing with. The normal, current format is called Advanced Format. Some players enjoy fan-run retro formats that only allow cards up to an older date, such as Goat or Edison.

    I know this one

    Banlist (F&L list)

    Learned The list of Forbidden, Limited and Semi-Limited cards.

    The Forbidden & Limited List. Forbidden cards can't be played, Limited cards can be played at 1 copy, and Semi-Limited cards at 2. It's updated several times a year, and a card that "got hit" was put on it. See the Banlist section below, or our live Banlist page.

    See also TCG / OCG

    I know this one

    Locals

    Learned Regular tournaments at your local game store.

    The regular tournaments at your local game store. They're the friendliest place to start playing in person.

    See also OTS

    I know this one

    OTS

    Learned A store that runs official Konami tournaments.

    An Official Tournament Store: a shop that runs official Konami events. Its yearly OTS Championship can award invitations to bigger events.

    See also Locals, Regionals, YCS & WCQ

    I know this one

    Regionals, YCS & WCQ

    Learned Large tournaments, up to the World Championship qualifiers.

    The bigger events. Regionals are large one-day tournaments. A YCS (Yu-Gi-Oh! Championship Series) can draw thousands of players. WCQs (World Championship Qualifiers, called Nationals in some regions) decide who goes to the World Championship.

    See also Top / Top cut

    I know this one

    Premiere! event

    Learned An event where you play with a new set before its release.

    An event where you can play with a new set before its official release. Its older name was Sneak Peek.

    I know this one

    Match / Game 1, 2, 3

    Learned A best-of-three set of games.

    Tournament rounds are usually a match: the best of three games. Between games both players can side, swapping cards with their Side Deck, so game 1 is always played with the main deck alone.

    See also Side Deck / Siding

    I know this one

    Structure Deck

    Learned A pre-built, ready-to-play deck in one box.

    A pre-built deck sold as one box and built around one theme. It's the easiest way to own a playable core in paper, and many players buy two or three copies to get more of the key cards.

    See also Singles

    I know this one

    Singles

    Learned Individual cards bought on their own.

    Individual cards bought on their own instead of in random packs. If you want a specific deck, buying singles usually costs far less overall than hoping to open the right cards.

    See also Structure Deck

    I know this one

    Sim (simulator)

    Learned A free, unofficial program for playing online.

    An unofficial program for playing Yu-Gi-Oh! online for free. EDOPro is the most popular. Every card is available straight away, which makes it perfect for trying a deck before you buy it.

    See also Replay, MD (Master Duel)

    I know this one

    Replay

    Learned A recording of a game you can watch back.

    A recording of a game you can watch back. EDOPro saves replays as .yrpX files, and you can upload one to our Replay Analyzer for a turn-by-turn breakdown.

    See also Sim (simulator)

    I know this one

    Judge / Ruling

    Learned The rules official at a tournament.

    A judge is the official who answers rules questions and fixes problems at a tournament. Calling one is normal and never rude. A ruling is an official answer on how a specific interaction works when the card text alone isn't clear.

    See also PSCT (Problem-Solving Card Text)

    I know this one

    Sleeves / Double sleeving

    Learned Card protectors; Yu-Gi-Oh! cards need small size.

    Plastic covers for your cards. Yu-Gi-Oh! cards are smaller than Magic or Pokémon cards, so buy "small" or "Japanese size" sleeves. Double sleeving adds a snug inner sleeve for extra protection.
