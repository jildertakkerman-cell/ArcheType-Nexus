NEXUS

Ashtra

The Face-Down Synchro Shrine

Beyond the Brave OCG 18 Jul 2026 TCG 8 Oct 2026

Lore Archive

In Japanese the theme is written 依鬼 — Ashutora, the “Possessed Oni.” Its four main-deck spirits echo oni folklore: Akasuna and Mikumari form a red-and-blue pair, while Yoa and Kuzuha are sorcerer oni of plague and bewitchment. Their two rulers borrow from Shinto myth. Ashtraja Susanoo carries a blade modelled on Kusanagi-no-Tsurugi, the storm god's sword, inscribed with Taoist fú talismans. Ashtraya Mikumari-no-Mikoto takes her name from the deity of water distribution.

“Drag your foes through Ashtrashen – Gate to the Worlds Beyond and besiege them with terrifying Traps!” — Konami, Beyond the Brave product notes

Executive Summary

Ashtra is a ten-card Trap–Synchro theme from Beyond the Brave that plays as hybrid control. Its whole economy runs on one resource: face-down cards. Every main-deck Ashtra monster Special Summons itself from the hand by revealing a face-down card you control and returning it to the hand or Extra Deck. Because a returned Spell or Trap can simply be Set again, one card can pay for several Summons in the same turn. All three Ashtra Traps can also Special Summon themselves as Level 1 Tuners and then Synchro Summon immediately.

That turns the back row into off-turn Synchro plays. A Level 1 Trap Tuner plus a free Level 7 or 8 body reaches Ashtraya Mikumari-no-Mikoto (Level 8) or Ashtraja Susanoo (Level 9) during your opponent's turn. Yoa the Plague Ashtra can shift a Level by 1 at instant speed to reach whichever one you need. Susanoo's End Phase recursion then rebuilds the board every turn, so the deck grinds very well.

The catch is going second. A Trap can't be activated the turn it is Set, and the monsters need a face-down card before they can be Summoned. That leaves pure Ashtra with almost nothing to do on its first turn when facing an established board. In the OCG it launched into a Toon-dominated format and posted poor results. The strongest builds pair it with an engine that fixes turn two, most notably Labrynth.

Mikumari the Barrier Ashtra

Mikumari the Barrier Ashtra

OCG: Mikumari the Sealing Deity Ashtra

Starter Wyrm / WATER LV7 2000 / 2500 ×3 Genesys 5
Card text

You can Special Summon this card (from your hand) by revealing 1 face-down card you control and returning it to the hand/Extra Deck. You can only Special Summon "Mikumari the Barrier Ashtra" once per turn this way. You can only use each of the following effects of "Mikumari the Barrier Ashtra" once per turn. During your Main Phase: You can add 1 non-Wyrm "Ashtra" monster from your Deck to your hand. If this card is sent to the GY as Synchro Material for an "Ashtra" monster: You can target 1 Normal Trap in your GY; Set it.

The deck's primary starter. It searches any other main-deck body (the non-Wyrm clause stops it fetching itself). As material for an Ashtra Synchro it re-Sets any Normal Trap, including the Trap Tuner used in that same Summon.

Yoa the Plague Ashtra

Yoa the Plague Ashtra

OCG: Yoa the Plague Deity Ashtra

Setter / Level Fixer Spellcaster / WIND LV7 2400 / 1500 ×3 Genesys 3
Card text

You can Special Summon this card (from your hand) by revealing 1 face-down card you control and returning it to the hand or Extra Deck. You can only Special Summon "Yoa the Plague Ashtra" once per turn this way. You can only use each of the following effects of "Yoa the Plague Ashtra" once per turn. Sets an Ashtra Trap or Ashtrashen straight from the Deck, which is your opponent's-turn Synchro threat. The Level shift works both ways: it breaks the opponent's Xyz and Synchro maths, or it lifts Yoa to Level 8 so a Trap Tuner reaches Susanoo.

Akasuna the Battle Ashtra

Card text

You can Special Summon this card (from your hand) by revealing 1 face-down card you control and returning it to the hand or Extra Deck. A Book of Eclipse on a 3000-ATK body. Flipping every face-up opposing monster answers an established board without targeting or destroying anything. It is also the one engine card community testers found live going second. The cost competes with your Set Traps, so plan which one it eats.

You can Special Summon this card (from your hand) by revealing 1 face-down card you control and returning it to the hand or Extra Deck. Destruction that scales with face-down cards on both sides. Testers rate it the weakest of the four. Most lists run a single copy as a second Level 8 body for Susanoo, which leaves Akasuna free to keep its flip effect.

Ashtraya Mikumari-no-Mikoto

Ashtraya Mikumari-no-Mikoto

Interaction Synchro Wyrm / WATER LV8 3000 / 2500 ×2
Card text

1 Tuner + 1+ non-Tuners
If this card is Special Summoned: You can send 1 "Ashtra" card from your Deck to the GY, and if you do, send 1 card on the field to the GY. You can only use this effect of "Ashtraya Mikumari-no-Mikoto" once per turn. If your opponent activates a monster effect (except during the Damage Step): You can target 1 "Ashtra" Trap in your GY or banishment, or if 3 or more cards are face-down on the field, you can target any 1 Trap in your GY instead; Set it.

Its Summon effect removes a card without targeting and mills an Ashtra Trap to fuel Susanoo. It triggers on any Special Summon, so a revival re-uses it. The re-Set effect has no once-per-turn limit. A Trap it Sets can't be activated that turn, but the Set itself triggers Ashtrashen's bounce during your opponent's turn.

Ashtraja Susanoo

Ashtraja Susanoo

Mass Banish Synchro Fiend / FIRE LV9 3300 / 0 ×2
Card text

1 Tuner + 1+ non-Tuners
If this card is Synchro Summoned: You can banish any number of Traps from your GY; banish that many cards from the field. During the End Phase: You can target up to 2 "Ashtra" monsters and/or "Ashtra" Traps in your GY and/or banishment; add to your hand or Set each card, then if you targeted 2 cards, return this card to the Extra Deck. You can only use each effect of "Ashtraja Susanoo" once per turn.

A field wipe that doesn't target, paid for with GY Traps, which is exactly what the engine produces. Returning itself to the Extra Deck is the point, not the drawback: it can be Synchro Summoned again next turn to re-trigger the banish. See the Susanoo Loop under Combo Flow.

Ashtrashen - Gate to the Worlds Beyond

Ashtrashen - Gate to the Worlds Beyond

OCG: Ashtrashen Trichiliocosm

Engine Field Spell ×2–3 Genesys 7
Card text

You can target any number of face-down cards you control; add "Ashtra" cards from your Deck to your hand with different names from each other, except Field Spells, equal to the number of those targeted cards that are still face-down, then send those face-down cards to the GY. If a card(s) becomes Set on the field, while you control an "Ashtra" monster and a face-down card (except during the Damage Step): You can target 1 card on the field; return it to the hand. You can only use each effect of "Ashtrashen - Gate to the Worlds Beyond" once per turn.

The deck's best consistency card and its highest Genesys cost. It turns Set cards into a multi-card search, and the cards it spends load GY Traps for Susanoo. Its bounce (targeted) fires whenever any card becomes Set, including End Phase Sets and Mikumari-no-Mikoto's off-turn Sets.

Combo Lines

The one trick behind every line

  1. Set a card
  2. Reveal it, take it back
  3. Summon an Ashtra monster
  4. Set it again for the next one

Each monster's Summon is once per turn per name, but the card paying for them can be reused.

Two free Level 7 bodies and two Set cards on your turn, then an off-turn Synchro when your opponent moves.

Extra Deck Toolbox

  • Graydle Dragon: Domain + Mikumari counts two WATER materials, so it destroys 2 of your opponent's cards on the Synchro Summon.
  • Crystron Quariongandrax: two Trap Tuners + a Level 7 banishes up to 3 monsters from the field and/or GY.
  • Mecha Phantom Beast Dracossack: Mikumari + Yoa, both Level 7, on your own turn.
  • Level 10 Synchros (e.g. Baronne de Fleur): Paleozoic builds reach them with LV1 Trap Tuner + LV2 Paleozoic + LV7 Ashtra.

Non-Engine Support

  • Audhumla, Progenitor of the Frozen Expanse: Konami's own recommended partner. It swaps Set cards for draws, and its Quick Effect protects every face-down card you control from destruction and targeting for the turn.
  • Synchro Emergency: can be activated from the hand when only your opponent controls a monster, which helps the deck's worst matchup, going second.
  • Generic Normal Traps: Mikumari and Mikumari-no-Mikoto recycle them, so high-impact picks like Infinite Impermanence and Compulsory Evacuation Device get replayed.

Hybrid Engines

Pure Ashtra has no one-card combo and a weak turn two. These partners each fix a specific gap.

First Penguin

First Penguin

Fixes: no 1-card starter

It Summons itself onto an empty field, searches Mikumari by revealing Mikumari-no-Mikoto, then flips face-down to pay for two Ashtra Summons.

Its Extra Deck lock is WATER-only for the turn, so Susanoo waits until the opponent's turn.

Arias the Labrynth Butler

Arias the Labrynth Butler

Fixes: going second

As a Main Phase Quick Effect it Sets a Normal Trap from your hand, and that Trap can be activated the same turn.

An Ashtra Trap Set by Arias can become a Tuner and Synchro on the turn it lands, which directly fixes the deck's biggest weakness.

Lady Labrynth of the Silver Castle

Lady Labrynth of the Silver Castle

Fixes: trap density

Ashtra always controls a Set card, which keeps her protected. When a Normal Trap is activated, she Sets a different Normal Trap from the Deck.

Ashtra Traps trigger her, and her Set triggers Ashtrashen's bounce. As a Level 8 she is also Susanoo material with a Trap Tuner.

Paleozoic Canadia

Paleozoic Traps

Fixes: Extra Deck reach

Whenever a Trap is activated, Paleozoics in the GY Summon themselves as Level 2 WATER Aqua monsters, which opens Level 10 lines.

They also stock the GY with Traps for Susanoo. Banishing them to pay Susanoo, though, ends their own loop.

Audhumla, Progenitor of the Frozen Expanse

Audhumla, Progenitor of the Frozen Expanse

Fixes: backrow hate

It Special Summons itself once 4+ cards are on the field. Its Quick Effect flips it face-down and shields all your face-down cards for the turn.

It answers Harpie's Feather Duster and Lightning Storm, the cards that hurt this deck most.

Casual builds have also paired Ashtra with Tindangle and Enneacraft. Note that Enneacraft's Summon restriction covers the whole turn, so Ashtra plays must come first.

Going First vs. Going Second

First: this is where the deck shines. A 1.5-card line leaves two bodies and two Set cards. The opponent's turn then brings a Trap-Tuner Synchro with non-targeting removal, Yoa's Level shift, and End Phase refuelling from Akasuna or Susanoo.

Second: this is the deck's weak point. Traps can't be activated the turn they are Set, and hand Summons need a face-down card that an established board can simply remove. Pure Ashtra can't threaten a Synchro on turn two. Arias, Synchro Emergency and generic board breakers are the fixes.

Format outlook: the OCG launched it into a Toon-dominated format and results were poor. The TCG has no equivalent pressure, and the low Genesys costs make it an easy fit there.

Strengths

  • Every Ashtra Trap is a self-contained Synchro starter: Tuner and starter in one card, with nothing committed from hand.
  • Level 7 and 8 bodies Summon themselves for free, and one Set card pays for several of them.
  • Most removal doesn't target: Mikumari-no-Mikoto's send, Susanoo's banish, and Akasuna's mass flip.
  • The Susanoo Loop and three End Phase Set effects make the deck exceptional in long games.

Critical Weaknesses

  • No true one-card combo; pure builds rely on two-card hands or First Penguin.
  • Face-down cards are the engine, so backrow hate damages consistency, not just disruption.
  • Going second into an established board is close to unwinnable without hybrid help.
  • Hard once-per-turn on nearly every effect, including one activation per Trap name, limits how far a single turn can go.
