Lore Archive

"The Enneacraft are nine autonomous mecha that watch over the city of Enneapolis, each one judging a different sin. With the advent of the Blazing Dominion protocols, their formations have evolved from reactive traps into a formalized doctrine of applied statistics—reducing opponent actions to mathematically zeroed variables."

The futuristic city of Enneapolis established nine sins: anger, arrogance, deception, envy, greed, dread, gluttony, longing and sloth. Nine autonomous mecha, the Enneacraft, watch its residents at all times. Each sin moves a sin count forward, and when it builds up, the Enneacraft that governs that sin manifests in the city, its octahedron expanding into an animal form, and begins disciplining mankind (The Valuable Book EX 6).

  1. The 2nd Enneacraft, which judged those arrogant enough to interfere with the sublime Enneacraft disciplining program.

  2. The 3rd Enneacraft, which judged the deceitful, smeared in ostentation and suppression.

  3. The 5th Enneacraft, which judged the greedy, whose exploits for profit knew no boundaries.

  4. The 6th Enneacraft, which judged those who recklessly caused terror on feebler lifeforms.

  5. The 8th Enneacraft, which judged those who longed for salvation greater than they deserved.

  6. A sin is detected, an Enneacraft deploys, and the sinner is disciplined, then reset: a future monitored from top to bottom, from beginning to end.

Executive Summary

Phase 1: Reactive Obscurity

The original Enneacraft archetype functions as a psychological minefield. By fusing Pendulum Summoning and Flip Effects, the deck subverts the "Face-Up" nature of modern play. Threats are hidden in Face-Down Defense Position, effectively "blanking" common interactions like Infinite Impermanence. Every monster flipped face-up puts an E.C. Counter on each Enneacraft in your Pendulum Zones, and Enneapolis turns them into 900 damage each in the End Phase.

Phase 2: Blazing Dominion Advancements

The recent Blazing Dominion core expansion elevated the deck into a "Full Gas" engine of attrition. The strategy pivots sharply away from probability toward a doctrine of "applied statistics". Utilizing the new multi-layered tools, the deck calculates interactions as a "Simultaneous Equation Canon"—systematically nullifying opposing variables, dictating Chain Links, and evading targeted removal entirely.

Core Operational Mechanics

The Face-Down Lock

"You cannot Special Summon... except in Face-Down Defense Position."

Every Enneacraft reveals itself from the hand to Special Summon a monster face-down, and for the rest of that turn you can only Special Summon face-down, which shuts off your Extra Deck. In return, the monsters arrive face-down, where effects that need a face-up monster, like Infinite Impermanence or Effect Veiler, can't touch them. The searches can still be hit by Ash Blossom.

The Sinquisition Economy

Formula: D = 900 × n (where n = E.C. Counters)

Each time a monster is flipped face-up, every Enneacraft in your Pendulum Zones gets 1 E.C. Counter. In the End Phase, Enneapolis removes them all and deals 900 damage for each, so two Enneacraft scales count every flip twice.

Deterministic Searching

The Illusion of Probability (Rule of Threes)

Every Level 1 Enneacraft, exapatisIA included, has the same Pendulum Effect: pay 900 LP, reveal 3 Enneacraft cards from your Deck, and your opponent picks 1 at random for your hand. Nothing stops you revealing three copies of the same card, which makes the pick certain. Each Level 1 can do it once per turn.

Sovereignty Over The Stack

Chain Link Erasure & Response Lock

Atil.SPIA punishes long chains. When your opponent activates a card effect as Chain Link 3 or later, it flips face-up and negates and destroys every effect they had already activated on that chain. While it stays face-up, they can't chain effects to their own effect activations.

Strengths

  • Face-down setup: Monsters summoned face-down can't be hit by Infinite Impermanence or Effect Veiler.
  • Chain control: Face-up, Atil.SPIA stops your opponent chaining effects to their own.
  • Second flip: Enneacraft Reset in the GY turns your Enneacraft face-down again in response, so they can flip a second time.

Weaknesses

  • Droll & Lock Bird: Remains a critical chokepoint if searching starts early.
  • Non-destruction removal: Banishing, bouncing or shuffling gets around Archa.TAIL, which only stops destruction.
  • Pacing/Time: Highly reactive nature makes it vulnerable to tournament end-of-match procedures.
