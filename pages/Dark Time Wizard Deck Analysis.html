Lore Archive

"Joey never had the best cards in the room. He had the nerve to call the coin anyway."

Joey Wheeler (Katsuya Jonouchi in Japan) started Yu-Gi-Oh! as the weakest duelist in Yugi's circle and fought his way to the Battle City finals. His deck was built from what he won along the way: Red-Eyes Black Dragon, taken from Rex Raptor in Duelist Kingdom, and Jinzo, won from Espa Roba during Battle City. Around them sat the cards that made him who he was as a duelist, the ones that asked him to trust a coin or a die when the odds were against him.

Two ideas run through that deck. The Gambler: Time Wizard's coin toss, Graceful Dice and Skull Dice. The Aged: Time Wizard's time magic, which turned Baby Dragon into a thousand-year-old Thousand Dragon. Beyond the Brave rebuilds the Battle City side of that deck around a new Quick-Play Spell, Dark Time Wizard, and gives Joey his first real engine.

Executive Summary: The Luck of the Draw

Until Beyond the Brave, a Joey deck was a tribute rather than an archetype. His cards share no name, no Type and no way to search each other. What ties them together is how Joey played them: Time Wizard's coin toss, Graceful Dice and Skull Dice, and Fusions like Thousand Dragon and Alligator's Sword Dragon that turn two small Normal Monsters into one bigger threat.

Played as they are, the classic cards win when the coin does. Time Wizard destroys your own monsters half the time, the dice swing ATK by 100 to 600, and each Fusion needs Polymerization plus two specific cards. This tab keeps that deck and its history on record. The Beyond the Brave tab covers the ten new cards that make it a real strategy.

The Gambler & The Aged

The Gambler

Coin Tosses

Time Wizard tosses one coin once per turn. Called right, it destroys every monster your opponent controls. Called wrong, it destroys your monsters and you take half their ATK as damage. Second Coin Toss lets you redo one toss a turn.

The Gambler

Dice Rolls

Graceful Dice gives all your monsters the result × 100 ATK and DEF for the turn, and Skull Dice takes the same from all of your opponent's. Roulette Barrel rolls twice and destroys a monster whose Level matches one result.

The Aged

Time Magic

Time Wizard and Baby Dragon fuse into Thousand Dragon, a Baby Dragon a thousand years older. Time Wizard of Tomorrow (Time Wizard + 1 Effect Monster) tosses a coin to destroy every monster on the field.

Classic Competitive Analysis

Strengths

  • A board wipe on a coin: one Normal Summoned Time Wizard can clear your opponent's field.
  • Flexible material: Baby Dragon feeds both Thousand Dragon and Alligator's Sword Dragon.
  • Trap lock: Jinzo shuts off every Trap on the field while it's face-up.

Weaknesses

  • Half the time, it backfires: a wrong call destroys your own monsters and damages you.
  • No searchers: nothing in the classic set adds a card from the Deck.
  • Costs: Panther Warrior needs a Tribute every attack, and Graverobber costs 2000 LP. Jinzo also locks your own Skull Dice and Graverobber.

Synergies & Tech

  • Second Coin Toss: redo one coin toss a turn, which turns a 50% call into 75%.
  • Umi: switches on The Legendary Fisherman's protection. See the Umi guide.
  • Red-Eyes support: Joey's dragon has its own Red-Eyes guide.

Beyond the Brave: What Changed

  • TCG · Beyond the Brave · 8 Oct 2026
  • OCG · 18 Jul 2026

Beyond the Brave's cover theme is Joey's Battle City deck, rebuilt around a new Quick-Play Spell, Dark Time Wizard. Seven of the ten cards below mention it, so the monsters fetch the Spells, the Spells summon the monsters, and the Traps recycle both. Jinzo - Energy Shocker, Fisherman of Legend and Red-Eyes Black Dragon Exceed from the same set round out the deck. The Classic Analysis tab still covers the originals.

A Gamble With a Safety Net

Dark Time Wizard's first effect is a plain search that comes back in the End Phase. Its coin toss unlocks Red-Eyes Black Dragon Exceed whichever way it lands.

One Name Ties It Together

Alligator's Dragon Knight, Dark Time Wizard, Swiftwind Panther Warrior and Foolish Graverobber all fetch, summon or send "cards that mention Dark Time Wizard".

Luck You Can Count

The dice destroy on 26 of 36 rolls, the coins pay out either way, and Second Coin Toss turns a 50% call into 75%. The Chance Lab below has the numbers.

Dark Time Wizard

Spell · the engine

Dark Time Wizard

Card
Quick-Play Spell
Luck
1 coin toss (second effect)

Card text

  1. ActivateActivate 1 of these effects (but you can only use each effect of "Dark Time Wizard" once per turn);
    • Add 1 card that mentions "Dark Time Wizard" from your Deck to your hand, except "Dark Time Wizard", also during the End Phase of this turn, add 1 "Dark Time Wizard" from your GY to your hand.
    • Toss a coin and call it. If you call it right, destroy as many monsters your opponent controls as possible, and if you do, inflict damage to your opponent equal to half the combined original ATK of those destroyed monsters. If you call it wrong, destroy all monsters you control.

Two cards in one. The first effect is safe card advantage: it adds any card that mentions Dark Time Wizard and brings itself back in the End Phase, ready for next turn. The second is Time Wizard's gamble as a Quick-Play: a board wipe plus burn if you call it right, your own board if you don't.

Either result counts as Dark Time Wizard destroying a monster, which is exactly what Red-Eyes Black Dragon Exceed's procedure needs. Set it, and you can flip it on your opponent's turn, where both effects are available again.

Retrains Time Wizard: a wrong call no longer damages you, and a right call now burns your opponent.

Second Coin Toss

A classic Continuous Spell: once per turn, when a coin toss is performed, you can choose to redo it. A wrong call only sticks if the redo is wrong too, so one toss a turn succeeds 75% of the time (1 − ½ × ½). It works on Dark Time Wizard, Reversal Box, Just Flip a Coin and Time Wizard. It does nothing for dice.

Rules That Decide Your Turn

  • Right or wrong, Exceed is open

    Red-Eyes Black Dragon Exceed only needs a monster destroyed by Dark Time Wizard that turn, on either field. After a wrong call your field is empty, so Tribute one of your opponent's monsters instead. Tributing doesn't target or destroy, so it gets past most protection.

  • A wrong call takes the Tokens too

    Dark Time Wizard destroys all monsters you control, Sacrificial Sheep Tokens included, so don't count on Sleepy Scapegoats to save anything from your own misfire. Its shield is for your opponent's removal and for battle.

  • Once per turn, per effect

    Each Dark Time Wizard effect is once per turn, so search with one copy and toss with another. A Set copy can be flipped on your opponent's turn, where both effects are fresh. From the hand it only works on your own turn.

  • Only 7 of the 10 mention Dark Time Wizard

    Revenge of the Paper Dolls, Crow, the Carrion Cutpurse and Just Flip a Coin don't. Alligator's Dragon Knight and Dark Time Wizard can't add them, and Panther and Graverobber can't send them.

  • Tokens pay costs

    Sacrificial Sheep Tokens can't be Tributed for a Tribute Summon that turn. Swiftwind Panther Warrior's effect and Exceed's procedure aren't Tribute Summons, so the Tokens pay for both.

  • The Scapegoats' lock

    After Sleepy Scapegoats, only Fusion Monsters come out of your Extra Deck for the rest of the turn. Exceed is a Fusion Monster; Rank 4 Xyz and Link Monsters are not.

  • Flip Reversal Box early

    It only collects counters in a Standby Phase while it's face-up. Activate it in your opponent's Draw Phase, and it rolls in their Standby Phase before they can do much.

  • Jinzo's lock needs a partner

    Jinzo - Energy Shocker stops your opponent's Traps while you have another monster that mentions Dark Time Wizard on your field or in your GY. A Dragon Knight or Panther in the GY keeps the lock on after your field is cleared.

Weak Points to Play Around

  • One search to stop

    Ash Blossom on Alligator's Dragon Knight or on Dark Time Wizard's search takes most of your turn. Panther's summon from the Deck is another Ash target.

  • The coin still matters

    A wrong call still gives you Exceed, but you lose your own board to get it. Players put it bluntly: going second, it's "a 50% chance of a Raigeki, and a 50% chance of a Kaiju."

  • Trap-heavy hands

    Five of the ten cards are Traps. Going second into a full board, a hand of Reversal Box, Crow and Just Flip a Coin does very little.

  • Reversal Box is slow

    You don't choose how many counters you get, and each one is a coin toss. One player called it "the epitome of a card that's simply not reliable enough for a competitive environment."

  • Extra Deck limits

    Sleepy Scapegoats locks you into Fusion Monsters for the turn, and the deck has only one Fusion that fits.

  • GY disruption

    Graceful & Skull Dice and Foolish Graverobber do their best work from the GY. D.D. Crow and Macro Cosmos shut them off.

Verdict

Beyond the Brave gives Joey a deck that plays like Joey: every turn has a gamble in it, and most gambles pay out either way. It's consistent for a luck deck, with Alligator's Dragon Knight and Dark Time Wizard as true one-card starters, a Trap lock going first, and a going-second wipe that ends in a 3400-ATK monster whichever way the coin lands. It isn't a top competitive deck. In one player's words, it's "an extremely fair deck in an extremely unfair game", and in early OCG play it mostly showed up as a small engine. As a casual deck and a nostalgia deck it's excellent, and the Dark Time Wizard package is worth testing in Red-Eyes and other decks that want a going-second wipe.
