Trirealm Rift

Eight Psychic monsters that bank your Deck face-down, then play from the banishment.

Lore Archive

“The touch of these bizarre creatures defies the laws of physics, banishing cards in your Deck to the reverse side of the universe where only you can see and use them!” Konami, Glorious Victors product page

No Trirealm Rift story has been published yet, so the slideshow follows the cards' own artwork, Level by Level. In the OCG all eight monsters are named after underworlds; the TCG gives them plainer English names.

  1. Lightning splits the sky over a valley of mountains and a fiery volcano, and triangle-shaped rifts glow open in the storm: Trirealm Rift Vilvortex. The theme's Japanese name, 異解△ (Ikai), is 異界, "other world", with its second character swapped for the sound-alike 解, "to unleash".
  2. The smallest to come through is Level 1 Hollows, a horned little creature with violet eyes. Its Japanese name pairs Gehenna, the place of punishment in Judaism, with 虚, "emptiness".
  3. Level 2 Heavens crackles in a cage of golden lightning, a bright core in its chest. In Japanese it pairs Sheol, the land of the dead in Judaism, with 天, "sky".
  4. Level 3 Blizzards rises in blue over snowy peaks. In Japanese it is Tuonela, the land of the dead in Finnish myth, with 碧, "blue".
  5. Level 4 Blaze burns red above molten rock. In Japanese it is Naraka, the hell realm of the Indian religions, with 緋, "scarlet".
  6. Level 5 Havocs spreads huge pale wings in a storm of lightning. It reads as Heavens grown up: both are LIGHT, and both carry 天, "sky", in their Japanese names. Its own is Yomi, the land of the dead in Shinto myth, under 天霆, "heaven's thunder".
  7. Level 6 Gorging Gorge bursts out of shattered rock, a white-masked face with a gaping mouth in a mane of dark tendrils. In Japanese it is named after Helheim, the Norse land of the dead, under 葬嶺, "burial peak".
  8. Level 7 Savage Storms tears up out of the ground in green light, a fanged maw ringed by glowing eyes. In Japanese it is the Ploutonion, the ancient Greek sanctuary of Pluto, god of the underworld, under 狂嵐, "mad tempest".
  9. In Trirealm Rift Delectation (異解△福音, "gospel"), Savage Storms' whirlwind tears through a city, buildings and all.
  10. Last comes the Level 8, Darkness, all wings and pink eyes. Its Japanese name is Jahannam, the hell of Islam, and it reads as Hollows grown up: both are DARK, both carry 虚, "emptiness", and Gehenna is the Jewish counterpart of Jahannam.
  11. In Trirealm Rift Verdict (異解△審判, "judgment"), Darkness hangs in the middle of the rift, ringed by the triangular portals of all seven others.

Overview: a second Deck, face-down

  • New to the TCG in Glorious Victors, 3 December 2026
  • OCG: Deck-Build Pack: Glorious Victors, 5 September 2026

Trirealm Rift Darkness

Trirealm Rift is a Main Deck archetype of eight Psychic monsters, one at each Level from 1 to 8, plus three Spells and Traps. It has no Extra Deck monsters of its own.

Every Trirealm Rift monster banishes cards from the top of your Deck face-down when it is Normal or Special Summoned, as many as its Level. Those cards aren't lost: every search and summon in the archetype works from your face-down banished cards. Trirealm Rift Hollows and Heavens add them to your hand, Blizzards, Blaze and Delectation Special Summon the Level 5 and higher ones, Gorging Gorge summons almost any of them, and Havocs and Vilvortex take back any Trirealm Rift card. The banishment works like a second Deck that only you can draw from.

Because none of those effects touches the Deck, Ash Blossom & Joyous Spring and Droll & Lock Bird can't stop them. Ash only reaches the generic cards the deck plays: Small World, Pot of Desires, Emergency Teleport, Terraforming, and Savage Storms' draw. Only face-down banished cards count. A card banished face-up is out of the archetype's reach.

Three rules before your first combo

  1. The mills are random, the setup isn't

    A monster's summon banishes whatever is on top of your Deck. To put a chosen card face-down, use Small World (the card you reveal and the bridge), Savage Storms (itself and another Trirealm Rift card from your hand), Havocs or Gorging Gorge (each banishes itself), Delectation (a Level 4 or lower monster you control), Eater of Millions, or the 5-card costs of Vilvortex and Delectation paid from your GY.

  2. Five effects lock you in

    After Hollows' add, Heavens' add, Blizzards' or Blaze's summon, or Verdict's effect, you can only activate Trirealm Rift cards and effects for the rest of that turn. Play Small World, Pot of Desires, Emergency Teleport, Terraforming and Gizmek Orochi first. Gorging Gorge, Havocs, Savage Storms, Vilvortex, Delectation and Darkness don't lock you.

  3. Darkness counts both sides

    Trirealm Rift Darkness counts every face-down banished card, yours and your opponent's, Extra Deck cards included. Gizmek Orochi and Eater of Millions banish Extra Deck cards face-down, and those count too.

How the deck wins

Early on, the deck wins with Trirealm Rift Darkness, a 3000 ATK Level 8 whose effects switch on as the face-down pile grows:

  1. 10+Can't be destroyed by battle.
  2. 20+Once per turn, as a Quick Effect, target and destroy 1 card on the field.
  3. 30+Once per turn, in your opponent's End Phase, add 1 of your face-down banished cards (any card) to your hand.
  4. 40+Both players skip their Draw Phases.

A full turn-one line reaches 20: Lines 1 and 4 in the Combo Lines tab end on 23 and 22 face-down cards with a 40-card Deck. Pot of Desires (10) and Gizmek Orochi (8) push it toward 30. Around Darkness, the other monsters interrupt by Attribute. Havocs negates a monster effect your opponent activates by returning a face-down monster of the same Attribute to your Deck, and Savage Storms shuffles away an opposing monster the same way. Gorging Gorge keeps your opponent's card effects from targeting or destroying your other Trirealm Rift cards.

The endgame: an empty Deck

Three cards reward you for having no cards left in your Deck:

  • Trirealm Rift Vilvortex: while you control a Level 5 or higher Trirealm Rift monster and your Deck is empty, your opponent can't activate cards or effects during your turn.
  • Trirealm Rift Verdict: with your Deck empty, once per battle, a Trirealm Rift monster's opponent in battle has its ATK set to 0 during damage calculation.
  • Trirealm Rift Darkness at 40+: nobody draws in the Draw Phase, so an empty Deck can't make you lose there.

The plan is to banish the rest of your Deck during your Main Phase, then attack while your opponent can't respond. Without 40 face-down cards, keep one card to draw: Savage Storms' Quick Effect and Havocs' negate both shuffle a face-down monster back into your Deck. Every summon's banish is mandatory, so plan that card before the last mill, not after.

Do the effects use it?

Yes, in four ways, though no effect adds Levels together and none needs an exact total.

  • The banish count matches the Level. Hollows banishes 1, Heavens 2, and so on up to Darkness's 8. Each card prints its own number rather than saying "equal to its Level", so changing a monster's Level wouldn't change it. Summoning all eight once banishes 36 cards, and the bigger monsters feed Darkness's count fastest.
  • The low half summons the high half. Blizzards (3) and Blaze (4) Special Summon Level 5 or higher monsters from the banishment, and Delectation trades a monster whose original Level is 4 or lower for one. Levels 1–4 are the starters and extenders; Levels 5–8 are the payoffs, waiting face-down until they're called.
  • Each middle monster skips its own Level. Havocs can't add a Level 5 monster and Gorging Gorge can't summon a Level 6. They are the only Level 5 and Level 6 cards, so in practice neither can pick itself. Vilvortex's lock needs a Level 5 or higher Trirealm Rift monster on your field.
  • It makes the archetype a Small World network. No two of them share a Level, ATK or DEF, so most pairs share exactly one property, their Type, which is what Small World asks for. The exceptions are the two pairs that also share an Attribute: Hollows and Darkness (both DARK), and Heavens and Havocs (both LIGHT). Those pairs share two properties, so neither card in a pair can be the bridge for the other.

The Attributes matter too. Havocs, Savage Storms and Verdict all match a face-down monster's Attribute to your opponent's monster, and the eight cover six of them: DARK (Hollows, Darkness), LIGHT (Heavens, Havocs), WATER, FIRE, EARTH and WIND. Only DIVINE is missing.

What Vilvortex, Delectation and Verdict add

The monsters alone can only reach what the mills happen to banish. The three non-monster cards fix that in different ways.

CardPaysGives every turnWith an empty Deck
Vilvortex5 face-down (GY and/or Deck)1 face-down Trirealm Rift card to your handYour opponent can't activate on your turn (needs a Level 5+ on your field)
Delectation5 face-down (GY and/or Deck)An extra Trirealm Rift Normal Summon, and a Level 4-or-lower for Level 5+ tradeNothing extra
VerdictNothing (a Set Trap)A same-Attribute answer to each opposing summonOpposing ATK becomes 0 in battle
  • Vilvortex is the only search you can repeat every turn. Its cost adds 5 to Darkness's count, and its add takes any face-down Trirealm Rift card, Spells and Traps included. It works from the banishment, so Ash Blossom can't touch it. With Terraforming it is effectively a fourth or fifth copy. Line 4 uses it to take back the Small World bridge.
  • Delectation turns a used starter into a payoff. After Hollows or Blizzards has done its job, Delectation banishes it face-down (where Havocs or Vilvortex can take it back later) and summons a Level 5 or higher monster in its place. The extra Normal Summon gives you a second Level 1–4 for the same turn. Line 1 uses the trade to swap Blizzards for Gorging Gorge.
  • Verdict is the opponent's-turn card. It has no once-per-turn limit beyond "a different Attribute each time", so a turn of several summons can trigger it more than once. It reaches its best late, when your Deck is empty and every battle goes your way. Its lock also stops your hand traps for the rest of that turn.

Paying the 5-card costs from your GY instead of the Deck keeps your Deck from running out early. In Line 1, Delectation's cost takes Small World from the GY and only 4 cards from the Deck.

The Glorious Victors reprints

Glorious Victors reprints four older cards next to Trirealm Rift, and all four banish cards face-down. Here is what each does for this deck, going by its card text.

Small World
Helps a lot

Small World

Reveal a monster in your hand, choose a bridge in your Deck that shares exactly one property with it, and add a monster that shares exactly one with the bridge. The revealed card and the bridge are both banished face-down, so it puts two chosen monsters where Blizzards, Gorging Gorge and Hollows can use them. The ladder makes almost any pair work (see the bridge finder). Lines 1, 2 and 4 use it; Ash Blossom can stop it.

Pot of Desires
Helps a lot

Pot of Desires

Its cost banishes the top 10 cards face-down, then you draw 2. In most decks that cost is a price; here it adds 10 to Darkness's count and usually banishes Trirealm Rift cards where Hollows, Heavens, Havocs and Vilvortex can reach them. It's once per turn and not a Trirealm Rift card, so play it before any lock.

Gizmek Orochi, the Serpentron Sky Slasher
Helps

Gizmek Orochi, the Serpentron Sky Slasher

A Quick Effect from your hand or GY banishes the top 8 cards face-down to Special Summon it, on either player's turn. That is 8 more for Darkness and a 2450 ATK body. It can instead banish 3 Extra Deck cards face-down to destroy a face-up monster, but only one Orochi effect per turn. For Small World it connects only to Hollows: it shares both DARK and Level 8 with Darkness.

Eater of Millions
Situational

Eater of Millions

It is Special Summoned from the hand by banishing 5 or more of your cards face-down from your hand, field and/or Extra Deck. That summon isn't an activation, so it still works after a Trirealm Rift lock. Trirealm Rift monsters banished this way become face-down resources, and Extra Deck cards banished this way count for Darkness. It gains 100 ATK and DEF per face-down banished card, but it can't be Tributed or used for a Fusion, Synchro or Xyz Summon. For Small World it links to Darkness (DARK is all they share).

Why it works: Vilvortex's add takes the Small World bridge, Gorging Gorge, back to your hand. Gorging Gorge banishes itself to summon Darkness, and Blizzards summons Gorging Gorge again. Watch for: Ash Blossom on Small World, and Spell/Trap removal on Vilvortex before Gorging Gorge is on the field.

What the mills usually add

The lines above leave out luck, but most real turns also get something from the random banishes. The table assumes the test deck with a typical opening: two Trirealm Rift monsters in your hand, one of them Level 5 or higher, which leaves 18 Trirealm Rift monsters (9 of them Level 5+) in the 35-card Deck.

Emergency Teleport banishes its monster face-up in the End Phase, which keeps it out of the archetype's reach. Use that monster up first, or trade it away with Delectation, which banishes it face-down.

Weaknesses and counters

  • Anything that stops banishing. Imperial Iron Wall (neither player can banish) shuts off every summon's mill, every hand effect, and the costs of Small World, Pot of Desires, Vilvortex and Delectation. Artifact Lancea does the same for one of your turns. Keep Spell/Trap removal in the Side Deck, and play around a Set Lancea where you can.
  • The locks. After Hollows, Heavens, Blizzards, Blaze or Verdict, only Trirealm Rift cards can be activated for the rest of that turn, hand traps included. Using Blaze or Verdict on your opponent's turn turns off your own hand traps for the rest of it.
  • Monster-effect negation before the shield. Effect Veiler or Infinite Impermanence on Blizzards or Hollows stops a line. Once Gorging Gorge is on the field it can't be targeted for this, but Gorging Gorge itself can.
  • Removal that neither targets nor destroys. Gorging Gorge stops targeting and destruction only. Effects that banish, send to the GY, shuffle or negate without targeting still work, and nothing protects Gorging Gorge.
  • Decking out. Every summon's banish is mandatory, so a long turn can empty your Deck before you're ready. Without Darkness at 40+, keep a way to put a card back (Savage Storms or Havocs), or you lose at your next draw.
  • Hand traps on the generic cards. Ash Blossom can negate Small World, Pot of Desires, Emergency Teleport, Terraforming and Savage Storms' draw. Droll & Lock Bird after one of them stops further draws and Deck adds, but not the adds from the banishment. Mulcharmy Purulia draws whenever you summon from the hand, and Hollows, Heavens, Blizzards and Blaze all do. Mulcharmy Fuwalos only counts summons from the Deck or Extra Deck, so it misses everything from the banishment.
