DORIADO

Analysis of the Elemental Lineage

Lore Archive

"Elemental Mistress Doriado is the convergence of all six attributes—a being of perfect elemental harmony. Her mere presence on the field transforms the attribute landscape, enabling strategies that require specific elemental compositions. She is the prism through which all elemental light passes, refracting it into tactical advantage."

Executive Summary

The "Doriado" series is not a conventional archetype, but a thematic lineage of monsters united by a singular concept: mastery over the elemental Attributes. This collection—a Ritual monster, a Pendulum monster, and a powerful Nomi monster—forms the basis for unique and challenging strategies. Their power is not in isolation but in their synergistic potential with each other and a vast pool of generic support that enables their demanding mechanics.

The Elemental Conclave

Elemental Mistress Doriado

The elemental nexus. As a Ritual Monster, her continuous effect treats her as WIND, WATER, FIRE, and EARTH simultaneously, enabling powerful support cards that require multiple attributes on the field.

Dark Doriado

The pendulum oracle. Its monster effect allows you to stack the top of your deck with one monster of each of the four main elements, giving you unparalleled control over your future draws.

Elemental Grace Doriado

The ascended matriarch. This boss monster requires 6+ different Attributes in the graveyards to be summoned and can negate opponent's summons multiple times in a turn.

Blueprint: Mistress Control

This strategy focuses on summoning Elemental Mistress Doriado to enable the devastating trap card, Fuh-Rin-Ka-Zan, which mimics four of the most powerful banned spell cards in the game's history.

Competitive Analysis

Strengths

  • Element of Surprise: As a rogue strategy, opponents are unlikely to know your choke points.
  • High-Impact Payoffs: A resolved Fuh-Rin-Ka-Zan or Elemental Grace Doriado can win the game on the spot.
  • Engine Compatibility: Can be integrated with various powerful generic engines (Ritual, Sky Striker, Lightsworn).

Weaknesses

  • Normal Summon Reliance: Key combos often depend on a single Normal Summon, making them vulnerable to hand traps.
  • Graveyard Dependency: The Grace Turbo strategy is completely shut down by Graveyard-hate like D.D. Crow or Dimension Shifter.
  • Fragile Boards: The Mistress Control setup is easily dismantled by simple Spell/Trap removal or monster negation.

Key Synergies

  • Sky Striker Engine: The most efficient way to summon Elemental Grace Doriado, providing all 6 attributes from a one-card starter.
  • Ritual Support: Generic searchers like Manju and Preparation of Rites are mandatory for the Mistress Control variant.
  • Elementsaber Engine: A slower but thematically appropriate partner for filling the Graveyard with attributes over time.

Beyond the Brave: Elemental Saga Doriado

  • TCG · Beyond the Brave · 8 Oct 2026
  • OCG · 18 Jul 2026

Beyond the Brave adds a new Doriado, and she's the first one that works outside her own theme. Elemental Saga Doriado is a Level 7 LIGHT Spellcaster with no Ritual Spell and no Summoning condition. Once the monsters in your GY cover three different Attributes, she Special Summons herself from your hand along with any monster in your GY. From the field, she then Special Summons a monster from your Deck whose Attribute you don't have yet. Neither player can respond to either effect. The Mistress and Grace strategies in the Classic Analysis tab are unchanged. This tab covers how Saga fits into them and into other multi-Attribute decks.

Nothing Can Be Chained

Neither player can activate cards or effects in response to either of her effects. Ash Blossom, Effect Veiler, Infinite Impermanence, Ghost Belle and Called by the Grave can't be chained to her.

Three Monsters from One Card

From your hand, she Special Summons herself and any monster in your GY. On the field, she Special Summons one more from your Deck. None of this uses your Normal Summon.

A Tutor Keyed to Attributes

The Deck summon takes any monster whose Attribute isn't on your field or in your GY, including floodgates, Tuners and engine pieces. It arrives with its effects intact.

    Rules That Decide Your Turn

    • Only monsters in your GY count

      Doriado in your hand doesn't count. Neither do Spells and Traps such as Infinite Impermanence, or anything in your opponent's GY.

    • A revive can switch off the Deck summon

      The monster you revive leaves the GY. If no other monster there shares its Attribute, you drop below three. Revive a monster whose Attribute another GY monster shares, or get a fourth Attribute in first.

    • LIGHT is never a legal pull

      You use the Deck summon from the field, and Doriado is LIGHT. The pull checks your field and GY together, but the three-Attribute condition only checks your GY.

    • The lock covers her activations, not your turn

      Continuous effects already on the field still apply. With a Barrier Statue of another Attribute out, she can't be Special Summoned at all. If you activate another card between her two effects, your opponent can respond to that card, including with Effect Veiler on Doriado.

    • Some monsters can't be pulled

      Elemental Mistress Doriado is a Ritual Monster. Elemental Grace Doriado and the Elemental Lords can only be Special Summoned from the hand by their own condition. All three stay in the Deck. Everything else arrives with its effects working.

    • Each effect is once per turn by name

      A second copy can't revive or pull again in the same turn. Plan which copy uses which effect.

    • Declared Attributes count

      An Elementsaber declared as a new Attribute counts as that Attribute while it stays in the GY, until the end of the turn. Revive it and it has its printed Attribute again on the field.

    • Nibiru can't be chained to her

      Lines 1 and 2 reach five summons with Doriado's revive or the Fusion just before it. Go straight into the Deck summon after that. Both end on Barrier Statue of the Abyss, and Nibiru is LIGHT, so it can't be Special Summoned once the Abyss is out.

    Elemental Saga Doriado vs. Summon Sorceress

    Players compare her to Summon Sorceress because both pull a monster from the Deck. The printed text shows how differently they do it.

    Elemental Saga Doriado compared with Summon Sorceress
    Summon Sorceress Elemental Saga Doriado
    Where it lives Extra Deck (Link-3), available every game Main Deck, so you have to draw or add it
    What it needs 2+ monsters with the same Type, except Tokens 3+ different Attributes among the monsters in your GY
    What it pulls A monster with the same Type as one it points to, in Defense Position, with its effects negated Any monster whose Attribute is missing from your field and GY, with its effects intact
    Responses Can be chained to as usual Neither player can respond

    Takeaway: Summon Sorceress is always available, but everything it pulls is negated. Doriado has to be drawn or added to your hand, but her pulls keep their effects, so a floodgate works the moment it lands.

    Saga and the Rest of the Doriado Line

    Saga doesn't replace the classic cards. Here is how she works with each of them.

    Elemental Mistress Doriado

    Elemental Mistress Doriado

    Saga can't pull her, because she's a Ritual Monster. Once she's been Ritual Summoned and sent to the GY, Saga's hand effect can revive her, which turns a Set Fuh-Rin-Ka-Zan back on.

    On the field she's also treated as WIND, WATER, FIRE and EARTH, so DARK is the only Attribute Saga can pull while Mistress is face-up. In the GY she counts only as LIGHT.

    Dark Doriado

    Dark Doriado

    A legal pull whenever DARK is missing, and the natural one next to Mistress. Its monster effect triggers on a Special Summon too: stack an EARTH, WATER, FIRE and WIND monster on top of your Deck in any order.

    In the Pendulum Zone, it gives your EARTH, WATER, FIRE and WIND monsters 200 ATK/DEF for each different Attribute among the monsters you control.

    Elemental Grace Doriado

    Elemental Grace Doriado

    Saga can't pull her either: Grace is Special Summoned only from the hand, and she's LIGHT. What Saga does is fill the GY that Grace needs.

    Grace needs 6 or more monsters with different Attributes across both GYs and gains 500 ATK/DEF for each. The Elementsaber line alone leaves four in yours once the turn ends.

    Where She Fits

    She's worth playing wherever three Attributes reach the GY without extra effort.

    Weak Points to Play Around

    • Going first

      Your hand traps don't reach the GY before your first turn. Unless your deck sends several Attributes to the GY as part of its combo, she's a dead card in your opening hand.

    • GY removal before you start

      The lock only starts when she activates. D.D. Crow, Called by the Grave or a Bystial used earlier can drop you below three Attributes. Dimension Shifter and Macro Cosmos stop cards from reaching the GY at all.

    • Floodgates and continuous negation

      Anything already on the field still applies. A Barrier Statue of another Attribute stops her Special Summon, and Skill Drain negates her Deck summon on the field.

    • The gap between her two effects

      If you activate anything else between the revive and the Deck summon, your opponent can respond to it, including with Effect Veiler or Infinite Impermanence on Doriado. When you can, use the Deck summon straight after the revive.

    Verdict

    Elemental Saga Doriado is the most flexible Doriado yet. In any deck that gets three Attributes into its GY, she's a +2 that nobody can respond to, and her Deck summon can end your turn on a floodgate. She doesn't fix Mistress Control's reliance on Ritual pieces or Grace's six-Attribute requirement, but she fills the GY that Grace needs. Play two in Elementsaber and one in Invoked, side her in hand-trap-heavy decks for going second, and leave her out of mono-Attribute decks.
