Zenet

Ritual Monsters that climb back out of the GY, carried by Normal Monsters.

Lore Archive

“When the mortal world and the underworld meet, a journey to decide the king begins!”

Konami's tagline for Zenet (Deck-Build Pack: Glorious Victors product page, translated). Zenet is senet, the ancient Egyptian board game that came to stand for the soul's passage through the afterlife. No Zenet story has been published yet, so the slideshow follows the cards' own artwork.

  1. Two Revelators open the game. Nefertari, named after the queen of Ramesses II, wears jackal ears and holds a jackal-shaped senet piece, while a senet board shines out of the device on her arm.
  2. Amenhotep, the other Revelator, carries a name several pharaohs bore, the most famous being Amenhotep III. His crown is a blue-and-gold cobra hood.
  3. In a temple hall among burning braziers, Nefertari calls up a ghostly dragon, most likely Twin-Headed Thunder Dragon, reprinted in the same pack: Pere-Zenet Em Heru. The name echoes peret em heru, "coming forth by day", the Egyptian title of the Book of the Dead.
  4. Zenet Per Aarkh sets Nefertari between the Sphinx and a close-up of her own smiling face. Per akh seems to mean "house of the glorified soul", a house being a square on the board; the Japanese name is a pledge to return to the light.
  5. Zenet-Nakhte Sphinx is a giant in gold and jade armour with a jackal's head, modelled on Anubis rather than the lion-bodied sphinx of legend. Its 5000 ATK ties for the highest original ATK of any legal monster.
  6. At Zenet Cataract, Amenhotep stands in a crashing torrent with Adjat's serpent coils around him.
  7. Adjat (Tau-Zenet Adjat) comes from Wadjet, the cobra goddess who guarded Lower Egypt: a jade figure in white and gold with a cobra rising over her head, wreathed in coils.
  8. Amduat Zenet shows Nekhbet in a storm of pale feathers. The Amduat is the Egyptian text that follows the sun through the twelve hours of the night.
  9. Nekhbet (Zenet-Mose Nekhbet) comes from Nekhbet, the vulture goddess who guarded Upper Egypt. With Wadjet she was one of the "Two Ladies" who protected the king, the vulture and the cobra on the royal brow.
  10. Zenet Thunder Fall brings the ghostly dragon back, its head breaking out of a storm of green lightning.
  11. In Return Journey to the Light "Zenet", Nefertari and the Sphinx stand on a golden board laid out like a maze, a pyramid shining at the far end. Its Japanese name is 光帰の旅, "the journey back to the light".

Zenet: Overview

  • TCG · Glorious Victors · 3 Dec 2026
  • OCG · Deck-Build Pack: Glorious Victors · 5 Sep 2026
  • 11 cards · 3 Ritual Monsters

Zenet is a Ritual deck that runs on Normal Monsters. Its three Ritual Monsters, all DARK Zombies, can be Ritual Summoned from the hand or the GY, and the material is Normal Monster Cards with different names sent from your hand, Deck or face-up field. Two Level 4 Revelators Set the archetype's Spells and Traps straight from the Deck, two Spells bring out non-Effect Fusion Monsters such as Blue-Eyes Ultimate Dragon as Normal Monster Cards, and three Traps defend the board. The cards are new to the TCG in Glorious Victors on 3 December 2026; the OCG has had them since Deck-Build Pack: Glorious Victors on 5 September 2026.

Discard, Then Rise

Each Ritual Monster discards itself for a search, a send or a recovery. That puts it in the GY, which is exactly where Pere-Zenet Em Heru can Ritual Summon it from.

Normal Monsters as Armour

The Ritual cards equip a Normal Monster from the GY to the monster they summon, and an equipped Zenet Ritual Monster gains protection: immunity, no destruction by effects, or no destruction by battle.

Fusions That Count as Normal

Zenet Per Aarkh and Return Journey summon non-Effect Fusion Monsters from the Extra Deck as Normal Monster Cards. Those count for every Zenet card that looks for Normal Monsters, and they trigger Em Heru's draw.

How the Deck Wins

Going first, Zenet builds one protected Ritual Monster, a big Normal Monster Card beside it and two Set Traps, then plays a slow game from behind them. Going second, the Ritual Monsters' Quick Effects clear the way for attacks.

  1. 1

    Open with any starter

    Zenet Revelator Nefertari, Zenet-Nakhte Sphinx, Zenet Per Aarkh and Return Journey to the Light "Zenet" each reach the others. The usual first goal is Nefertari's on-summon effect, which Sets Pere-Zenet Em Heru and Per Aarkh from the Deck. Nefertari pays its cost by sending itself to the GY, so Effect Veiler or Infinite Impermanence on it does nothing.

  2. 2

    Discard a Ritual Monster, gain a Fusion

    Per Aarkh adds a Ritual Monster; that monster discards itself for its own effect. With Per Aarkh in the GY, the discard lets it banish itself to Special Summon a non-Effect Fusion Monster with ATK no higher than the discarded monster's, treated as a Normal Monster Card: Blue-Eyes Ultimate Dragon for Sphinx, Black Skull Dragon for Tau-Zenet Adjat. Em Heru draws you a card for that summon.

  3. 3

    Ritual Summon from the GY and equip

    Em Heru Ritual Summons the discarded monster back from the GY by sending Normal Monsters from the Deck, then equips one of them to it. Sphinx with an Equip Card is unaffected by your opponent's activated effects.

  4. 4

    Set the Traps

    Return Journey summons Zenet Revelator Amenhotep from the Deck (or you Normal Summon it), and Amenhotep Sets two of Zenet Cataract, Zenet Thunder Fall and Amduat Zenet. Once your Normal Monster Cards total more than 3000 ATK, Cataract and Thunder Fall each have a second use from the GY.

  5. 5

    Interrupt, then attack

    On your opponent's turn, Sphinx flips their monsters face-down, Cataract negates monster effects zone by zone, Thunder Fall negates a Spell or Trap, and Amduat Zenet rebuilds a Ritual Monster if the first one falls. Each Special Summon of a Normal Monster gives Em Heru another chance to draw, once per turn on each turn. Going second, Tau-Zenet Adjat returns blockers to the hand and Return Journey adds a Fusion that can attack directly; Line 4 in the Combos tab reaches 8850 damage that way.

The Ritual Engine

Nothing is Tributed in the usual way. Pere-Zenet Em Heru (once per turn, on your turn) and Amduat Zenet (a Trap, so also on your opponent's turn) send Normal Monster Cards to the GY as the material. Each must have a different name, they can come from your hand, your Deck or your face-up field, and their Levels must add up to at least the Ritual Monster's. The Ritual Monster comes from your hand or your GY, and afterwards you can equip a Normal Monster from your GY to it.

A summoned Fusion counts as a Normal Monster Card only while it is face-up in the Monster Zone, so from the GY it can't be equipped or summoned as a Normal Monster.

Reaching the three Ritual Monsters

Zenet Per Aarkh adds any Zenet monster from the Deck, and Zenet-Nakhte Sphinx's discard adds any other Zenet card, so either one can fetch Tau-Zenet Adjat or Zenet-Mose Nekhbet. Once a Ritual Monster has discarded itself, it is already in the GY for Em Heru or Amduat Zenet. Return Journey can't summon one from the Deck: a Ritual Monster has to be Ritual Summoned first.

Which one to raise depends on the turn. Sphinx is the default going first: 5000 ATK, immune while equipped, and its Quick Effect flips every monster your opponent controls face-down. Adjat is the going-second choice, because its Quick Effect returns up to three of their cards to the hand. Nekhbet is mostly a discard: it brings back a used Zenet card from the GY (a Return Journey, or the Thunder Fall that a GY effect used), and on the field its Quick Effect turns the equipped Normal Monster back into a monster with 2500 more ATK.

Zenet-Nakhte Sphinx

Ritual boss · starter

Zenet-Nakhte Sphinx

Role: the deck's boss and one of its best starters; it searches from the hand, then returns from the GY.

  • Ritual / Effect
  • Level 12
  • DARK Zombie
  • 5000 / 2500

What it does

  1. RitualAny Zenet card can Ritual Summon it.
  2. ProtectionWhile it has an Equip Card, your opponent's activated effects don't affect it.
  3. HandDiscard it to add any other Zenet card from your Deck to your hand.
  4. Quick EffectSend a face-up Normal Monster Card you control to the GY to turn every monster your opponent controls face-down in Defense Position; then you may Special Summon a Normal Monster from your GY.

The search and the Quick Effect are each once per turn.

A starter that comes back as the boss. Its search takes any Zenet card, so it can open a combo by itself: Return Journey from the Deck, or Amenhotep when your Normal Summon is free. With Zenet Per Aarkh already in the GY, the discard also brings out Blue-Eyes Ultimate Dragon.

The Quick Effect's cost can be the Equip Card itself, but then Sphinx loses its immunity; pay with a Fusion on the field if you have one. Flipping face-down stops face-up effects and attacks for the turn, and the Normal Monster you revive can trigger Em Heru's draw on your opponent's turn.

Pere-Zenet Em Heru

Ritual card · draw engine

Pere-Zenet Em Heru

Role: the deck's only on-turn way to Ritual Summon, and a card of draw on each turn.

  • Field Spell

What it does

  1. RitualOnce per turn, Ritual Summon a Zenet Ritual Monster from your hand or GY by sending Normal Monster Cards with different names from your hand, Deck or face-up field to the GY, with Levels adding up to at least its Level. Zenet Per Aarkh

Searcher · Fusion caller

Zenet Per Aarkh

Role: a search now, and a free Fusion body the moment you discard a Ritual Monster.

  • Normal Spell

What it does

  1. SearchAdd a Zenet monster from your Deck to your hand.
  2. GYIf you discard a Zenet Ritual Monster while it is in your GY, banish it to Special Summon a non-Effect Fusion Monster from your Extra Deck with ATK no higher than the discarded monster's. It is treated as a Normal Monster Card and can't attack directly that turn, and you can't activate the effects of monsters other than Zombies for the rest of the turn.

Each effect is once per turn.

The engine's best card. Search a Ritual Monster, discard it for its own effect, and Per Aarkh is already in the GY waiting for exactly that discard. The order matters: activate Per Aarkh's search first, then discard.

The Zombie restriction rarely bites inside the engine, but play non-Zombie extenders and hand traps you need on your own turn before this effect. The Fusion stays a Normal Monster Card while it is face-up in the Monster Zone, so it can be Ritual material, a Sphinx or Adjat cost, and the ATK behind Cataract's and Thunder Fall's GY effects.

Zenet Cataract

Trap · monster negation

Zenet Cataract

Role: negates monster effects zone by zone, then sends a monster to the GY from your GY.

  • Normal Trap

What it does

Role: negates a Spell or Trap, then Sets the next Zenet card from your GY.

  • Counter Trap

What it does

  1. NegateWhen your opponent activates a Spell or Trap Card while you control a Normal Monster or a Zenet Ritual Monster, negate the activation and destroy it.
  2. GYIf the face-up Normal Monster Cards you control total more than 3000 ATK, banish this card from your GY to Set another Zenet Spell or Trap from your Deck or GY.

Each effect is once per turn.

A negate that refills the back row. It answers board breakers like Dark Ruler No More or Evenly Matched, and as a Counter Trap only other Counter Traps can respond to it.

Its GY effect is a toolbox: Set Amduat Zenet for a Ritual Summon next turn (Line 2), a second Pere-Zenet Em Heru if the first one gets negated, or Cataract. Tau-Zenet Adjat can send it from the Deck to the GY just to use this.

How the Traps Protect the Board

A Zenet board is one protected Ritual Monster and the Traps behind it. Each Trap has a job on your opponent's turn and a second use once it is in the GY.

  • Amduat Zenet

    Normal Trap · rebuild

    Amduat Zenet

    On their turn
    Ritual Summons a Zenet Ritual Monster from your hand or GY and equips it. After a board wipe, that puts Sphinx (immune again) or Adjat (with its bounce) back in time to use their Quick Effects.
    From the GY
    Banish it instead of losing a Normal Monster Card to battle or a card effect. That includes the Equip Card, so destroying the equip to strip Sphinx's immunity fails once.
  • Zenet Cataract

    Normal Trap · monster negation

    Zenet Cataract

    On their turn
    Covers one opposing Main Monster Zone for each Normal Monster and Zenet Ritual Monster you control: the first monster effect activated there is negated. It doesn't target, and Konami's notes say it even stops monsters that are unaffected by activated effects.
    From the GY
    With more than 3000 ATK of Normal Monster Cards on your field, banish it to target one of their monsters and send it to the GY. It doesn't destroy, so protection from destruction doesn't save that monster.
  • Zenet Thunder Fall

    Counter Trap · Spell/Trap negation

    Zenet Thunder Fall

    On their turn
    Negates and destroys a Spell or Trap Card your opponent activates while you control a Normal Monster or a Zenet Ritual Monster: Dark Ruler No More, Evenly Matched, Harpie's Feather Duster.
    From the GY
    With more than 3000 ATK of Normal Monster Cards, banish it to Set another Zenet Spell or Trap from the Deck or GY: Amduat Zenet, Cataract, or a fresh Em Heru.
  • Zenet-Nakhte Sphinx

    The monsters behind them

    Sphinx, Adjat, Nekhbet and Nefertari

    Quick Effects
    Sphinx flips their monsters face-down, Tau-Zenet Adjat returns up to three cards to the hand, and Zenet-Mose Nekhbet trades places with its Equip Card. Each Ritual Monster's protection only works while it has an Equip Card.
    From the GY
    Zenet Revelator Nefertari banishes itself during their turn to equip a new Normal Monster to your Ritual Monster, putting that protection back.

The 3000-ATK line: both GY effects add up the ATK of the face-up Normal Monster Cards you control, Equip Cards included. Blue-Eyes Ultimate Dragon or Black Skull Dragon clears it alone; a Ritual Monster doesn't count, because it isn't a Normal Monster Card.

Weaknesses

The engine is consistent and hard to stop with monster negation, but the board it builds is only as strong as its Equip Card and its GY.

  • GY banishment

    Every Ritual Monster comes back from the GY, and Per Aarkh's Fusion needs Per Aarkh in the GY when the discard happens. Banishing either one (D.D. Crow, a Bystial on a DARK Ritual Monster) cuts the line, and Ghost Belle & Haunted Mansion stops Nekhbet's recovery from the GY.

  • Protection lives on the Equip Card

    Sphinx is immune only while it has an Equip Card. Spell/Trap removal such as Harpie's Feather Duster, or a chain to Sphinx's own Quick Effect after it sent its Equip Card, leaves it open; Nefertari's GY effect and Amduat Zenet are the only fixes.

  • Ash Blossom and Droll & Lock Bird

    Per Aarkh's search, Sphinx's search, Em Heru's Ritual Summon and Return Journey's summon from the Deck are all Ash targets, and Droll & Lock Bird after the first search stops the second. Ash on Em Heru is the worst: it is your only Ritual Summon on your own turn, because a Trap can't be activated the turn it is Set.

  • Normal Monsters in the opening hand

    The deck needs Normal Monsters in the Deck, and drawing them is dead weight. Return Journey turns a Normal Monster in hand into a draw, and Em Heru and Amduat can still use it as material, but a hand of two Normal Monsters and no starter does nothing.

  • Per Aarkh's Zombie-only turn

    After its GY effect, you can't activate non-Zombie monster effects for the rest of your turn. Non-Zombie extenders, and hand traps against your opponent's own interruptions, have to come before it.

  • Gaps in the interruption

    Cataract covers only Main Monster Zones, so a monster in the Extra Monster Zone gets through, and it counts only the monsters in your Monster Zone. Thunder Fall stops Spell and Trap Cards, not monster effects. A deck with several monster effects can play through one Sphinx flip and one Cataract.
