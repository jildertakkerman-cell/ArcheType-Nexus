An introduction into the Modern Blue-Eyes Archetype

Lore Archive

"The Blue-Eyes White Dragon is more than a monster—it is a force of nature, a being of pure destructive light whose very name echoes through the annals of dueling history. With 3000 ATK that has defined power standards since the game's inception, the Blue-Eyes archetype channels the pride and fury of Seto Kaiba himself. Their alternative forms, fusions, and rituals are all expressions of one singular truth: supreme power demands supreme respect."

Seto Kaiba's Blue-Eyes White Dragon and its many forms, from Sugoroku's stolen card to the Tablet of Lost Memories. Follow a marked name to walk the trail.

  1. Kaiba's dragon, and in the manga the ka of Kisara. Only four copies existed: Kaiba gathered three and tore up the fourth, which had been Sugoroku's. It was the first monster Special Summoned in the manga, and its 3000 ATK became the standard for every rival's ace, from Armed Dragon LV10 to Red Dragon Archfiend and Galaxy-Eyes Photon Dragon.

  2. Sugoroku's Blue-Eyes. Its name recalls manga chapter 9, where he refuses to sell it to Kaiba, and the Heart of the Cards. In chapter 10 the stolen card refused to attack for Kaiba and destroyed itself; the art is the panel where it returns on Dark Yugi's side. It works once per Duel, as Sugoroku had only one copy.

  3. Three Blue-Eyes in one, from the title page of manga chapter 104. Its Level, ATK and DEF are the three dragons' totals halved. It was the first Level 12 in the anime, and inspired Cyber End Dragon and Thunder Dragon Titan.

  4. Pegasus's Toon, from the manga chapter 109 panel where it takes form. In the anime Kaiba saw it as a disgrace to his dragon.

  5. Kaiba's card for the film Pyramid of Light, where its eyes were drawn yellow. It needs Blue-Eyes Ultimate Dragon to summon, but reached the TCG in August 2004, long before Ultimate Dragon did: for over a year it was legal and impossible to play.

  6. Kaiba's Blue-Eyes jet from the anime, as a dragon.

  7. In the anime Kaiba fused The Fang of Critias with Tyrant Wing into Tyrant Burst Dragon, then fused that with Blue-Eyes; only the English dub gave the result a name. Its card came 18 years later.

  8. Kaiba never used it: in Bonds Beyond Time, Paradox stole his Blue-Eyes and made it this. The first DARK Blue-Eyes, and the first Malefic card in the TCG.

  9. Kaiba's new dragon in The Dark Side of Dimensions, where he faced Aigami at the Shrine of the Millennium Stone in Kul Elna. Its Red-Eyes counterpart is Red-Eyes Alternative Black Dragon.

  10. It looks like three Alternatives fused, though in the film Kaiba made it from three ordinary Blue-Eyes.

  11. Named after Obelisk's attack, "Soul Energy MAX", and with Obelisk's 4000 ATK. Its rival is Palladium Oracle Mahad, also from the film, with swapped Attributes and a mirrored pose.

  12. Named after Solid Vision, KaibaCorp's holograms: its wings are a Duel Disk, and there is a projector in its mouth.

  13. A three-headed Synchro Ultimate Dragon, mirroring the original's pose. With its effect its ATK reaches 4500, the same as Blue-Eyes Ultimate Dragon.

  14. The newest Blue-Eyes, a cover card of the upcoming Magnificent Maestros. "White Phantom Beast" is the name of its ka on the Tablet of Lost Memories, from manga chapter 146; Dark Magician the Magician of Black Magic is named the same way. Destroying every opposing monster recalls Burst Stream of Destruction.

The modern "Blue-Eyes" archetype is a complex, resource-driven engine that channels the spirit of its legendary dragon not just as a mighty attacker, but as a catalyst for a web of powerful support cards. The strategy's primary objective is to seize control of the board through a storm of high-ATK monsters, targeted removal, and potent negation effects. This is all made possible by a core of Main Deck cards designed to search, summon, and endlessly recur its key pieces, transforming the graveyard from a place of defeat into an engine of overwhelming advantage.

Pillars of the Blue-Eyes Strategy

The Legendary Dragons: The Heart of the Deck

Blue-Eyes White Dragon

The central resource. Its presence in hand, Deck, or GY is the prerequisite for the archetype's most powerful effects, making it an essential enabler rather than a primary attacker.

Blue-Eyes Alternative White Dragon

A classic extender. Special Summoned by revealing a BEWD, it provides a 3000 ATK body and monster removal. Note: While iconic, this card is often considered too slow for modern competitive builds.

Blue-Eyes Jet Dragon

The ultimate protector. It shields your field from destruction and resurrects itself from the GY, forming a resilient defensive loop that is difficult to break.

The Eyes of Blue Conduits: Starters and Extenders

Sage with Eyes of Blue

The premier one-card starter. Its primary and most powerful function is to search Maiden of White, initiating the deck's main 'True Light' combo.

Neo Kaiser Sea Horse

The definitive modern setup tool from Structure Deck: White Destiny. A Level 4 LIGHT Dragon Tuner that inherently Special Summons itself, modulates levels, and mills any 'Blue-Eyes' card when sent to the GY, completely outclassing older normal-summon reliant engines.

Maiden of White

The core combo enabler. Its 'Ash Blossom'-immune effect sends itself from hand to GY to place True Light from the Deck. It revives itself from the GY when BEWD is summoned, providing a Level 1 Tuner for Synchro plays.

The White Stone of Ancients

A secondary resource generator. Its End Phase effect is now too slow for the main combo. It serves as a follow-up search target or discard fodder for other card effects.

The White Stone of Legend

Provides card advantage by adding BEWD to hand. Its utility is secondary in a strategy now focused on *sending* BEWD to the GY with cards like Mausoleum of White or Dictator of D.

Dictator of D.

A powerful starter that mitigates "bricking". It sends BEWD from the Deck to GY to summon itself and can revive another "Blue-Eyes" from the GY.

The Arcane Arsenal: Key Spells & Traps

The Melody of Awakening Dragon

A powerful search Spell. Discard one card to add two high-ATK Dragons to your hand. Note: While strong, its discard cost is often less favorable than other consistency tools in modern builds.

True Light

A high-impact Continuous Trap that revives BEWD from the GY or sets a "Blue-Eyes" Spell/Trap from the Deck. Now a core, searchable combo piece via Maiden of White. A double-edged sword, as its destruction wipes your board.

Roar of the Blue-Eyed Dragons

A vital continuous spell. Its incredibly potent Graveyard effect allows for an immediate Fusion Summon by banishing itself, effortlessly bringing out massive boss monsters without traditional Fusion spells.

Wishes for Eyes of Blue

A massive consistency boost. Searches a Level 1 Tuner and a "Blue-Eyes" Spell/Trap. Its GY effect is the key to summoning Blue-Eyes Tyrant Dragon or cheating Extra Deck monsters into the Spell/Trap zone.

Majesty of the White Dragons

Empowers Ritual plays. Its Graveyard effect enables a swift Ritual Summon by banishing itself, entirely bypassing the need to draw dedicated Ritual Spells like Chaos Form.

Mausoleum of White

This Field Spell provides an additional Normal Summon for a Level 1 Tuner and can send a BEWD from Deck to GY, extending combos and setting up resources.

The Command Center: Mastering the Extra Deck

Control & Consistency Monsters

Spirit with Eyes of Blue (Link-1)

The new cornerstone. It transforms any low-level starter into a search for Mausoleum of White and a revive for a mighty "Blue-Eyes" monster from the GY.

Blue-Eyes Spirit Dragon (Synchro)

The primary piece of interaction. It provides GY negation, acts as a floodgate against mass summons, and can "tag out" into other powerful Synchro monsters.

Tag-Out Target: Azure-Eyes Silver Dragon

The main defensive option. Protects your Dragons from being targeted or destroyed for a turn and revives a Normal Monster in the next Standby Phase.

Hieratic Seal of the Heavenly Spheres (Link-2)

A key piece of non-destruction removal. Tributes a monster to bounce an opponent's card, then summons a Dragon from the Deck to replace itself.

Board Breakers & Game Finishers

Blue-Eyes Tyrant Dragon (Fusion)

A devastating board breaker. Immune to Traps and can attack all opposing monsters once each. Summoned efficiently via the GY effect of Wishes for Eyes of Blue.

Rank 8 Xyz Toolbox

The deck's ability to easily field multiple Level 8s gives access to powerful problem-solvers like Number 38 for Spell negation or Dingirsu for non-targeting removal.

Neo Blue-Eyes Ultimate Dragon (Fusion)

While a powerful OTK tool, its main modern role is to be sent to the GY to enable Tyrant Dragon's summon and provide targeting protection for your other "Blue-Eyes" monsters.

Blueprint for Victory: Core Combos

Deep Dive: The Chronicle Sorceress Paradox

Chronicle Sorceress represents a fascinating case study in card design and typological tension within the Blue-Eyes ecosystem. Designed as a bridge support card for both Dark Magician and Blue-Eyes, it offers a highly specific "Foolish Burial" effect capable of depositing essential combo pieces—including newly released Spells and Traps—directly from the deck to the Graveyard.

However, theoretical utility does not always translate into competitive viability. Through rigorous morphological and mathematical analysis, we observe that her impact drastically shifts depending on the format being played—acting as a liability in the physical card game, yet standing as a pillar of power in digital formats.

Competitive Obsolescence & Typological Dissonance

In the unrestricted Advanced Format, Chronicle Sorceress currently sees a 0% adoption rate in top-cut decklists. This is due to severe design friction:

  • The Pre-Condition Paradox: Sorceress requires a pre-existing LIGHT monster in the Graveyard to activate its Blue-Eyes effect. It cannot independently initiate a combo loop on an empty board, requiring fragile 3-card combinations just to function.
  • The Normal Summon Bottleneck: It directly competes with superior, immediate +1 advantage starters like Sage with Eyes of Blue. Dedicating your only Normal Summon to a Level 4 DARK Spellcaster leaves you with a body that cannot be used for the deck's primary Level 9 Synchro or Rank 8 Xyz plays.
  • Explicit Replacement: The release of Neo Kaiser Sea Horse in Structure Deck: Blue-Eyes White Destiny rendered Sorceress entirely obsolete. Sea Horse offers a strictly superior LIGHT Dragon alternative with inherent Special Summon capabilities that mill cards naturally without consuming the Normal Summon.

Format-Defining Staple via Skill Integration

While statistically unviable in the physical TCG, Chronicle Sorceress undergoes a radical inversion when subjected to the artificial ruleset of Yu-Gi-Oh! Duel Links, specifically via the "Battle Chronicle" Skill.

  • Skill Ignition: In Duel Links, Normal Summoning the Sorceress does not represent a tempo loss; it acts as the explicit ignition key for the Battle Chronicle skill, instantly searching disruptive Quick-Play Spells like Successor Soul or Ultimate Fusion.
  • Free Setup: The skill automatically dumps a vanilla Blue-Eyes into the Graveyard to fulfill conditions. This inadvertently and perfectly satisfies the setup requirement for Sorceress's LIGHT prerequisite, bypassing the TCG paradox entirely.
  • The Advantage Loop: In this isolated digital environment, the typological dissonance is masked by the sheer brute force of the digital rule alteration, transforming the card into a hyper-consistent advantage engine that centralizes the strategy.

Variant Spotlight: The Primite Control Build

This powerful variant shifts the deck's strategic focus from explosive combo plays to a more methodical, control-oriented game plan. By integrating the "Primite" engine, the deck aims to out-resource the opponent over several turns with recursive threats and layered trap-based interruptions.

This build is less focused on a single, overwhelming Turn 1 board and more on winning a longer, interactive duel. Cards like Primite Dragon Ether Beryl and the powerful trap Primite Drillbeam provide additional consistency, resource loops, and potent disruptions that complement the core Blue-Eyes strategy. Because these cards have inherent Special Summon conditions, they completely avoid the Normal Summon bottlenecks that plague older support cards like Chronicle Sorceress.

Strategic Analysis

Strategic Weaknesses & Choke Points

  • Hand Trap Vulnerability: Ash Blossom on key searchers (though not Maiden of White!) or Infinite Impermanence on Sage can be devastating.
  • Graveyard Disruption: Cards like Called by the Grave or opposing Bystials can banish crucial resources.
  • Dragon Lock Management: Spirit with Eyes of Blue locks you into Dragons. Ensure you summon Spellcasters like Maiden of White before using Spirit's tribute effect.

External Synergies

  • The Bystial Alliance: A natural partnership. Bystials provide free extenders for your plays and act as hand traps against LIGHT/DARK strategies, patching a key weakness.
  • The Primite Engine: As explored above, self-contained advantage loops like Primite Dragon Ether Beryl seamlessly overlay onto the native Blue-Eyes framework without demanding a Normal Summon.

Magnificent Maestros: Kaiba's New Dragons

  • TCG · Magnificent Maestros · 12 Nov 2026
  • OCG · LIMIT OVER COLLECTION -THE RIVALS- · 20 Mar 2026

Three retrains from Japan's LIMIT OVER COLLECTION -THE RIVALS- reach the TCG in Magnificent Maestros. They fix three old problems. The deck had too few starters. Going second, it had little to do against a full board. And it needed a pile of Tuners to reach its Level 12 boss.

A Starter That Comes Back

Deep-Eyes discards itself for Sage, Maiden or any other Level 1 LIGHT Tuner. Then it waits in the GY for the next Blue-Eyes White Dragon and returns as a Level 10 body.

A Wipe Hiding in the Deck

White Phantom Beast clears your opponent's monsters whenever it's Special Summoned from the hand or Deck. Cards that already summon or add "Blue-Eyes" monsters from the Deck now double as board breakers.

A Spellcaster Becomes a Dragon

The Guiding Flute needs only a Spellcaster on the field. Sage, Maiden and Dictator of D. are all Spellcasters, and the deck has Level 5 or higher Dragons to call from the Deck.

The New Cards

Each effect in plain words, then what it means for a Blue-Eyes deck. Every effect listed is once per turn unless it says otherwise.

Deep-Eyes White Dragon, the Blue Abyss

The starter

Deep-Eyes White Dragon, the Blue Abyss

Level 10 · LIGHT · Dragon · 0 / 0 · always a "Blue-Eyes" card

  1. Discard it: add 1 Level 1 LIGHT Tuner from your Deck to your hand.
  2. When a "Blue-Eyes" Ritual Monster or Blue-Eyes White Dragon is sent to your GY: target 1 of them. Special Summon Deep-Eyes from your GY (if it was already there) or your hand. Its ATK becomes that monster's, and it's banished when it leaves the field.

In this deck

Its search reaches Sage with Eyes of Blue, Maiden of White, both White Stones, Kaibaman the Legend and Effect Veiler. Its revival goes off whenever the deck already sends Blue-Eyes White Dragon to the GY. That includes Dictator of D.'s cost, Mausoleum of White, Neo Kaiser Sea Horse, Dragon Shrine, Primite Dragon Ether Beryl, and the dragon used as Synchro or Link Material. Off a Blue-Eyes Chaos MAX Dragon it comes back with 4000 ATK.

The big change is the Level. Deep-Eyes is a non-Tuner "Blue-Eyes" monster, so Deep-Eyes and two Level 1 Tuners make exactly Blue-Eyes Ultimate Spirit Dragon (Level 12). Its text also mentions Blue-Eyes White Dragon, so it can pay Dictator of D.'s discard cost.

Blue-Eyes White Dragon, the White Phantom Beast

The board breaker

Blue-Eyes White Dragon, the White Phantom Beast

Level 8 · LIGHT · Dragon · 3000 / 2500

  1. When it's added from the Deck to your hand, or a monster you control is destroyed by battle: reveal it and Special Summon it.
  2. When it's Special Summoned from the hand or Deck: destroy every monster your opponent controls. For the rest of the turn, only "Blue-Eyes" monsters can attack directly.
  3. When a card or effect that targets it on the field is activated: discard 1 card to negate that effect. No once-per-turn limit.

In this deck

Anything that summons a "Blue-Eyes" monster from the Deck now wipes the board. That covers Sage with Eyes of Blue's discard effect and The White Stone of Ancients in the End Phase. It covers Hieratic Seal of the Heavenly Spheres on your opponent's turn, and Roar of the Blue-Eyed Dragons once you control Blue-Eyes White Dragon. The Guiding Flute works too, and so does Spirit with Eyes of Blue, because summoning it from the hand counts. Cards that add it from the Deck work if the add is the last thing that happens: The Melody of Awakening Dragon (it has 3000 ATK and 2500 DEF), Kaibaman the Legend's GY effect, and Bystial Magnamhut in the End Phase.

It's a "Blue-Eyes" monster, but it isn't Blue-Eyes White Dragon, and its text doesn't mention that card. So Maiden of White, True Light, Dictator of D., Neo Kaiser Sea Horse and Deep-Eyes don't count it.

Its name also makes it a "Phantom Beast" card, like every Mecha Phantom Beast. Nothing in a Blue-Eyes deck reads that. What it means for the Phantom Beasts is on the Mecha Phantom Beast page.

The Guiding Flute of Summoning Dragons

The bridge

The Guiding Flute of Summoning Dragons

Normal Spell

  1. If a Spellcaster is on the field (either side): Special Summon 1 Level 5 or higher Dragon from your Deck. Then, if your opponent controls 2 or more monsters, you can Special Summon 1 monster with the same name from your Deck. For the rest of the turn you can only Special Summon Dragon or Spellcaster monsters.
  2. During your Main Phase 2, if it was sent to the GY this turn: banish it and draw 1 card.

In this deck

Going first, call Deep-Eyes, a Level 10 Dragon, for Ultimate Spirit Dragon. White Phantom Beast's wipe would have nothing to hit. Going second, call White Phantom Beast and clear their monsters. Into 2 or more monsters it can call two Blue-Eyes White Dragon instead, and either one brings Maiden of White back from the GY. Both locks in the deck fit it. The Flute allows Dragons and Spellcasters, and Roar allows only Dragons from the Extra Deck.

Nothing in the Blue-Eyes engine searches it. It doesn't list Blue-Eyes White Dragon, so Bingo Machine, Go!!!, Wishes for Eyes of Blue and True Light can't find it.

Rulings That Matter

From Konami's official FAQ for the OCG release, which the TCG follows unless it publishes otherwise.

White Phantom Beast: only straight after the add

It can summon itself only right after it's added from the Deck. If an effect adds it at Chain Link 2 or later, or anything else happens after the add, the moment is gone. A normal draw in your Draw Phase counts. The five cards you start the Duel with don't.

The engine our combo checker runs is more lenient here, so none of our lines rely on this effect.

The attack limit is on you

Once the wipe resolves, only "Blue-Eyes" monsters can attack directly for the rest of the turn, even if nothing was destroyed. It applies to you as a player, so monsters that are unaffected by effects can't get around it. Direct attacks you made earlier that turn still count.

The negate has no limit

Discard 1 card for each effect that targets White Phantom Beast. You can do it several times in one chain, but not in the Damage Step. Chain it to Infinite Impermanence or Effect Veiler, and the wipe goes through.

Deep-Eyes has to be in the GY first

To come back from the GY, Deep-Eyes has to be there before the dragon arrives. If both go to the GY at the same time, it can't. That includes using both as material for the same summon. From the hand it always works. It can't be used in the Damage Step, so a Blue-Eyes White Dragon destroyed by battle doesn't count.

Alternative White Dragon counts

Blue-Eyes Alternative White Dragon is treated as Blue-Eyes White Dragon in the GY, so sending it there also brings Deep-Eyes back. Deep-Eyes counts as a "Blue-Eyes" card by rule, not by an effect, so it still counts while its effects are negated.

One self-summon per chain

Deep-Eyes, White Phantom Beast and Blue-Eyes Jet Dragon each have a trigger that Special Summons the card itself from the hand (or GY). Konami lets each player activate only one of this kind per chain, even if several have their condition met.

The Flute reads the name on the field

The second summon copies the first monster's name as it is on the field. Albion the Shrouded Dragon is "Fallen of Albaz" there, so the second monster can be Fallen of Albaz. The two summons don't happen at the same time.

The Flute's lock always applies

After the Flute resolves, you can only Special Summon Dragons and Spellcasters for the rest of the turn, even if its summon failed. Only negating the Flute avoids it. It doesn't stop summons you made before the Flute.

No Main Phase 2 on turn one

The player who goes first has no Battle Phase on the first turn, so there's no Main Phase 2 and no Flute draw. The draw only works when you go second, or in later turns.

Wipes on Your Opponent's Turn

White Phantom Beast's wipe has no turn restriction. These plays clear your opponent's monsters after they've committed to them.

Hieratic Seal of the Heavenly Spheres

Once per your opponent's turn, from the Extra Monster Zone, Tribute the Seal itself to return a face-up card to the hand. Because it was Tributed, it then Special Summons a Dragon from your hand or Deck at 0 ATK/DEF. Pick White Phantom Beast from the Deck: at 0 ATK it still destroys all their monsters.

Losing a battle

If your opponent destroys one of your monsters by battle, White Phantom Beast in your hand can summon itself, even in the Damage Step. It came from the hand, so the wipe follows in the middle of their Battle Phase.

Bystial Magnamhut (Limited)

Going second, Magnamhut summons itself on your opponent's turn by banishing a LIGHT or DARK monster from either GY. In that End Phase it adds a Dragon from your Deck: White Phantom Beast summons itself and wipes before your turn. Their own End Phase triggers go on the chain first, though. If Magnamhut ends up at Chain Link 2 or later, the add comes too late.

The White Stone of Ancients

If it goes to the GY during your opponent's turn, destroyed in battle or by their effect, its End Phase effect Special Summons a "Blue-Eyes" monster from your Deck. White Phantom Beast arrives and wipes their End Phase board.

Mulcharmy Fuwalos and Purulia

With no cards on your field, Fuwalos draws a card each time your opponent Special Summons from the Deck or Extra Deck. Purulia draws each time they Normal or Special Summon from the hand. A drawn White Phantom Beast can summon itself only if that draw was the last thing to happen. A draw in the middle of a longer chain is too late. If their new monster's own summon effect goes on the chain first, you can still chain the summon to it (Konami FAQ).

Deep-Eyes as a follow-up

A Deep-Eyes left in the GY, as in Line 3, comes back on either player's turn when a Blue-Eyes White Dragon is sent there by an effect, such as one your opponent uses to destroy it. It can't be battle destruction, since that happens in the Damage Step.

Deck Building Notes

These counts are our recommendation for a pure Blue-Eyes deck, not a tournament statistic.

Deep-Eyes ×3

Every copy is a starter. A second copy still searches; you just get one revival per turn.

White Phantom Beast ×1–2

One is enough if you only fetch it from the Deck with Sage, Hieratic Seal or the Flute. Play two if you lean on The Melody of Awakening Dragon or Kaibaman the Legend. From the opening hand it needs Spirit with Eyes of Blue's Tribute, a battle, or a discard cost.

The Guiding Flute ×1

Its best case is going second, with a wipe and a draw. Going first it's another way to reach Deep-Eyes, but nothing searches it. Count your Spellcasters: Sage, Maiden, Dictator of D. and Effect Veiler all turn it on.

Weak Points to Play Around

Ash Blossom & Joyous Spring

It hits Deep-Eyes's search, Sage's search and its summon from the Deck, The Guiding Flute, Roar, and Spirit's Mausoleum search. It can't touch Deep-Eyes's revival, White Phantom Beast's self-summon or its wipe, Maiden placing True Light, or True Light Setting a card.

Droll & Lock Bird (Limited)

After your first search, nothing else comes from the Deck to the hand that turn, and that includes White Phantom Beast off Melody or Kaibaman. Deck summons still work, so Sage's discard effect and the Flute are your way around it.

GY banishing

Bystial Druiswurm, Bystial Magnamhut or D.D. Crow on Deep-Eyes's target only costs it the copied ATK. Banishing the Blue-Eyes White Dragon that Spirit or True Light wanted to revive hurts more. Called by the Grave, listed in the classic tab, is Forbidden in the TCG now.

Targeted negation

Infinite Impermanence or Effect Veiler on White Phantom Beast loses to its discard negate, so keep a card in hand. On Sage or Spirit with Eyes of Blue they still stop the line, because neither can protect itself.

Answers that don't target

The discard negate only stops effects that target White Phantom Beast. Forbidden Droplet, Dark Ruler No More and Super Polymerization don't target, so they get past it. While Super Starslayer TY-PHON - Sky Crisis is on the field, monsters with 3000 or more ATK can't activate effects at all. A White Phantom Beast at 3000 can't negate there, and it can't wipe.

The Flute Outside Blue-Eyes

Branded. Blazing Cartesia, the Virtuous and Guiding Quem, the Virtuous are Level 4 LIGHT Spellcasters, so either one turns on the Flute. It calls Albion the Shrouded Dragon (Level 8) from the Deck. Into 2 or more monsters, Albion is "Fallen of Albaz" on the field, so the second summon can be Fallen of Albaz. After that, the lock allows only Dragons and Spellcasters. Lubellion the Searing Dragon (Dragon) and Granguignol the Dusk Dragon (Spellcaster) fit. Mirrorjade the Iceblade Dragon is a Wyrm, so it doesn't. More on the engine: Branded and Fallen of Albaz.
