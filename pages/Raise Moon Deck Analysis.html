Raise Moon

Six Level 7s that summon themselves when you draw them.

Lore Archive

“Adorning the sky are the moon and the stars.”

Konami's tagline for Raise Moon (Deck-Build Pack: Glorious Victors product page, translated). No Raise Moon story has been published yet, so the slideshow follows the cards' own artwork through one night, from the New Moon to the Full Moon. In Chinese the theme is called 盈彩月夜, "Colorful Waxing Moonlit Night".

  1. Under a full moon, searchlights sweep the sky over a gold-lit palace with Raise Moon's name above its doors: Raise Moon - The City that Never Sleeps. The building is modelled on Caesars Palace on the Las Vegas Strip, and "the city that never sleeps" is one of Las Vegas's nicknames.
  2. The house belongs to Cielo, the Veil of Raise Moon. Her Japanese title, 帳 (tobari), is the curtain that night draws over the sky, and a golden crescent rises behind her as coins rain down.
  3. Four more work its floor. Carol is its Elegance (雅, miyabi): a DARK Zombie in a high-collared cape that spreads into bat wings, with blue bat-shaped spirits around her.
  4. Lapi is its Wings (翼, tsubasa), drawn from the moon rabbit, the rabbit people see in the Moon's dark markings. Rabbits stand for good fortune in Japan, which is what her gold clothes and heels and the coins around her show.
  5. Sharna is its Blade (刃, yaiba): a WIND Warrior in a white shirt and black waistcoat, a slim knife in one hand, golden coins drifting past.
  6. Kulshe is its Beacon (灯, akari, a lamp's light): a FIRE Dragon with horns, wings, a red scaled tail and armoured fists, leaping out of the frame with a grin.
  7. And there is Squeeze, the New Moon: a small crowned turtle with slot-machine reels in its shell, turning in a ring of coins. Its title, 朔, is the new moon, read here as hajime, "the beginning".
  8. Away from the tables, all five crowd into a lounge: Carol with a drink and a book, Kulshe sprawled in the middle with Lapi's paw on her head, Cielo and Sharna behind them. Stars of Raise Moon lets you pick the card that goes on top of your Deck, the way the house is always said to rig the game.
  9. Later in the night the Veil becomes the Night Sky (天, read yozora): Cielo sets the roulette wheel spinning and calls "No More Bets!", the dealer's signal that betting is closed before the ball lands.
  10. Out on the floor, two guests cheer as the 7s come up and Lapi cheers with them: The Bright Lights of Raise Moon (煌めき, "glitter"). The machine spitting out coins is Squeeze, now at its full moon.
  11. At the full moon (望, read nozomi, "hope"), Squeeze is a giant golden machine-turtle: 7s on its reels, JACKPOT in lights, coins heaped at its feet. Its design follows Grandopolis, The Eternal Golden City, a gambling-themed turtle from the 2013 World Championship prize cards.

Raise Moon at a Glance

TCG: Glorious Victors · 3 Dec 2026 OCG: Deck-Build Pack: Glorious Victors · 5 Sep 2026 11 cards: 6 Main Deck monsters, 2 Xyz, 2 Spells, 1 Trap

Raise Moon is new to the TCG in Glorious Victors, out on 3 December 2026. The OCG got it three months earlier in Deck-Build Pack: Glorious Victors. Every Main Deck Raise Moon monster is a Level 7, each with a different Attribute and Type, and five of the six Special Summon themselves when you draw them. The deck never searches: its cards stop you adding cards from the Deck to your hand except by drawing. Instead, Stars of Raise Moon and Raise Moon - The City that Never Sleeps decide which card is on top of the Deck, and Carol, Cielo and the rest draw it.

Two Level 7s on the field make a Rank 7: Cielo, the Night Sky of Raise Moon - No More Bets!, which ignores every other card's effects from the Extra Monster Zone, or Squeeze, the Full Moon of Raise Moon - JACKPOT!, which puts your opponent's cards on the bottom of their Deck. The same set reprints Number 76: Harmonizer Gradielle, Number C6: Chronomaly Chaos Atlandis, Magical Mallet and Sacred Sword of Seven Stars, and all four help a Level 7 deck.

Cielo, the Night Sky of Raise Moon - No More Bets!

No More Bets!: the going-first tower

In the Extra Monster Zone it is unaffected by other cards' effects, it can turn your opponent's search into "draw 1 card", and it detaches a material to draw on either turn.

Squeeze, the Full Moon of Raise Moon - JACKPOT!

JACKPOT!: the going-second payout

When it is Xyz Summoned, it detaches materials to put that many of your opponent's cards on the bottom of their Deck, without targeting. Their monsters can only attack it.

How the Deck Wins

Raise Moon is a midrange deck. It doesn't end turn one on a wall of negates. It makes one Rank 7 that's hard to remove, keeps drawing, and takes cards away from your opponent on both players' turns.

1. A draw is a summon

Cielo, the Veil, Carol, Lapi, Sharna and Kulshe each Special Summon themselves when you draw them, from any draw, including your normal draw in the Draw Phase. Stars of Raise Moon picks the card on top, so the right draw effect becomes a known summon.

2. Two Level 7s, one tower

Going first, two Level 7 Raise Moon monsters become No More Bets! in the Extra Monster Zone. Effects can't touch it there, and it punishes the search that most combos open with by turning it into a blind draw.

3. Bottom of the Deck, not the GY

Sharna, Kulshe and JACKPOT! put cards on the bottom of your opponent's Deck. That gets around destruction protection and GY effects. Sharna and Kulshe target; JACKPOT! doesn't.

4. It plays on their turn

Lapi Xyz Summons a Rank 7 LIGHT monster during your opponent's turn, The Bright Lights of Raise Moon puts a Raise Moon Xyz Monster on the field at Trap speed, and No More Bets! and Cielo, the Veil draw at Quick-Effect speed.

What's luck and what isn't

No Raise Moon card flips a coin or rolls a die. The luck is in the draws. A plain draw from a shuffled Deck is a gamble. A draw after Stars of Raise Moon or the City has put a card on top is not. The combo lines on this page only name a drawn card when one of those two cards put it there, and treat every other draw as a card that does nothing.

Six Level 7s, Six Attributes

All six Main Deck monsters are Level 7, and no two share an Attribute or a Type. That matters twice: any two of them make a Rank 7, and Number 76: Harmonizer Gradielle takes on the Attribute of every monster attached to it.

Where Stars, the City and Bright Lights Fit

Stars of Raise Moon: the stacker

Stars is what turns a draw engine into a combo deck. It puts any other Raise Moon card on top of your Deck. Play Lapi when you want an extra draw and the threat of an Xyz Summon on their turn, Sharna when they have a monster to remove, Kulshe for a Spell/Trap, and Cielo, the Veil when you want a Quick-Effect draw. For the rest of that turn neither player can add cards from the Deck to the hand except by drawing, so going second it also stops your opponent's searching hand traps. From the GY it banishes itself to draw: with a Spellcaster "Raise Moon" Xyz Monster on your field (No More Bets!) only you draw, otherwise both players draw 1.

Raise Moon - The City that Never Sleeps: the safety net

The City is a Continuous Spell, not a Field Spell. Once per turn it either puts a card from your hand on the top or bottom of the Deck and draws 1, or draws 1 if you control a Spellcaster "Raise Moon" Xyz Monster. The first mode is the fix for a hand of Lapi, Sharna or Kulshe: put one on top, draw it, and it summons itself. While it's face-up, each Level 7 Raise Moon monster survives the first time it would be destroyed by a card effect each turn (the Rank 7s have no Level, so this doesn't cover them). In the End Phase it shuffles every Raise Moon card in your GY and face-up banishment back into the Deck, which refills the Deck for the next turn's draws.

The Bright Lights of Raise Moon: a Rank 7 at Trap speed

This Normal Trap Special Summons a Raise Moon Xyz Monster from the Extra Deck with no materials and attaches itself to it, then can shuffle your GY's Raise Moon cards back into the Deck. Set going first, it gives you No More Bets! on your opponent's turn even when the hand had no two Level 7s. It isn't an Xyz Summon, though, so JACKPOT! brought out this way doesn't get its "Xyz Summoned" trigger. In the GY it banishes itself to draw 1. Activating either effect stops you adding cards from the Deck to your hand for the rest of the Duel, except by drawing.

The rule the whole deck shares

Every draw effect in the archetype says you can't add cards from the Deck to your hand except by drawing: for that turn on the monsters, for the whole Duel on Squeeze, the New Moon and The Bright Lights. So a search earlier in the turn switches off the monsters' draw effects for that turn, and a search at any point in the Duel switches off Squeeze and The Bright Lights. That's why the test deck plays draw cards and no searchers.

The Rank 7 Payoffs

Both Raise Moon Xyz Monsters are Rank 7 LIGHT, so they fit inside Carol's and Lapi's lock (LIGHT Xyz only that turn) and Squeeze, the New Moon's (Rank 7 Xyz only). Which one to make depends on who went first.

What it does

  1. Locked inIt can't be used as material for a Fusion, Xyz or Link Summon.
  2. LeavesIf your opponent's card sends it from your field to the GY, you can draw 1.
  3. EMZ onlyWhile in the Extra Monster Zone, it's unaffected by every other card's effects.
  4. EMZ onlyWhen an effect your opponent activated, one that would add a card from the Deck to the hand, resolves, you can make it "Draw 1 card" instead.
  5. EMZ onlyOnce per turn, on either player's turn, it can detach 1 material to draw 1 card.

Role: a removal-proof monster that taxes searching. Put it in the Extra Monster Zone, because in a Main Monster Zone it loses its last three effects. There, only things that aren't card effects get rid of it: a stronger monster in battle, or a summoning procedure that Tributes it, such as a Kaiju or Lava Golem.

Turning a search into a draw doesn't negate the card. Their search still resolves, but as a blind draw, so a combo that needed one specific card usually stalls there. Its detach draw on their turn can hit Sharna or Kulshe, which summon themselves and remove a card.

What it does

  1. AttacksYour opponent's monsters can only choose it as an attack target.
  2. RemovalWhen it's Xyz Summoned, or a monster is Special Summoned from the hand, it can detach any number of materials to put that many cards your opponent controls on the bottom of their Deck, in the order you choose. Once per turn.
  3. MillEach time your opponent's card moves their cards out of their Deck, except by drawing them, it can attach the top card of their Deck to itself.

Role: non-targeting removal that grows its own ammunition. It takes any Level 7s, not only Raise Moon ones. Built with three materials, it removes three cards. The second trigger works on your opponent's turn too: if Cielo's or No More Bets!' draw hits a monster that summons itself, that summon from the hand fires it again on a later turn.

Every search, mill or Deck summon your opponent makes gives it a new material, so it gets stronger against the decks that use their Deck most. Its 1400 ATK is the weak point: their monsters can only attack it, so it soaks battles and loses most of them.

Why it works: the City is the only card that summons a Lapi, Sharna or Kulshe that is already in your hand. Watch for: a Trap chained to Kulshe's targeting effect.

Weaknesses

Raise Moon's strengths come from drawing, and so do most of its problems.
