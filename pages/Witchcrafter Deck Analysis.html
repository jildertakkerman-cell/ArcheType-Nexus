WITCHCRAFTER

The Art of Magical Recursion & Attrition

Lore Archive

"The Witchcrafter atelier is part laboratory, part library, and part battlefield. These magical artisans have organized their practice around the fundamental advantage loop: spells fuel their monsters, and their monsters recycle the spells from the graveyard. Madame Verre alone represents the pinnacle of their craft — a creature who reads your hand and translates it into disruption with the confidence of someone who has done this a thousand times before."

In a magical city of trade near Endymion, the Witchcrafter guild infuses its crafts with magic, and has become the talk of the town (Master Guide 6). Its artworks show the guild at work, and the card trivia shows who turns up where. Follow a marked name to walk the trail.

  1. The guild's street. The emblem of the Spellbooks and a Spell Counter appear in its art, and over its shops hang seven symbols, one for each of the seven main Witchcrafters.

  2. Contrary to her cute looks, Madame Verre runs the guild with incredible magic and nerves of steel, and skilled craftswomen from all over have joined her for her talent, despite her hedonistic personality (Master Guide 6). She appears in Collaboration, Confusion Confession, Distortion, Holiday, Masterpiece and Unveiling.

  3. Edel, Haine and Madame Verre at work on their masterpiece: the construction of Witchcrafter Golem Aruru.

  4. The finished Golem Aruru, with Genni, Haine and Madame Verre.

  5. Genni is Servant of Endymion after moving to the Witchcrafter guild. The Witchcrafter Scroll and two Spell Counters, one on her staff and one on a safe, appear in her art.

  6. Potterie, Schmietta and Pittore with a colorful Terracottan. The art mirrors Unveiling: just as Edel, Haine and Verre built Golem Aruru, the three pupils seem to have made this Terracottan together.

  7. According to the Official Card Game Art Works book, Haine is 20, between 170 and 180 cm tall, and the type to cry easily; her working name was "Witchcraft Raptier". She appears in Celebration, Confusion Confession, Distortion, Draping, Masterpiece, Unveiling and Walpurgis.

  8. The whole guild, Edel, Genni, Haine, Pittore, Potterie and Schmietta, receives a letter from their guild master, Madame Verre, to exasperation and dismay all round: it is the scene where Verre announces her leave of absence.

  9. With Verre gone, Haine was put in charge of the guild (The Valuable Book EX 2), and Vice-Madame is her Fusion form. Her hand trembles: she isn't comfortable as the new head, as her shocked face in Confusion Confession suggests. Edel and Genni appear with her.

  10. Haine, Madame Verre and the disappearing hat of Madame Rilliona: the art seems to show Rilliona turning back into Verre, which suggests the hat is what keeps her adult form.

  11. Madame Rilliona is Verre's true form: the adult she once was as Rilliona, the Magistus of Verre. A stylized portrait of her hangs in the art of Patronus.

  12. Everyone together: Edel, Genni, Haine, Madame Rilliona, Pittore, Potterie and Schmietta.

Executive Summary

The Witchcrafter archetype is a control-oriented Spellcaster strategy defined by its unique and resilient resource loop. The deck excels in a "grind game," aiming to out-resource the opponent over multiple turns rather than achieving a swift one-turn victory. Its core mechanic revolves around the ability of "Witchcrafter" Spell Cards to return from the Graveyard to the hand during the End Phase, providing an endless stream of fuel for the powerful effects of its boss monsters. This creates a gameplay loop of attritional control, where recurring Spells are traded for the opponent's finite resources, eventually leading to an insurmountable advantage.

The Coven's Arsenal

The Coven: Core Monsters

Witchcrafter Schmietta

The most important one-card starter. Tributes to summon a "Master" from the deck and sets up the Graveyard by sending any "Witchcrafter" card from the deck.

Witchcrafter Genni

A powerful extender. Banishes herself from the GY to copy the effect of a "Witchcrafter" Spell in the GY, enabling double searches or revivals.

Witchcrafter Madame Verre

The premier boss monster. Provides a devastating board-wide monster effect negation and can boost her own ATK/DEF to colossal levels.

The Grimoire: Essential Spells

Witchcrafter Creation

The primary monster searcher, adding any "Witchcrafter" monster from deck to hand to ensure you start your plays.

Witchcrafter Holiday

The in-archetype "Monster Reborn," used to revive key boss monsters or reuse the effects of your starters.

Witchcrafter Bystreet

Provides crucial once-per-turn destruction protection for all "Witchcrafter" monsters and can even set itself back on the field from the GY.

The Foundational Formula

The deck's consistency stems from its simple yet powerful one-card combo. A single "Apprentice" like Schmietta can establish a formidable board, allowing you to witness the coven's full power and control the duel from the very first turn.

Competitive Analysis

Strengths

  • Unmatched Grind Game: The recursive spell loop provides unparalleled resource advantage over long duels.
  • Layered Control: A full board combines negation, destruction, and protection, making it very difficult to break.
  • Engine Synergy: Pairs well with powerful external engines like Dogmatika and Invoked due to minimal Extra Deck usage.

Weaknesses

  • Normal Summon Reliant: The entire strategy hinges on the first starter's effect resolving, making it vulnerable to hand traps.
  • Graveyard Hate: Cards like Abyss Dweller or D.D. Crow can shut down the resource loop completely.
  • Slow Pace: The deck is inherently defensive and can struggle to close out games quickly against aggressive strategies.

Key Tech Choices

  • Dogmatika Engine: Provides powerful disruption without using the Normal Summon, covering the deck's main weakness.
  • Invoked Engine: Offers an omni-negate in Invoked Mechaba, which synergizes perfectly with the Spell-heavy hands.
  • Board Breakers: Non-targeting cards like Dark Ruler No More or Forbidden Droplet are essential for disabling established enemy boards.

Magnificent Maestros: Seven New Witchcrafters

  • Magnificent Maestros
  • TCG: 12 Nov 2026
  • OCG: 30 May 2026, Revolution Booster: Toon / Witchcrafter / Unchained

Older Witchcrafter turns spent the Normal Summon on a low-Level starter and paid every effect with a Spell from the hand. The seven new cards change both. Witchcrafter Marka Tributes itself from the hand to add the new Field Spell, Witchcrafter Walpurgis, which searches any Witchcrafter monster and, on your turn, pays your monsters' discard costs from the Deck. Witchcrafter Terracottan and Witchcrafter Celebration are two Fusion Summons inside the archetype, and Witchcrafter Madame Rilliona turns one of them into three more Witchcrafters from the Deck.

No Normal Summon needed

Marka adds Walpurgis, and Walpurgis adds any Witchcrafter. Line 1 below ends on five monsters without ever Normal Summoning.

Costs paid from the Deck

On your turn, Walpurgis sends a Witchcrafter Spell/Trap from the Deck instead of a discard. The Spells it sends still return to your hand in the End Phase.

Answers on their turn

Distortion negates, Pupils applies a Witchcrafter Spell from your hand as a Quick Effect, and Marka comes back from the GY to Tribute a monster when they remove yours.

Rulings in short

Based on the card text and Konami's notes and Q&A on this card, the official rulings in Konami's Japanese card database. The TCG follows them unless it rules otherwise.

  • Not in the Damage Step, and not against an empty field. The revive is a GY trigger, and it can't be activated at all if your opponent controls no monster.
  • Summon and Tribute happen together. Both are part of the same resolution.
  • Only their card effects count. Battle doesn't trigger it, and neither do your own cards: Celebration destroying your own Witchcrafter, or a Witchcrafter you Tribute for a cost, leaves Marka in the GY.
  • Banished when it leaves. Each effect is once per turn, so you can Tribute it for the search on your turn and revive it on theirs.

Based on the card text and Konami's notes and Q&A on this card, the official rulings in Konami's Japanese card database. The TCG follows them unless it rules otherwise.

How the Pieces Fit

1. Costs from the Deck

Marka adds Walpurgis. On your turn, every discard cost of a Witchcrafter monster on your field can be paid with a Witchcrafter Spell/Trap from the Deck, and the Spells come back in your End Phase. The limits: only on your turn, only for monsters you control, and only once Walpurgis's activation has resolved.

2. GY into Fusion

Terracottan fuses from the hand or field and Celebration from the GY or banishment, and Pupils searches Celebration. Neither needs Confusion Confession, and Celebration puts its materials back in the Deck for the deck's summon effects to use again.

3. Rilliona's three

One Fusion Summon of Madame Rilliona becomes three more Witchcrafters from the Deck. Picking low-Level ones with Quick Effects keeps the turn going after she resolves.

4. Their turn

Distortion, Pupils' reveal, Marka in the GY, Haine, Madame Verre and Vice-Madame. Pupils can reveal a Spell even if that Spell's own once-per-turn is already used, so a Spell you activated that turn can still be applied again through Pupils.

Building With the New Cards

Suggested counts

This site's starting point for a pure build, not a tested list.

  • 3 Witchcrafter Marka
  • 2 Witchcrafter Walpurgis
  • 1–2 Witchcrafter Terracottan
  • 2 Witchcrafter Celebration
  • 1 Witchcrafter Distortion
  • 1 Witchcrafter Pupils (Extra Deck)
  • 1 Witchcrafter Madame Rilliona (Extra Deck)
  • 2 Witchcrafter Vice-Madame (Extra Deck)

Keep at least one Witchcrafter Madame Verre in the Deck for Rilliona's material, plus Creation, Schmietta, Haine and Verre, the Maid of Endymion from the older core. Two Walpurgis plus three Marka (and Terraforming, if you run it) is enough ways to reach the Field Spell without drawing several.

One posted list

A Witchcrafter deck posted to Konami's Neuron deck database ran 3 Marka, 3 Walpurgis, 3 Creation, 2 Celebration, 2 Unveiling, 3 Verre Magic - Lacrima of Light, 1 Terracottan and 1 Distortion, with 2 Vice-Madame, 1 Pupils and 1 Madame Rilliona in the Extra Deck.

It is not a TCG-legal list. Neuron itself flags four of its cards as Forbidden in the TCG (Maxx "C", Cross-Sheep, Red-Eyes Dark Dragoon and Charmer Quartet in Bloom), and two more, Witchcrafter Seed and a Silent Magician card, haven't been released in the TCG.

What Changes, and What Still Hurts

Better

  • Two-card boards that leave the Normal Summon free.
  • Discard costs paid from the Deck on your turn, so the hand keeps its Spells for the opponent's turn.
  • Interaction on both turns: Distortion, Pupils' reveal and Marka on top of Haine, Madame Verre and Vice-Madame.

Still a problem

  • Droll & Lock Bird (Limited): both lines search the Deck four times, and after the first search the Deck is closed.
  • Ash Blossom has many targets. Terracottan covers a negated Marka search, and a negated Walpurgis search still leaves Vice-Madame, but not Rilliona.
  • Nibiru, once Rilliona's three summons resolve.
  • Macro Cosmos turns off Walpurgis's replacement, and GY banishing before Terracottan resolves takes its target away.
  • Rilliona's Fusion-only lock, if you still need a Link Monster that turn.

Current TCG Banlist Status

UNRESTRICTED STATUS: The Witchcrafter archetype remains at full power with no cards on the official TCG Forbidden & Limited list.

All "Witchcrafter" monsters, spells, and traps are currently unlimited (allowed at 3 copies per deck). This allows the archetype to be played at its maximum consistency and power level, free from any direct restrictions.
