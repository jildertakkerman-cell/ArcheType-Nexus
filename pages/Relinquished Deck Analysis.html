Lore Archive

"Relinquished is Pegasus's masterwork—a Ritual monster with 0 ATK that absorbs an opponent's monster as an equip, stealing its ATK and using it as a human shield. Millennium-Eyes Restrict evolved this concept into a Fusion that negates hand traps and monster effects. They prove that the most terrifying power is the power to take."

Executive Summary

The Relinquished archetype is a classic control strategy focused on a unique parasitic mechanic: absorbing an opponent's monsters and turning their power against them. This playstyle is executed through a small but potent lineup of strange and powerful entities, primarily Ritual and Fusion monsters known as "Eyes Restrict". The deck excels at non-destructive removal, bypassing many forms of protection, and thrives in a "go-second" environment where it can dismantle established boards.

New for 2026. Nightmare-Eyes Restrict is the first "Eyes Restrict" Fusion that does not name Relinquished as a material. Read the impact report.

The Pantheon of Eyes: Core Monsters

Relinquished

The Ritual foundation. Absorbs one monster to steal its stats. While outdated as a boss, it is the essential search target for Pre-Preparation of Rites, making the entire engine consistent.

Thousand-Eyes Restrict

The proactive board-breaker. After absorbing a monster, it prevents all other monsters from attacking or changing position. A prime target for Instant Fusion.

Millennium-Eyes Restrict

The reactive disruption. A Quick Effect allows it to absorb an opponent's monster when they activate an effect, negating it and all other copies of that monster. The deck's primary interactive piece.

Relinquished Anima

The Link-1 tool. Absorbs any monster it points to. Its main purpose is to enable the "Kaiju Slingshot" combo, turning a Kaiju given to the opponent into your own high-ATK monster.

Competitive Analysis

Strengths

  • Unconventional Removal: Kaiju tributing and non-destructive absorption bypass most monster protection.
  • Potent Disruption: Millennium-Eyes Restrict can shut down key monster effects and hand traps.
  • Go-Second Power: Excels at breaking established boards rather than building its own.

Weaknesses

  • Inconsistency: Relies on drawing specific starter cards or two-card combos to function.
  • Weak Going First: Struggles to establish a meaningful board without external engines like Invoked.
  • Backrow Vulnerability: Has few in-archetype answers to powerful Spells, Traps, and floodgates.

Tech & Engines

  • Kaiju Engine: Essential for consistent board-breaking and enabling Anima.
  • Pre-Preparation of Rites: The deck's best starter, providing a +1 in card advantage.
  • Super Polymerization: Another powerful, un-chainable removal tool against DARK-heavy metas.
Impact Report TCG · Magnificent Monsters · 4 Sep 2026 OCG · Duelist Box: Prismatic Summon · 20 Dec 2025

Nightmare-Eyes Restrict

The first "Eyes Restrict" Fusion that does not name Relinquished as a material. It absorbs without targeting, it cannot be run over in battle, and it drags every attack declaration onto itself. In exchange it asks the deck for one thing the classic lineup has never carried.

The Card, As Printed

Nightmare-Eyes Restrict
Level 1 DARK Illusion Fusion / Effect 0 / 0
What it asks for

Two monsters with different Types, each one a Fiend, Illusion or Spellcaster. No name is listed, so for the first time an "Eyes Restrict" Fusion does not need Relinquished or Thousand-Eyes Idol sitting underneath it.

When it absorbs

On Special Summon, or at the end of a Battle Phase in which it battled. Two separate windows, one hard once per turn between them.

How it absorbs

It equips one monster your opponent controls. The text never uses the word target, so targeting protection does not stop it and there is nothing to redirect.

What it gains

ATK equal to the combined ATK of everything equipped to it by its effect. Nothing in the text caps how many monsters it can hold at once.

Why it survives

If it battles a monster, neither is destroyed by that battle. A 0 ATK body can crash into anything and walk away.

Why they cannot go around it

Your opponent's monsters cannot attack any monster except this one. Everything else you control is covered for as long as it stands.

Card text. 2 monsters with different Types (Fiend, Illusion, or Spellcaster). Gains ATK equal to the combined ATK of the monsters equipped to this card by its effect. If this card battles a monster, neither can be destroyed by that battle. Your opponent's monsters cannot attack any monsters, except this one. If this card is Special Summoned, or at the end of the Battle Phase, if this card battled: You can equip 1 monster your opponent controls to this card as an Equip Spell. You can only use this effect of "Nightmare-Eyes Restrict" once per turn.

The Type Tax

Dropping the named materials is not free. The two bodies you feed it must have different Types, and the classic Relinquished lineup is entirely Spellcaster. Two Spellcasters is not a legal pair, so the deck has to carry an Illusion or a Fiend it never used to run.

What Actually Changes

Removal that cannot be dodged

No targeting, no destruction, no banish. Most modern protection clauses are written against words this card never uses.

A play on their turn

Relinquished Fusion is a Quick-Play Spell. Go first, set it, and the Fusion Summon itself becomes the disruption during their turn. Going second it is live from the hand.

The board gets a bodyguard

The taunt plus the battle clause means your Ritual bodies stop being free attack targets. Clearing it costs a card effect, not a battle.

No cap on the stack

Thousand-Eyes and Millennium-Eyes hold one monster each. This one holds as many as you can feed it, and the ATK adds up.

The build has to change

An all-Spellcaster lineup cannot summon it. Room has to come from somewhere for an Illusion or Fiend body.

One negate undoes all of it

Infinite Impermanence or Effect Veiler switches off the ATK gain and the taunt at once, leaving a 0 ATK monster in the open.

How You Get It Onto the Field

Generic materials open up routes the older Fusions never had, and close off two that players keep reaching for out of habit.

Green light

4 routes
Relinquished Fusion Quick-Play Banishes materials from your hand, field and Graveyard, so the summon costs nothing on board. Set going first, it is the only route that interrupts their turn without giving up a card in hand.
Super Polymerization Quick-Play Fiend, Illusion and Spellcaster cover an enormous slice of the current field. Eat two of their monsters, then equip a third off the summon trigger. Uncounterable on activation.
Instant Fusion Level 1 The summon is treated as a Fusion Summon, so the equip triggers and the card counts as properly summoned for later revival. It dies in the End Phase and the absorbed monster follows it to the Graveyard.
Polymerization Normal Plain and slow, but it works from hand and field and asks for no archetype membership at all.

Blocked

3 traps
Fusion Deployment Illegal It summons a material whose name is specifically listed on the revealed Fusion Monster. This card lists Types, not names, so there is nothing to reveal it for.
Branded Fusion Illegal Restricted to Fusion Monsters that mention Fallen of Albaz as material. Nightmare-Eyes Restrict does not.
Two monsters of the same Type Illegal Relinquished plus Millennium-Eyes Illusionist reads like the obvious pair and is not a legal Fusion. Both are Spellcasters.

Line One: Three Monsters Off Their Board, On Their Turn

This is the payoff line. It needs Relinquished Fusion set, Millennium-Eyes Illusionist and Golden-Eyes Idol in the Graveyard, and one Illusion body available to banish.

1

On your opponent's turn, once they commit a monster worth taking, activate the set Relinquished Fusion.

2

Banish Golden-Eyes Idol and an Illusion body from your Graveyard as material. Spellcaster and Illusion are different Types, so the Fusion Summon is legal.

3

Three effects now want the same chain: the Fusion's own equip trigger, Golden-Eyes Idol paying out for having been banished, and Millennium-Eyes Illusionist returning itself from the Graveyard, which is not optional.

4

Resolve it back down. Millennium-Eyes Illusionist lands in your hand, Golden-Eyes Idol equips one Effect Monster, and Nightmare-Eyes Restrict equips a second without targeting.

5

Discard the recycled Millennium-Eyes Illusionist as a Quick Effect and equip a third, this time as if the Fusion had done it itself.

6

Their board is three monsters lighter, yours has a body sitting on the combined ATK of everything it took, and nothing on your side can be attacked around it.

Watch which clause pays the ATK. Millennium-Eyes Illusionist and the Graveyard effect of Relinquished Fusion both equip as if it were equipped by that monster's effect, which is exactly the wording the ATK clause looks for. Golden-Eyes Idol does not use that phrasing, so it supplies its own ATK boost instead. The total moves either way, but only the first two feed the Fusion's own count.

Line Two: Give It Back After They Answer It

Nightmare-Eyes Restrict has no protection, so the plan is not to keep it alive. The plan is to make removing it cost them a second turn.

1

Your opponent destroys Nightmare-Eyes Restrict. Everything equipped to it loses its host and goes to the Graveyard with it.

2

Illusionist Faceless Magician sees a face-up "Eyes Restrict" Fusion destroyed and Special Summons itself from your hand or Graveyard.

3

On your turn, send it from the field to the Graveyard as Link Material for Relinquished Anima or any generic Link-2.

4

Its second effect targets Nightmare-Eyes Restrict in your Graveyard and Special Summons it back, which fires the equip trigger all over again.

Destruction only. Illusionist Faceless Magician floats off a Fusion being destroyed by battle or card effect. Banishing, bouncing and sending to the Graveyard all leave you with nothing, which is exactly how the decks that beat this one already remove things.

Rulings You Will Be Asked About

Question Answer
Where do the absorbed monsters go when it leaves the field? They lose their host and are sent to the Graveyard by game rules. They are not destroyed, so anything that only cares about destruction stays quiet, but Graveyard effects on those monsters are live.
Does targeting protection stop the absorb? No. The effect never says target, so cards that only answer targeting have nothing to answer.
Is it a member of the archetype? Yes. Relinquished Fusion, Millennium-Eyes Illusionist, Illusionist Faceless Magician and Golden-Eyes Idol all read it by name.
Can it be revived from the Graveyard? Yes, as long as it was Fusion Summoned first. Relinquished Fusion, Polymerization, Super Polymerization and Instant Fusion all qualify.
How many times can it absorb in one turn? Once from its own effect. The Special Summon window and the end of Battle Phase window share one hard once per turn between them.
Does the taunt stop a direct attack? The clause covers attacks on your other monsters. Effects that let a monster attack directly still go around it.
What does a negate actually turn off? All three continuous clauses at once. The ATK gain drops to nothing, the battle protection stops, and the taunt lifts, leaving a 0 ATK Level 1 body.

Where It Still Falls Over

  • It has no protection of its own. Nothing in the text stops it from being targeted, destroyed, banished or bounced.
  • A single Effect Veiler or Infinite Impermanence turns a stacked attacker back into a 0 ATK body and reopens the rest of your board to attacks.
  • The absorb is dead against an empty field. Going first it is a wall and nothing more.
  • Absorbing a monster that wants to be in the Graveyard only delays it. When the Fusion leaves, the monster it took lands exactly where the opponent wanted it.
  • The Ritual half of the deck still opens on high-commitment Normal Summons, and Ash Blossom, Droll & Lock Bird and Infinite Impermanence still end those turns.
  • The Type requirement is a real deckbuilding cost. Slots spent on an Illusion body are slots not spent on handtraps or Ritual consistency.

Who Else Wants It

Generic materials cut both ways. The strongest version of this card is often not in a Relinquished deck at all.

Chimera Illusion

Best home

Chimera Fusion is a Quick-Play that summons using hand or field including a Beast or Fiend, and Big-Winged Berfomet revives any Illusion monster from the Graveyard when it is used as Fusion material. That pair loops Nightmare-Eyes Restrict back onto the field on the opponent's turn without spending another Fusion Spell.

Hecahands

Wall

An Illusion board of low-ATK bodies that strips resources but gets run over in battle. The taunt and the battle clause are a shield built to that exact shape, and every Hecahands monster is already a legal material.

Super Polymerization decks

Side deck

Any list already running Super Polymerization gains a second answer for Fiend, Illusion and Spellcaster boards, and the summon trigger takes a third monster on top of the two it ate.

The identity trade. Moving "Eyes Restrict" into the Illusion Type is what makes the card work with the rest of the archetype, and it is also what makes it splashable everywhere else. Pegasus finally got his modern boss, and half the decks playing it will never run Relinquished.

The Verdict

Nightmare-Eyes Restrict

Run 2 to 3
  • The best removal the archetype has ever printed, and the first that ignores targeting protection entirely.
  • Paired with a set Relinquished Fusion it gives the deck a genuine interruption on the opponent's turn instead of a hope that the board survives.
  • Multiple copies matter because the equip is once per turn per card, not once per turn per name on the field.
  • Even outside the deck it is a Super Polymerization button worth a slot in the side.

What It Does Not Fix

Still rogue
  • The opening hands have not changed. The deck still needs its Ritual starters to resolve, and they still fold to the same three handtraps.
  • No card in this wave answers a resolved floodgate or a backrow the archetype cannot touch.
  • The Illusion body it demands is a tax the deck pays before it gets anything back.
  • Read it as a rogue deck that finally has a real interruption, not as a jump up the tier list.
