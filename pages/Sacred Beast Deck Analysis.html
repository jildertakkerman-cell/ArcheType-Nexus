Sacred Beasts

Unleashing the Phantasms

Lore Archive

"The Sacred Beasts—Uria, Hamon, and Raviel—are the dark, corrupted mirrors of the legendary Egyptian Gods. These titanic entities are not divine saviors, but rather destructive forces of pure chaos."

Known as the "Three Phantasms", these ancient Ka spirits possessed power so overwhelming and volatile that they had to be sealed away within stone tablets buried deep beneath Duel Academy. Slumbering for generations, their seals were eventually broken by those seeking to harness their god-like strength for selfish desires, such as the restoration of youth and absolute dominance. Unleashing these shadowed reflections—Uria, Lord of Searing Flames, Hamon, Lord of Striking Thunder, and Raviel, Lord of Phantasms—demands a terrifying sacrifice, as their very presence drains the life energy of the world around them.

The three Sacred Beasts of Yu-Gi-Oh! GX, the Egyptian Gods' dark mirrors, named after angels and sealed under Duel Academy until Kagemaru set them free. Follow a marked name to walk the trail.

Executive Summary

The Sacred Beasts?Uria, Hamon, and Raviel'?began as anime icons with immense power but impractical summoning conditions. Modern support has transformed them, shifting focus from their original summoning methods to a consistent engine that "cheats" them onto the field. This report analyzes how the Sacred Beasts evolved from three unwieldy titans into the synergistic core of a potent control and One-Turn Kill (OTK) deck, capable of establishing oppressive board states and ending duels with overwhelming force. The strategy now revolves around a suite of support cards designed to deploy the Three Phantoms with remarkable efficiency.

The Engine Room

The Prime Movers

The deck's consistency hinges on a core of 0 ATK/DEF Fiend monsters that search and summon the Sacred Beasts.

  • Dark Beckoning Beast: The one-card starter. On Normal Summon, it searches any "Sacred Beast" card and grants an additional Normal Summon for another 0 ATK/DEF Fiend.
  • Chaos Summoning Beast: Tributes itself to summon a Sacred Beast from the hand. Its GY effect banishes itself to search "Fallen Paradise".
  • Dark Summoning Beast: Tributes itself to summon a Sacred Beast from the hand or Deck, but prevents you from attacking that turn.

The Central Hub

The deck's spells provide recursion, protection, and card advantage, forming a resilient loop.

  • Opening of the Spirit Gates: A Continuous Spell that searches a monster on activation, revives a 0 ATK/DEF Fiend from the GY by discarding, and recycles a Continuous Spell from the GY.
  • Fallen Paradise: The Field Spell. While you control a Sacred Beast, it provides targeting and destruction protection and lets you draw 2 cards once per turn.

The Titans: Role and Application

Raviel, Lord of Phantasms

The primary beater. A 4000 ATK/DEF wall that enables a devastating OTK with its support card, Shimmering Scraper, which doubles its ATK to 8000 and allows it to attack all monsters.

Hamon, Lord of Striking Thunder

The defensive juggernaut. While in Defense Position, it forces the opponent to attack it. Its support spell, Cerulean Skyfire, provides a Spell/Trap negate, then swaps Hamon to Defense to activate its protection.

Uria, Lord of Searing Flames

The backrow punisher. It gains 1000 ATK for each Continuous Trap in the GY and can destroy a Set Spell/Trap once per turn. Its trap, Hyper Blaze, fuels its ATK and provides revival for any Sacred Beast.

The Grand Finale: Typical End Boards

The deck's goal is to establish a protected boss monster or, with a stronger hand, a complete lockdown of the opponent's monster effects.

The Core Fortress

  • A Sacred Beast (e.g., Raviel): A 4000 ATK/DEF body that's difficult to remove by battle.
  • Fallen Paradise: Provides complete targeting and destruction protection for your Beasts.
  • Card Advantage: The "Draw 2" from Fallen Paradise refills your hand with disruption or follow-up plays.

The "High Roll" Lockdown

  • Two+ Sacred Beasts: Achieved with Opening of the Spirit Gates, getting two different Beasts on field.
  • Awakening of the Sacred Beasts: With two Beasts, this Continuous Trap gains its devastating floodgate effect.
  • The Lock: "Negate the activated effects of monsters your opponent controls." This is a one-sided Skill Drain that paralyzes most decks.

Competitive Analysis

Strengths

  • High Consistency: Powerful one-card starters like Dark Beckoning Beast.
  • Strong Resilience: Fallen Paradise provides excellent protection, and the engine has many recovery loops.
  • Powerful End Boards: Can end on a 10,000 ATK OTK machine, a protected boss, or a one-sided Skill Drain.

Weaknesses

  • Normal Summon Reliant: The pure build is extremely vulnerable to hand traps (Ash, Imperm) on Dark Beckoning Beast.
  • Floodgate Vulnerability: Susceptible to cards like Macro Cosmos or Summon Limit that stop its engine.
  • Linear Combos: The main combo lines are predictable, allowing experienced opponents to disrupt key chokepoints.

External Engines & Hybrids

  • Branded & Bystial: The most competitive variant. Branded Fusion acts as an alternate starter, setting up the GY for Spirit Gates.
  • Eldlich: Synergizes with the focus on Continuous Traps, fueling Uria and recycling "Golden Land" cards with Awakening.
  • Dogmatika: Nadir Servant can send monsters to the GY while Ecclesia provides on-field negation.

Chaos Origins Tactical Realignment

The release of the Chaos Origins booster set completely updates the mechanical paradigm of the Sacred Beasts, transitioning it from a fragile, casual gimmick deck into a highly consistent, mid-range combo engine. Rather than relying on external engines to bypass summoning conditions, the new support integrates directly into the core theme. It introduces in-hand reveal-and-search effects to prevent bricking, self-replicating Continuous Spells/Traps to satisfy card costs, and retrained forms of the three iconic titans. However, players must account for vulnerabilities to hand traps (like Droll & Lock Bird and Mulcharmy Fuwalos), graveyard-banishing effects (like Dimension Shifter), and a TCG database naming discrepancy for the Fusion boss.

The Retrained Titans: Role and Application

Uria, Lord of Searing Flames - Sacred Beast of Cataclysmic Fire

Global Trap scaling. Reveals in hand to search a "Sacred Beast" Trap and discard 1. Gains 1000 ATK/DEF for each Trap in both GYs. Quick Effect: targets and destroys any Spell/Trap on the field (neither player can respond).

Hamon, Lord of Striking Thunder - Sacred Beast of Sinful Catastrophe

Passive burn. Reveals in hand to search a "Sacred Beast" Spell and discard 1. Inflicts 1000 damage whenever a monster is sent to the opponent's GY. If destroyed, reduces all damage taken that turn to 0.

Raviel, Lord of Phantasms - Sacred Beast of Endless Eternity

Board-wipe finish. Reveals in hand to search a "Sacred Beast" monster (except itself) and discard 1. Quick Effect: tributes 2 other Sacred Beasts to destroy all opponent's monsters and gain 1000 ATK for each.

The Chaotic Phantasmal Sacred Beasts

Fusion Boss (3 Level 10s). Thrice-per-turn Quick Effect: target, negate an opponent's monster, and gain LP equal to half its ATK. Cannot be destroyed by card effects twice per turn. Note: OCG name translation differences may affect TCG compatibility with legacy searchers.

Beyond the Brave: Sacred Beasts Skyfall

  • TCG · Beyond the Brave · 8 Oct 2026
  • OCG · 18 Jul 2026
  • Genesys · 10 points

Beyond the Brave gives the Chaos Origins Sacred Beasts one new card: Sacred Beasts Skyfall, a Quick-Play Spell. It fixes the deck's two weak points: hand traps on the Level 10 reveal effects, and a thin going-second turn.

Early coverage of the OCG release calls it Divine Destruction of the Sacred Beasts or Heavenly Destruction of Sacred Beast. Those are fan translations; this guide uses the TCG name. The Genesys value is from the initial Beyond the Brave points list (22 Sep 2026).

Chaos Origins cards only

Skyfall only counts cards whose names contain "Sacred Beast". The original Uria, Hamon and Raviel, Shimmering Scraper and Dark Beckoning Beast don't, so Skyfall can't reveal those monsters or protect their effects. The Standard tab's lines play exactly as before. See which cards count.

The Reveal Gets Through

Ash Blossom on Calamity Hamon or Infinity Raviel used to cost you the card they add and leave a Level 10 stuck in your hand. The first effect negates it, and the reveal resolves.

A Board Wipe You Can Search

The second effect adds Illusion Gate, which destroys every monster your opponent controls. Calamity Hamon adds Skyfall, so every Hamon in your opening hand leads to the wipe.

Three Traps In, One Card Gone

The third effect shuffles 3 "Sacred Beast" Traps back into the Deck and destroys any card your opponent controls. Set, it works on their turn.

What Skyfall Adds: Illusion Gate

Illusion Gate

Effect 2's search target · Normal Spell

Illusion Gate

Card text

Pay half your LP; destroy as many monsters your opponent controls as possible, then you can Special Summon 1 monster from your opponent's GY to your field, ignoring its Summoning conditions. You can only apply this effect of "Illusion Gate" once per Duel.

Destroys; it doesn't target or banish. Monsters that can't be destroyed by card effects stay on the field. The summon ignores Summoning conditions, so an Extra Deck boss you just destroyed can join your attack. Its effect applies once per Duel, so run one copy: a second one does nothing after the first has resolved.

Can Skyfall Stop It?

Effect 1 answers a card or effect your opponent activates in response to a "Sacred Beast" monster's effect. It all depends on the timing: what you activated, how they answered, and where your Skyfall is.

Adding Skyfall to the Deck

The Skyfall package

  • 2
    Sacred Beasts Skyfall

    Two in the Main Deck, a third in the Side Deck for going second. Only one can be activated per turn, so a third copy often sits in your hand going first.

  • 1
    Illusion Gate

    Its effect applies once per Duel, and Skyfall is how you find it.

  • 3

    Keep it at three: it's the only "Sacred Beast" card that adds Skyfall from the Deck.

  • 0/3
    Abyss of the Sacred Beasts

    Play three only if you want effect 3 to be live. With just 1 Awakening and 1 Combined Assault, you'll rarely have 3 "Sacred Beast" Traps.

These counts are this guide's recommendation for a 40-card Advanced deck, built on the registered Chaos Origins Fiendsmith lists. They don't change anything in the Standard or Chaos Origins tabs.

What changes in the engine

  • Calamity Hamon gets a new target. Its reveal now picks between Skyfall, Thunderclap, Fallen Paradise of the Sacred Beasts and Sacred Beasts Released. Going second, take Skyfall.
  • Hold Skyfall going first. Keep it in hand until your opponent chains to one of your reveals. Using effect 2 or 3 first spends your one Skyfall activation for the turn.
  • Discard with a plan. Discarding a spare Level 10 to effect 2 sets up Fallen Paradise, Summoner and Martyr's End Phase return.
  • Mind Inferno Uria. In an Uria build, fire effect 3 only when the destroyed card is worth 3000 ATK on Uria.

Limits and Verdict

  • One Skyfall per turn

    The limit covers the card, not each effect. If you add Illusion Gate with it on your turn, you have no effect 1 for the rest of that turn.

  • It can't protect itself

    Ash Blossom on effect 2's search, and Droll & Lock Bird after any search, still work. Effect 1 only covers monster effects.

  • Destruction only

    Illusion Gate and effect 3 both destroy. Monsters that can't be destroyed by card effects stay, and Illusion Gate costs half your LP.

  • Effect 3 needs its own Traps

    Without Abyss, three "Sacred Beast" Traps in the GY are rare. Hyper Blaze and other Traps don't count.

Verdict

Sacred Beasts Skyfall belongs in every Chaos Origins Sacred Beast deck at two copies. Going first it protects the Level 10 reveals the whole engine depends on. Going second, every Calamity Hamon now leads to a board wipe. Effect 3 is a late-game bonus unless you build around Abyss.
