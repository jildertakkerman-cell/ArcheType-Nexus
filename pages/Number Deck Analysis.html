Executive Summary

The "Number" cards are an expansive and unique collection of Xyz Monsters from the Yu-Gi-Oh! ZEXAL era. In lore, they are fragments of a celestial being's memory, which translates to a diverse series of over one hundred and sixty unique monsters spanning countless Ranks, Types, and effects. Rather than a synergistic archetype, "Number" is a broad designation that lacks a cohesive internal engine. Its power is instead unlocked through powerful, dedicated support cards and external engines�most notably the Onomat engine�designed to rapidly assemble materials for their summon.

Competitive Analysis

Strengths

  • High Ceiling: Can produce nearly unbreakable boards with multiple negations.
  • Consistent Engine: Numerous searchers and one-card starters make the core combo highly accessible.
  • Vast Toolbox: Access to the entire "Number" lineup and generic Xyz monsters like Zeus offers great flexibility.

Weaknesses

  • Choke Points: The combo is linear and highly vulnerable to well-timed hand traps on key searchers or extenders.
  • Board Breakers: Can struggle going second against established boards, and is susceptible to cards like Lava Golem or Kaijus.

New Card Spotlight: Number 39: Utopia, Emissary of Light

Konami spent 2026 modernising the ace monsters of the first six anime series, and Yuma Tsukumo's turn produced a brand new Rank 4 Utopia. It is the first real-world "Number" to carry the anime battle immunity clause, and it is the cheapest competitive "Number" boss the archetype has ever had — in the one format where cheap matters.

Naming

The OCG print is Number 39: Utopia the Envoy of Light, and early listings also used Messenger of Light. All three names refer to this card.

The Clause That Never Made It To Print

Every "Number" in the ZEXAL anime carried the same line: cannot be destroyed by battle, except with a "Number" monster. Konami stripped it from the real cards in 2011, reasoning that a Rank 4 anyone could summon should not also be an unkillable wall. Fifteen years later, Emissary of Light puts it back — and it is still the only mainline "Number" you can make off two generic Level 4 monsters that has it.

The mirror-match tell. The immunity has a "Number" exception, so the one thing that reliably kills it in combat is another "Number" monster. In a room full of Utopia decks that exception matters far more than it looks on paper.

The ATK Ladder

The boost is permanent and it triggers on the second attack of the turn, declared by either player. Stack it under an existing "Utopia" doubler and the number stops being theoretical.

2500 On summon

Printed body off any two Level 4 monsters.

5000 Second attack declared

Gains 2500 permanently, and starts stealing whatever it destroys by battle.

7500 Summoned off Utopia Double

Utopia Double doubles it to 5000 on summon, then the battle trigger adds 2500 on top.

Read the Utopia Double line carefully. A monster summoned by Number 39: Utopia Double cannot attack directly, so the 7500 body is a wall-breaker rather than a one-swing kill. The steal effect is what turns that into a two-turn win instead of a stalled board.

What Actually Changes

A protected Rank 4

Two generic Level 4 monsters now buy a body that survives combat outright and hands a destruction save to something else on board.

Battle steps became removal

Past 5000 ATK it does not just win combat, it takes the monster. Every attack is a one-for-one that also grows your board.

Five points in Genesys

The archetype's real boss monsters cost 80 to 100 points. This one costs five, which is the entire reason it sees play.

No answer to non-destruction

Bounce, banish and shuffle effects go straight through it. The shield only ever reads "destroyed".

One Card, Two Verdicts

The same card is a pass in one format and a staple in the other. Which one you are building for settles the question entirely.

Advanced Format

Leave it out

Modern Yu-Gi-Oh! is decided in Main Phase 1. A monster that needs to survive to the Battle Phase, then wait for a second attack, is answered by almost every interruption in the format before it does anything. Play S:P Little Knight or Utopia the Lightning instead.

Genesys Format

Auto-include

Links and Pendulums are banned, the game is slower, and every point of your 100-point budget is contested. At five points this is the cheapest real boss monster in the "Number" pool. The full case is in the Impact Report tab.

Where It Falls Over

Dracotail Horn answers it cleanly. The trap targets an Attack Position monster and returns it to the hand or Extra Deck. No destruction, so the shield never reads.
It has to reach the Battle Phase. The 2500 boost and the steal both wait on a second attack being declared.
No interruption of its own. It negates nothing and removes nothing on the opponent's turn.

Impact Report Genesys Format 2026

The Card That Advanced Rejected And Genesys Adopted

Competitive analysts wrote Emissary of Light off within a week of the reveal: too slow, too battle-dependent, no answer to bounce. They were right about Advanced. Then the point-based Genesys list taxed every real "Number" boss monster into unplayability, and the card nobody wanted turned out to cost five points.

How Genesys Works

A 100-point budget

The Forbidden and Limited list is replaced by point costs. Most cards are free, powerful ones are priced, and your whole deck must fit inside 100.

No Links, no Pendulums

Both mechanics are banned outright. The field reverts to the original layout with no Extra Monster Zones and no Pendulum Zones.

Forced trade-offs

Elite generic cards or an elite archetype engine, not both. Every point spent on a boss monster is a hand trap you cannot play.

Battles decide games

Without Links the format is slower and more combat-oriented, which is exactly the environment Emissary of Light was designed for.

The Eclipse Twins Recursion

This is the interaction that makes the card durable rather than merely cheap. The shield resets every time the monster comes back.

Board state Utopia, Emissary of Light with material  +  Eclipse Twins on field or heading to the graveyard
Opponent commits A mass removal effect resolves. Detach one material as a Quick Effect to shield Emissary or a key monster beside it
Eclipse Twins hits the graveyard Destroyed on field, or simply sent as Xyz material. Either route turns the effect on
The refill Target Emissary of Light and another Rank 4 Xyz in the graveyard. Special Summon one, attach the other as material
Back where you started Emissary is on board again with fresh material, so the destruction shield is available for the next turn

A five-point monster that keeps coming back with its protection reloaded is a very different proposition from a five-point monster that trades once.

The Bottom Line

  • Advanced Format: skip it. It asks to survive to the Battle Phase in a format decided in Main Phase 1, and any bounce or banish effect answers it without engaging the shield.
  • Genesys: play it. Five points buys a protected 2500 body that scales to 5000 and steals what it kills, leaving 95 points for the interaction that actually wins games.
  • Build around Eclipse Twins. The recursion is what separates this from a one-and-done beatstick, and it is free.
  • Casual appeal is real. It is the first printed "Number" to carry the anime battle immunity clause, which lands at any casual table.
  • The wider lesson. Genesys did not make a bad card good. It changed which questions the format asks, and this card happened to already have the answers.

Rulings and Timing

  • Double materials need 3 or more. Gorgonic and Shining count as 2 only for a Number that needs 3 or more materials. Masquerade V qualifies; Number 39: Utopia, which needs 2, does not.
  • One step up, once per turn. The monster summoned on Masquerade V gets the banish effect. The Dragon summoned on C104 does not, and a second Masquerade V ranked up in the same turn gets nothing.
  • The detach is per copy. It has no name clause, so two Masquerade V can add two Barian's cards in one turn.
  • Your opponent chooses. They can banish 4 monsters with different Attributes and 1 Spell of their choice, or refuse and lose the top 10 cards of their Deck.
  • The 2013 C104 wants the 2013 name. Its negate needs "Number 104: Masquerade" as material, and Masquerade V has a different name. Rank up the original to unlock it.
  • V works on any Masquerade Xyz. That includes both Rank 5 C104s, so a second V Umbral Horror can take one to a Rank 6 Number such as Number 39: Utopia Beyond.

New Rank-Up: Hyper Rank-Up-Magic Ultimate Force

Every earlier Utopia Rank-Up Spell stayed inside the Utopia family. Ultimate Force starts from a Utopia but can end on any Rank 10 or higher "Number". A Rank 4 board can become Number 77: The Seven Sins or Number 81: Superdreadnought Rail Cannon Super Dora as easily as Number 99: Utopia Dragonar. Activation · the launchpad

Target 1 Rank 9 or lower "Utopia" or "Utopic" Xyz Monster you control whose original Attribute is LIGHT. Its materials don't matter, so an Xyz Monster that has already spent them still works. The Japanese text uses one word, ホープ ("Hope"), for both names, so qualifies. Hyper Rank-Up-Magic Utopiforce asks for a "Utopia" monster and can't use it.

Resolution · any big Number

Special Summon 1 Rank 10 or higher "Number" Xyz Monster from your Extra Deck using the target. It is treated as an Xyz Summon: the target and its materials go under the new monster, then Ultimate Force attaches itself as well. A Rank 4 with 2 materials becomes a Rank 10 or higher monster with 4.

Granted effect · "Number 99" only

A "Number 99" Xyz Monster with Ultimate Force as material gains a Quick Effect. Once per Chain, when your opponent activates a monster effect during the Battle Phase, it detaches all its materials and destroys every monster your opponent controls. The effect is not optional: the text has no "you can". It works in either player's Battle Phase, Damage Step included, and the detach and the destruction happen at the same time.

Searching it

It is a "Rank-Up-Magic" Normal Spell, so a "Utopia" Xyz Monster summoned with on the field as material can add it. can't: it adds "Xyz", "Onomat", "Zexal" or "Number" Spells and Traps, and none of those words is in this card's name.

A "Number 99" Xyz Monster that has this card as material gains this effect.

    Claims Checked Against the Cards

    Early write-ups of this card repeated several plays that the printed text doesn't allow. Here is each claim next to what the cards actually say.

    Claim Verdict What the cards say
    Ultimate Force summons Number 100: Numeron Dragon for a going-second OTK. False Numeron Dragon is Rank 1. Dragonar can bring it out, because it is number 100.
    Dragonar can bring out Evolzar Lars. False Dragonar only summons "Number" monsters numbered 1 to 100. Lars isn't a Number.
    Dragonar can bring out Varudras. False Varudras, the Final Bringer of the End Times is a Rank 10 Xyz Monster, but it isn't a Number, so neither Dragonar nor Ultimate Force can summon it. It is also Forbidden in the TCG.
    Dragonar can bring out Numeron Dragon for the OTK instead. False It arrives with no materials, so it can't detach for its ATK effect and stays at 0 ATK. The turn Dragonar uses that effect, your other monsters can't attack directly either.
    Number C1000 drops a 10,000 ATK threat. False Its official name is Number C1000: Numerounius. Its ATK is "?", no effect sets it, so on the field it has 0.
    ZS - Utopic Sage detaches 1 to summon ZS - Ascended Sage. False It detaches 2 and summons any "ZW -" or "ZS -" monster from the Deck. After that you can only Special Summon Xyz Monsters from the Extra Deck, and only Numbers can attack.
    Ascended Sage searches Ultimate Force from the GY after it becomes material. False The search is an effect the new "Utopia" Xyz Monster gains, and it triggers when that monster is Xyz Summoned. ZS - Utopic Sage is a "Utopic" card, not a "Utopia" one, so it doesn't gain it.
    Gagaga Magic summons itself and a partner, and you overlay them later. False Its effect Xyz Summons straight away, using only those 2 monsters.
    Astraltopia searches Ultimate Force. False Astraltopia adds an "Xyz", "Onomat", "Zexal" or "Number" Spell or Trap.
    Utopia Double searches a Rank-Up-Magic Spell. False It adds Double or Nothing!, then ranks itself into another "Utopia" Xyz Monster.
    The Battle Phase wipe is a choice you make. Partly It is mandatory and goes off on the first monster effect your opponent activates in the Battle Phase. It works in your Battle Phase too.
    Bystial cards can remove the Utopia in response to Ultimate Force. Partly Bystial hand effects banish a LIGHT or DARK monster from a GY, not from the field. Bystial Baldrake on the field can banish the new Number after it is summoned, and its materials, Ultimate Force included, go to the GY. A card that does answer the Spell itself is S:P Little Knight: it banishes the Utopia until the End Phase, so nothing is summoned.
    The summon is an Xyz Summon, so Xyz Change Tactics draws a card. True For Dragonar, which is a "Utopia" monster. Not for Utopic Dragon or The Seven Sins.
    The card is the anime card from ZEXAL episode 146. True Astral used it there. The anime version only went to a Rank 10 "Number" from a Utopia; the real card reaches any Rank 10 or higher and adds the Number 99 effect.

    It targets. If the launchpad leaves the field or changes control before the Spell resolves, nothing is summoned and Ultimate Force is gone.
    Normal Spell speed. It only works in your own Main Phase, so it can't dodge removal on your opponent's turn.
