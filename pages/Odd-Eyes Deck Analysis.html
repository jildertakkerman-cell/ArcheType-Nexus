New Card Spotlight: The Four Heavenly Dragons

The 2026 support wave did not end with the Legendary ARC-V Decks box. A second, larger wave retrained all four ARC-V Dimension Dragons at once, and the one that matters most to this deck sits in the Main Deck: Odd-Eyes Pendulum Dragon of the Four Heavenly Dragons. It turns the archetype's original ace from an End Phase searcher into a Main Phase engine piece and a real win condition — at the cost of the most awkward restriction the deck has ever had to sequence around.

Odd-Eyes Pendulum Dragon of the Four Heavenly Dragons

Level 7 DARK Dragon / Pendulum 2500 / 2000 Scale 4
Main Phase self-pop

In the Pendulum Zone it destroys itself during your Main Phase to add any Pendulum monster with 1500 or less ATK from the Deck to your hand. The original card made you wait until the End Phase, which meant the search only ever paid off next turn.

Scale-scaled ATK

Pendulum Summoned, it gains 300 ATK for every point of gap between your two active scales, and any battle damage the opponent takes from a battle involving it is doubled.

Self-recovery

Destroyed in the Monster Zone by battle or by a card effect, it returns to your hand. If the opponent destroyed it, you may Special Summon it back instead of holding it.

The price

Once the search resolves you cannot activate monster effects on the field for the rest of the turn, unless you Pendulum Summon afterwards. Both of its effects are hard once per turn.

On the name. The printed card name is Odd-Eyes Pendulum Dragon of the Four Heavenly Dragons, but its own effect text and coverage articles both shorten it to Odd-Eyes Pendulum Dragon, Four Heavenly Dragons. All four dragons in the cycle share that quirk. Search the longer form when you look them up.

What Actually Changes

The search opens the turn

End Phase became Main Phase. The scale pop is now the first action of a combo instead of a consolation prize after it, and it is a clean plus one.

A free Extra Deck body

Popping itself sends a face-up DARK Dragon Pendulum to the Extra Deck, which the new Astrograph retrain can shuffle away on the opponent's turn to Fusion Summon.

The ace can end games

Extreme scales plus doubled battle damage means the same 2500 body that was a joke for a decade now closes out a turn three by itself.

Your Monster Zone goes quiet

No on-field monster effects until you Pendulum Summon. That is a genuine cost, and it collides head-on with Beyond the Pendulum.

The Sequencing Problem

Two Locks, One Pendulum Summon

Odd-Eyes Pendulum Dragon of the Four Heavenly Dragons and Beyond the Pendulum both shut you down until you Pendulum Summon, and you normally get one Pendulum Summon per turn. Resolve both searches in one turn and one of the two locks never lifts.

They are not the same lock, and the difference decides the order. Four Heavenly Dragons stops monster effects on the field. Beyond the Pendulum stops monster effects full stop, with no "on the field" qualifier, and negates the effects of both cards in your Pendulum Zones. Under Beyond's lock you cannot even pop a scale, so Beyond has to go first and its Pendulum Summon has to land before you touch Four Heavenly Dragons.

That is exactly the hole Odd-Eyes Override Dragon fills. Destroying a Pendulum Monster Card with its first effect grants a second Pendulum Summon, which is the only reason both searches fit in the same turn. The order below is not optional.

1

Build three Pendulum bodies with your Spell and Trap engine, then Link Summon Beyond the Pendulum in the Extra Monster Zone and pay 1200 Life Points to search. Lock one is on, and your scales are now negated too.

2

Pendulum Summon immediately. This is your normal Pendulum Summon, and it lifts both halves of Beyond the Pendulum's restriction, including the scale negation.

3

Link Summon Odd-Eyes Override Dragon using Beyond the Pendulum and a freshly summoned Pendulum monster. Its own-turn negate is now live.

4

Use Override Dragon to destroy a face-up Pendulum Monster Card: draw one, and bank a second Pendulum Summon.

5

Only now place Four Heavenly Dragons in the scale and pop it for the search. Lock two goes on, and your field effects are already spent.

6

Spend the second Pendulum Summon to clear lock two and put the endboard down, with Four Heavenly Dragons itself as one of the summoned bodies.

Do not do this Pop Four Heavenly Dragons first, then try to Link Summon into Beyond the Pendulum. Beyond the Pendulum's search is a monster effect on the field, so it is dead until you Pendulum Summon, and you only have one Pendulum Summon to spend
Read the four words "on the field". A card sitting in the Pendulum Zone is a Spell Card, so scale effects keep working through Four Heavenly Dragons' own lock — including the pop that applied it. What goes silent is the Monster Zone. Hand traps, graveyard effects, Spells and Traps are all untouched, which is why the Primite and Wings of Light package is the preferred way to open the turn.
The free interaction most people miss. Four Heavenly Dragons is an "Odd-Eyes" card, and Odd-Eyes Arc Pendulum Dragon's Pendulum Effect triggers when an Odd-Eyes card you control is destroyed, Special Summoning an Odd-Eyes monster from the hand, Deck or graveyard. Both are scale effects, so popping Four Heavenly Dragons with Arc Pendulum Dragon in the other zone puts a body on the board straight through the lock.

The Z-ARC Line Finally Works

Soul of the Supreme King was a meme for years. Pay half your Life Points for a Z-ARC with its effects negated, then later banish both from the graveyard to Special Summon one each of your "Pendulum Dragon", "Xyz Dragon", "Synchro Dragon" and "Fusion Dragon" monsters. The original four Dimension Dragons did almost nothing on arrival, so it was four Extra Deck slots for no interaction. The retrains fix that at the root: their disruption triggers on being Special Summoned, not on being properly Fusion, Synchro or Xyz Summoned, and each carries the exact name that clause is looking for.

Two Souls, Two Different Payoffs

The two Trap cards are easy to confuse and do opposite things with the same four dragons. One banishes them to switch a Z-ARC on. The other summons them for three triggers. Both start from Supreme King Gate Magician, which needs another Supreme King Gate card in your Pendulum Zone, sends a "Pendulum Dragon" from the hand or face-up Extra Deck to the graveyard to Special Summon itself, and then adds any card that mentions Supreme King Z-ARC from the Deck. The copy of Four Heavenly Dragons you popped in your scale is exactly that "Pendulum Dragon" fodder.

Building Verdict

Odd-Eyes Pendulum Dragon of the Four Heavenly Dragons

Play two, not three
  • Scale 4 is an awkward middle. Your combos Pendulum Summon Level 7s, which wants a 0 and an 8 or higher. Two copies in hand is a brick.
  • Both effects are hard once per turn. A second copy adds nothing on the turn you already used one.
  • It is trivially searchable. Skullcrobat Joker, Beyond the Pendulum and Spiral Flame Strike all find it, so drawing it is not the plan.
  • Commit it late. It is the last card you activate before your final Pendulum Summon, never the first thing you do.

The Three Extra Deck Retrains

One each, if you run the Trap
  • They only earn their slots alongside a Soul Trap and Supreme King Gate Magician to find one. Pick your payoff: the Celestial King version banishes them to switch Z-ARC on, the original summons them for three triggers. Without either they are three dead cards.
  • Each is treated as a Predaplant, Speedroid or Phantom Knights card, so they are also live in those decks independently of Odd-Eyes.
  • Dark Rebellion is the most flexible of the three, since its Quick Effect tags into any Rank 4 DARK Xyz you already play.

Where It Still Falls Over

The lock is a real tax. Between the pop and the Pendulum Summon you hold a board of functionally vanilla monsters. Any disruption in that window leaves you with no way to restart.
Nibiru math is tight. Override Dragon's negate can answer Nibiru, but you need three bodies for a Link-3 before your fifth summon. Often the Link comes one summon too late.
Dominus Impulse ends turns. Negating the Special Summons that hold the Link climb together stops the line before Override Dragon is ever online.
No Battle Phase protection. The 6100 ATK body dies to any attack negation, Impermanence or targeted removal, and the OTK dies with it.
Floodgates still win. Evilswarm Ophion stops Level 7 Pendulum Summons outright, which turns off the lock-clearing summon and the ATK payoff at the same time.
Engine bloat. Odd-Eyes, Supreme King, Performapal and Primite in one list leaves almost no room for hand traps or board breakers, which is exactly what going second needs.
Honest verdict. This wave raises the ceiling of an uninterrupted turn a long way and does very little for the deck's real problem, which is playing through interaction and going second. Odd-Eyes is a considerably better rogue deck than it was, with a genuine win condition attached to its own ace for the first time. It is still not a tiered strategy.

Current State: The Complete Extra Deck Toolbox

The Legendary ARC-V Decks box set (August 2026) finally completed Odd-Eyes' mechanical arsenal. With the arrival of Odd-Eyes Solblaze Dragon (a Synchro Tuner) and Odd-Eyes Override Dragon (the archetype's first dedicated Link Monster), Odd-Eyes became one of the only strategies in the game to coherently use every major Extra Deck mechanic—Fusion, Synchro, Xyz, Link, and Pendulum—in a single, unified game plan.

Rather than a fragile, linear combo deck, the modern build functions as an adaptable, multi-layered strategy capable of playing through high-impact disruption: Override Dragon insulates the combo from mid-play handtraps and can rebuild a broken board on the fly, while Solblaze Dragon turns spent Extra Deck resources into a bridge toward towering Level 12 Synchro boss monsters.

The New Keystones: Solblaze & Override

Odd-Eyes Solblaze Dragon

Bridge

Odd-Eyes Solblaze Dragon

Synchro Summoned mid-combo to briefly revive a face-up Pendulum monster from the Extra Deck, then used as Synchro material itself to climb into a Level 12 boss like Supreme King Z-ARC - Synchro Universe.

Once it's spent, banish two other Extra Deck monsters from the GY to bring it back as a live body—turning otherwise-dead resources into another combo piece without touching your Normal or Pendulum Summon.

Odd-Eyes Override Dragon

Shield

Odd-Eyes Override Dragon

Linked away before the main Pendulum Summon so its Quick Effect is live: discard a card to negate whatever handtrap or interruption the opponent throws at the rest of the combo.

Once the coast is clear, destroy one of your own scales to draw a card and pick up a second Pendulum Summon—instantly rebuilding the board if it was broken anyway.

Patching the Archetype's Historic Weaknesses

Handtrap Insulation

Pendulum decks have always been vulnerable to mid-combo handtraps like Nibiru, the Primal Being or Ash Blossom & Joyous Spring before an active negate is online. By laddering three Pendulum bodies into Beyond the Pendulum and then into Override Dragon, the deck can establish a discard-to-negate Quick Effect before committing its main Pendulum Summon, insulating the rest of the combo.

Resource Recovery

If the board still gets broken, Override Dragon can pop one of its own Pendulum scales to draw a card and immediately gain a second Pendulum Summon—rebuilding from a wipe in a single action. Solblaze Dragon complements this by banishing two spent Extra Deck monsters from the GY to revive itself as a live Level 8 body, without touching the Normal or Pendulum Summon.

Key Combo Lines

The Anti-Nibiru Override Line

  1. Use standard resources/engines to generate 3 Pendulum bodies on the field.
  2. Link Summon Beyond the Pendulum.
  3. Use Beyond the Pendulum and 1 other monster to Link Summon Odd-Eyes Override Dragon as your 5th summon.
  4. Override Dragon now provides a live discard-to-negate, safe from Nibiru before your main Pendulum Summon.
  5. Use Override Dragon's first effect to destroy one of your own Pendulum scales: draw 1 card and gain a second Pendulum Summon to flood the board.

The Solblaze Z-ARC Synchro Climb

  1. Pendulum Summon Harmonizing Magician and use its effect to Special Summon a Level 4 DARK Pendulum monster (e.g. Abyss Actor - Curtain Raiser or Supreme King Dragon Darkwurm) from the deck.
  2. Tune them together to Synchro Summon Odd-Eyes Solblaze Dragon.
  3. Use Solblaze's on-summon effect to Special Summon the Level 4 DARK Pendulum monster back from the Extra Deck (this lifts the "Pendulum monsters only" lock the moment it becomes material).
  4. Tune the Level 8 Solblaze Dragon and the Level 4 DARK Pendulum monster together to Synchro Summon Supreme King Z-ARC - Synchro Universe.

New Engine Synergies

The Primite Engine

Reprinted alongside the new dragons, Primite Lordly Lode searches Primite Roar, which Special Summons a Normal Tuner (Labradorite Dragon) and a DARK non-Tuner Normal Pendulum monster (Dragon Horn Hunter) straight from the deck—without using the Normal Summon. This sets up a Level 12 Synchro play before any hand resources are committed, baiting interaction early.

The Electrumite Climb (OCG only)

In formats where Heavymetalfoes Electrumite is legal, it searches Astrograph Sorcerer to trigger a rapid self-summon chain, climbing directly into Override Dragon as the 5th summon—ahead of any Nibiru window. Electrumite remains TCG-forbidden, so TCG lists lean on Beyond the Pendulum as the bridge instead.

Absolute Dragon + Double or Nothing!

For blind-second strategies, attacking with Odd-Eyes Absolute Dragon and negating your own attack to trigger its effect, then following up with the quick-play spell Double or Nothing!, doubles its ATK for a second attack—a direct, high-damage OTK line.

The Modern Endboard

Instead of passing on a purely reactive board, the modern build establishes Override Dragon mid-combo for handtrap insulation and a rebuildable Pendulum Summon, then still has the option to ladder into a towering Level 12 Synchro finisher.

Override Dragon as Mid-Combo Shield

Established before the main Pendulum Summon, Override Dragon's turn-player negate absorbs a handtrap that would otherwise blow out the rest of the combo, while its destroy-for-draw effect keeps a second Pendulum Summon in reserve if the board is broken anyway.

Level 12 Synchro Ladder (Master Duel)

In Master Duel, Solblaze Dragon serves as a bridge to Level 12 Synchro monsters reminiscent of Centur-Ion plays, laddering into Crimson Dragon and ultimately Cosmic Blazar Dragon as a towering alternative finisher.

Competitive Analysis: 2026 Update

New Strengths

  • Complete Extra Deck Coverage: One of the only archetypes to coherently use Fusion, Synchro, Xyz, Link, and Pendulum mechanics together.
  • Handtrap Insulation: Override Dragon's turn-player negate protects the combo from Nibiru and similar disruption.
  • Board Recovery: A broken board can be rebuilt via Override's bonus Pendulum Summon or Solblaze's GY recursion.

New Weaknesses

  • Ash Blossom / Impermanence Chokepoint: Chaining either to Override's destroy-to-draw effect denies the bonus Pendulum Summon entirely, leaving an exhausted board.
  • Turn-Player-Only Negation: Override's negate only works on your own turn; it provides zero disruption on the opponent's turn unless linked away into Divine Arsenal AA-ZEUS - Sky Thunder or S:P Little Knight.
  • Extra Deck Resource Depletion: Both dragons demand heavy Extra Deck commitment, and overcommitting early can leave the deck without answers in a grindy game.

Tier Assessment

Odd-Eyes is currently assessed as a strong Rogue-tier strategy: a high ceiling—including consistent OCG First-Turn-Kill lines (Solblaze alongside Denglong, Cupid Pitch, and Thunder Dragon Colossus placed 2nd at YCSJ Osaka)—held back by the Pendulum mechanic's inherent hand-commitment fragility.

Of the three decks in the Legendary ARC-V Decks box, the community largely views Lunalight as the most meta-relevant, with Odd-Eyes sitting firmly in "decent but not Tier 1."

Defining the Playstyle

The Odd-Eyes archetype is a dynamic and complex force, representing a combo-centric Pendulum strategy renowned for its explosive, high-ceiling turns and access to a remarkably versatile Extra Deck toolbox. Its identity is flexible, often serving as the central chassis for a fusion of multiple archetypes like "Performapal," "Magician," and "Supreme King." At its core, Odd-Eyes can pivot between a "Vortex Control" approach, focusing on interruptions with Odd-Eyes Vortex Dragon, and an "OTK Turbo" strategy that leverages its swarming capabilities to end the duel in a single turn.

The Core Engine: Mastering the Arc Pendulum Loop

The Lynchpin: Odd-Eyes Arc Pendulum Dragon

At the very center of the strategy lies Odd-Eyes Arc Pendulum Dragon. Its Pendulum Effect is the engine's ignition: "If an 'Odd-Eyes' card(s) you control is destroyed by battle or card effect: You can Special Summon 1 'Odd-Eyes' monster from your hand, Deck, or GY." This transforms any self-destruction into a free Special Summon, forming the basis of the deck's most explosive plays.

Odd-Eyes Arc Pendulum Dragon
Sky Iris

The Catalyst: Sky Iris

The premier method for triggering Arc Pendulum Dragon is the Field Spell, Sky Iris. Its effect to target and destroy another face-up card you control to search an "Odd-Eyes" card is the primary engine starter. This re-frames the deck's mechanics: destruction is not a cost but an activation condition for a far more powerful effect.

Optimal Endboard: Vortex Control

The primary goal when going first is to construct a board with multiple layers of interaction. The "Absolute-to-Vortex" play is the most resource-efficient way to achieve this, converting two Level 7 monsters into a powerful Link monster and the deck's ultimate boss monster.

Competitive Analysis

Strengths

  • High Ceiling & Explosiveness: Capable of creating massive boards with multiple interruptions or executing swift One-Turn Kills (OTKs).
  • Consistency: A deep pool of versatile and redundant searchers ensures reliable access to core engine components.
  • Flexible Toolbox: The Extra Deck provides a wide range of answers, allowing pivots between control and aggressive strategies.

Weaknesses

  • Anti-Pendulum Floodgates: Extremely vulnerable to cards like Anti-Spell Fragrance which prevent placing Pendulum Scales.
  • Key Choke Points: Relies on key Normal Summons and spells which can be negated by common hand traps like Effect Veiler.
  • Extra Deck Locking: Cards that prevent Extra Deck access, such as Dimension Barrier, can be crippling.

Deck Building Philosophy

  • Core Ratios: Maximize consistency by running three copies of crucial engine pieces like Odd-Eyes Arc Pendulum Dragon and Spiral Flame Strike.
  • Synergistic Engines: Integrates seamlessly with "Performapal", "Magician", and "Supreme King" engines to enhance consistency and power.
  • Tech Choices: Flexible slots are filled with hand traps and board-breakers to adapt to the specific metagame.
