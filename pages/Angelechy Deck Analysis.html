Angelechy

Lore Archive

"Descending from the celestial plane, the Angelechy are divine architects of the battlefield. Manifesting as ethereal, white-marble chess pieces, they view combat not as a brawl, but as a calculated grandmaster's game."

As a newly emerging World Premiere archetype, the true lore of the Angelechy is still an unfolding mystery. While their current forces dominate the board with precise, inevitable movements, their celestial royal court is notably incomplete, with the Queen still missing and the Bishop, Angelechy Castellan, only arriving with Beyond the Brave on 8 October 2026. Furthermore, recent visions and disturbances hint at a dark fate for these pristine figures. Ethereal white pieces, such as the Rook, are beginning to show signs of corruption, slowly turning dark. This ominous visual evolution signals an impending internal war—a battle between the white and black pieces upon the celestial chessboard.

The Angelechy are chess pieces, and their names and effects follow the game, from the opening to the endgame. Follow a marked name to walk the trail.

  1. The white king, with a spiked crown. The other Angelechy are named after terms from medieval Europe, but Shatranga comes from chaturanga and shatranj, the Indian and Persian ancestors of chess. He appears in Opening to e4, Problem and Opposition.

  2. Shatranga and a row of Enlisted: 1.e4, the classic first move. In chess White always moves first, and its effect lets you summon before your opponent can.

  3. The pawn, the lowest-ranking foot soldier. Its effect banishes a monster in an adjacent column, as a pawn captures diagonally one square left or right. Its spear turns up in Endgame Problem.

  4. The knight, named after the destrier, the finest medieval warhorse. It banishes a monster in a different column, in a knight's jump. It makes the move in Brilliant Move, named after the "!!" of chess notation for a hard-to-find move that looks wrong but wins, and its sword turns up in Endgame Problem.

  5. The rook, the castle-tower piece, named after a fortress's projecting corner. It banishes a card in its column, as a rook captures along a file, and it can swap places with Shatranga to protect the others, like castling. It appears in Disturbance. Beyond the Brave (TCG 8 October 2026) adds the bishop, Angelechy Castellan, named after the official who ran a castle for his lord: it banishes a monster in an adjacent column or a Spell/Trap two columns away, a bishop's diagonals.

  6. Interference: a piece moved into the line between an attacked piece and its defender.

  7. Shatranga, set a chess problem.

  8. The endgame, when few pieces are left: Angelechy Seneschal, with Enlisted's spear and Destrier's sword. Seneschal is the black king from Beyond the Brave (TCG 8 October 2026), Shatranga's equal in Level, Type, ATK and DEF but DARK. It is actually grey, and Konami's article called it a "grey king", perhaps after chess variants with grey pieces either player can move, which fits an effect that switches sides. In Opposition the two kings face each other two squares apart, each blocking the other.

Executive Summary: The Grandmasters

The Angelechy archetype is a sophisticated, Synchro-focused strategy that introduces a revolutionary layer of positional awareness to the Yu-Gi-Oh! TCG. Representing angelic chess pieces, these monsters demand spatial mastery—moving across columns and transitioning seamlessly between Monster Zones and Spell & Trap Zones.

Their defining trait is the "Turn 0" interaction. By utilizing specialized traps like Angelechy Opening to E4 directly from the hand, players can begin setting up their board and disrupting the opponent before their own first turn has even started.

New in Beyond the Brave: seven Angelechy cards, including the archetype's first DARK piece. Read the Endgame Expansion guide

Tactical Mechanics

Zone Interaction

Angelechy monsters are not bound to standard rules of engagement. They possess the unique ability to step back into your Spell & Trap Zones, where they are treated as Continuous Spells. From this vantage point, they offer passive protection, search capabilities, and the threat of re-deploying to the front lines at a moment's notice.

Turn 0 Openings

True to their theme, Angelechy players always control the opening moves. Certain archetype Trap cards can be activated directly from the hand during the opponent's first turn. This allows the deck to bypass the inherent disadvantage of going second, establishing an immediate presence on the board.

Angelechy Opening to E4

Angelechy Opening to E4

The defining "Turn 0" Trap card. If you control no monsters, it can be activated from the hand. It immediately sets up an Enlisted token and pulls a core piece from the deck, ensuring your defense is ready before the opponent's first Main Phase.

Angelechy Enlisted

The pawns of the strategy. Often generated as tokens or low-level swarming monsters, they provide the necessary bodies for Synchro climbing into Shatranga and fuel the archetype's zone-shifting costs.

Grandmaster's Guide

Tactical Maneuvers

  • The "Castling" Paradigm

    Resolve Bastion to place the Shatranga King directly from the Extra Deck into the S/T Zone. This shields the King while enforcing a hard 5-effect activation floodgate on the opponent.

  • Pawn Promotion Loop

    Use Enlisted to banish an adjacent enemy and swap control. Upon control change, it returns to the Extra Deck to immediately "promote" and summon a Boss piece without level matching.

  • The Nullgainer Bridge

    Integrate Glitch Clutch Nullgainer and Witness of the Ancient for generic Level 8 Synchro climbs and a seamless pivot into Ultimaya Tzolkin.

Overview

The Lore Archive warned of white pieces turning dark and of a war between the white and black pieces. Beyond the Brave delivers it: Angelechy Seneschal is the archetype's first DARK monster, and Angelechy Opposition deploys pieces straight onto your opponent's side of the board. Everything else is built to reward the Castling engine you already know. The more Angelechy you park in the Spell & Trap Zone, the stronger the new cards get.

Three New Pillars

Backrow Count Is Power

Endgame Problem scales with the Angelechy Monster Cards in your backrow, and Verdict with every Angelechy Monster Card on the field. Castling is no longer just protection: every piece you store as a Continuous Spell moves you closer to a win condition.

Both Sides of the Board

Opposition Special Summons a piece into your opponent's Main Monster Zone. Seneschal changes hands when an Angelechy attacks it, and Verdict counts Angelechy cards anywhere on the field.

Redeploy, Don't Rebuild

Strategy Score, Brilliant Move, Opposition and Verdict each have a Graveyard effect that re-summons, re-places or shuffles back your spent pieces, so losing the backrow no longer means starting from zero.

What Changes in the Matchup

New Answers
  • Recovery after backrow removal: Opposition shuffles up to 5 spent Angelechy cards back into the Deck, Brilliant Move's GY effect summons any Angelechy straight from the Extra Deck, and Verdict's GY effect places a fresh piece. All of these rebuild on your turn; none of them dodge removal in response.
  • A mass-removal finisher: Verdict at 5+ banishes every card your opponent controls, and Endgame Problem's 3+ tier lets you activate it the turn you Set it.
  • A second floodgate layer: Castellan in the backrow limits each player to 1 activation per Chain, on top of Shatranga's 5-monster-effect cap.
Still Exposed
  • Non-destruction backrow removal: Bastion only stops destruction. Banish or bounce effects still strip several tiers at once.
  • Extra Deck locks: Strategy Score and Opposition restrict you to LIGHT or DARK Synchro Monsters from the Extra Deck for the turn, so generic Link and Xyz extenders are off the table.
  • Seneschal is a gift with strings: Opposition hands your opponent a 3000-ATK body. Once its summon effect resolves it can't become Xyz or Link material and returns to your Extra Deck when it leaves the field. They can still attack with it, Tribute it or Synchro with it, and if the summon effect is negated those restrictions never apply.
  • Castellan's lock cuts both ways: you are also limited to 1 activation per Chain while it sits in your backrow.

Combo Guide

Pre-release theorycraft. These lines are built from the official BETB-EN card text revealed on 22 September 2026. Rulings for the new cards haven't been published yet, so treat chain-timing details as provisional.

1. Endgame Castling

Going second Turn 0 Starter: Angelechy Opening to e4

The original Castling play, upgraded: Opening to e4 now fetches the scaling Field Spell and puts the new Level 7 in the Extra Monster Zone.

Your opponent's Standby Phase (their first turn)

  1. Activate Angelechy Opening to e4 from your hand. Place Angelechy Endgame Problem from your Deck.
  2. Special Summon Angelechy Castellan (Level 7) from your Extra Deck to the Extra Monster Zone.
  3. Place Angelechy Bastion from your Extra Deck in your Spell & Trap Zone as a Continuous Spell.
  4. Bastion's effect: place Angelechy Shatranga from your Extra Deck in your Spell & Trap Zone.
  5. Shatranga's effect: add Angelechy Verdict from your Deck to your hand.
End board
  • Endgame Problem at 2 pieces: the draw tier is online and you choose where your opponent's Special Summons land.
  • Shatranga: your opponent can only attempt 5 monster effects this turn.
  • Bastion: your other Angelechy cards can't be destroyed by your opponent's card effects.
  • Castellan in the Extra Monster Zone and Verdict in hand for your turn.

Why Castellan over Destrier? It keeps Destrier in the Extra Deck for Opposition next turn. Opening to e4 also locks you to Synchro Monsters from the Extra Deck until the end of your next turn.

2. The Verdict

Your turn after Endgame Castling Needs: Strategy Score or Opposition

Fill the backrow to three, hand Seneschal to your opponent to push the field count past five, then Set and flip Verdict in the same turn.

Main Phase 1

  1. Activate Angelechy Strategy Score: add Angelechy Opposition. Skip this if you already hold Opposition.
  2. Activate Opposition, choosing Angelechy Seneschal and Angelechy Destrier. Seneschal goes to your opponent's Main Monster Zone; Destrier goes to your Spell & Trap Zone.
  3. Two effects trigger together. Seneschal's lets each player Special Summon 1 Fairy Synchro from their Extra Deck with its effects negated, so you Special Summon Angelechy Enlisted. Destrier's adds Angelechy Brilliant Move from your Deck.
  4. Endgame Problem now counts 3 pieces (Bastion, Shatranga, Destrier). Set Verdict and activate it this turn.
  5. Verdict counts 6 Angelechy Monster Cards on the field (Castellan, Enlisted, Seneschal and your 3 backrow pieces), so it banishes all cards your opponent controls. Per its own text, Seneschal returns to your Extra Deck instead.
  6. Optional: activate Brilliant Move to return Bastion or Shatranga to the Extra Deck and Special Summon it, then attack the empty board with Castellan (2000), Enlisted (1500) and the 3000-ATK piece.
End board
  • Your opponent's field is cleared.
  • Destrier stays in the backrow and burns for 500 each time a card or effect your opponent activates resolves.
  • Opposition and Strategy Score in the GY hold follow-up recursion for later turns.

Step 6 turns off the returned piece's backrow effect and drops Endgame Problem to 2 pieces. Skip it if you'd rather keep Shatranga's cap or Bastion's protection for your opponent's turn.

3. Seneschal Gambit

Any turn with a Battle Phase Starter: Angelechy Opposition

A one-card line built around the capture: lend Seneschal to your opponent, then take it back mid-battle.

Main Phase 1

  1. Activate Angelechy Opposition, choosing Angelechy Seneschal and Angelechy Bastion. Seneschal goes to your opponent's Main Monster Zone; Bastion goes to your Spell & Trap Zone.
  2. Seneschal's effect lets you Special Summon Angelechy Castellan with its effects negated. Bastion's effect places Angelechy Shatranga in your Spell & Trap Zone.
  3. Shatranga's effect: add Angelechy Verdict from your Deck to your hand.

Battle Phase

  1. Attack Seneschal with Castellan. Seneschal's effect negates the attack and gives you control of it.
  2. Seneschal hasn't attacked this turn, so it can still attack: directly for 3000 if your opponent controls no other monsters.
End board
  • Seneschal (3000) and Castellan on your field. Neither can become Xyz or Link material, and both return to your Extra Deck if they leave the field.
  • Bastion and Shatranga in the backrow: destruction protection plus the 5-effect cap.
  • Verdict in hand to Set in Main Phase 2.

Going first there is no Battle Phase. You still end on Bastion, Shatranga, Castellan and Verdict, but your opponent starts their turn with a 3000-ATK Seneschal.

Competitive Analysis

Strengths

  • Turn 0 Resilience: Near immunity to the "going second" disadvantage thanks to hand-trap starters.
  • Evasive Boards: Zone-shifting allows pieces to easily dodge targeting and column-based removal.
  • Grind Game: Storing monsters as Continuous Spells provides excellent resource recursion.

Weaknesses

  • Backrow Vulnerability: Heavy reliance on the Spell/Trap zone makes them uniquely weak to Harpie's Feather Duster and Evenly Matched.
  • High Skill Ceiling: Mismanaging columns or zone placements can easily result in self-inflicted bottlenecks.
  • Anti-Synchro Hate: Dimensional Barrier completely shuts down their endgame capabilities.

Synergies & Tech

  • Synchron Engine: Frequently paired with Synchron tuners to accelerate their powerful Synchro boss plays.
  • Elfnote: The archetype blends well with Elfnote's melodic swarming capabilities.
  • Anti-Spell Fragrance: Since Angelechy pieces treat *themselves* as Spells (bypassing activation), they can safely run this floodgate.
